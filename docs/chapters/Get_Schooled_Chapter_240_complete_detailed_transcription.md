### Content note

The chapter discusses teacher liability, a child’s field-trip death, hostile parent complaints, school safety rules, and a Ministry-supervised compromise.

## Strips 001–002 — Minister Choi apologizes publicly

The TRPA arrives outside the Ministry as teacher demonstrations continue.

The protesters demand immediate protection and warn that school excursions will become a thing of the past.

Minister Choi Gang-seok walks directly toward the crowd.

The inspectors expect him to respond with a forceful political statement.

Instead, Choi bows deeply.

He publicly apologizes to the teachers’ unions protesting the recent guilty verdict.

News coverage reports that the Minister understands their outrage and shares their fear.

He promises a Ministry task force and fundamental measures to protect supervising teachers.

The apology temporarily calms the crowd.

The inspectors are surprised by how seriously Choi treats the political and professional consequences of the ruling.

---

## Strip 003 — The law provides no stable boundary

The Ministry begins reviewing the national rules.

Courts have produced sharply different outcomes in similar field-trip accidents:

- fines;
- acquittals;
- imprisonment;
- and permanent loss of a teaching license.

Safety-compliance standards are vague enough that rulings fluctuate according to the court’s interpretation.

Na says teachers have gradually accepted an impossible premise: that any accident means the individual teacher failed.

That premise encourages schools to eliminate every activity containing risk.

The agency is ordered to study the Youngbin case and identify a practical standard.

---

## Strip 004 — Youngbin’s field trip has already disappeared

The team visits Youngbin Elementary.

The school has replaced its scheduled field trip with a less demanding one-day activity.

Teachers explain that parents and staff can no longer agree about acceptable responsibility.

Hanrim hears that the school’s previous field trip was canceled after a student died.

Na says the agency needs the full timeline before deciding what should change.

---

## Strips 005–006 — Parents and teachers confront one another

A parent meeting becomes hostile.

Parents say they want their children to have the same educational opportunities students once received.

Teachers ask who will carry the legal responsibility if another accident occurs.

A teacher says staff in the district have resolved to reject field trips until the rules change.

Some parents accuse teachers of selfishly withdrawing essential experiences.

Teachers respond that they cannot be treated as criminally liable for every unpredictable act committed by dozens of children.

Hanrim attempts to calm the room and introduces the TRPA delegation.

She explains that the Minister has ordered a fact-finding survey and that both sides must be heard rather than canceled or humiliated.

---

## Strips 006–008 — Responsibility cannot rest on one adult

The discussion identifies the structural problem.

Under the current system, the supervising teacher’s life may be destroyed after an accident even when:

- the school provided insufficient staff;
- parents did not attend;
- outside safety personnel were absent;
- and the event involved many children acting independently.

The teachers say they have already proposed a compromise.

For field trips, the school should:

- create clearer safety rules;
- use professional field-trip staff;
- involve parents or other chaperones;
- and spread responsibility across the institution rather than placing it on one homeroom teacher.

Some parents reject the proposal as unnecessary bureaucracy.

Others say it is the only way to preserve trips at all.

---

## Strips 008–009 — Both sides lower their voices

The principal and TRPA representatives require both groups to stop shouting.

Parents say they also want the trip.

Teachers say they are not opposed to educational experiences; they are afraid of being individually sacrificed if anything goes wrong.

The agency proposes a fact-finding process and asks both sides to cooperate.

The staff will not conduct the trip alone.

The parents will not be allowed to treat the teacher as an all-purpose insurer.

The immediate goal becomes arranging one supervised field trip that can test a shared-responsibility model.

---

## Strip 010 — The TRPA volunteers to supervise

The school still lacks enough trusted safety staff.

Sera raises her hand.

> **THEN GO ON THE PICNIC!**

She proposes that the TRPA act as temporary supervisors.

The agency reports directly to the Minister, so it has no reason to avoid the responsibility.

The children will receive their trip, teachers will not stand alone, and parents will have an official body observing the process.

The room accepts the proposal.

Sera becomes unexpectedly excited about attending an elementary-school picnic.

---

## Strip 011 — Hanrim and Sera prepare for the trip

Hanrim and Sera are assigned to supervise the children.

Sera thanks Hanrim dramatically for handling part of the work.

Hanrim tells her to move over because there is a new junior in town.

Sera vows that, after the trip, she will teach Hanrim a lesson for acting superior.

The chapter ends lightly, but the arrangement carries serious weight: the agency has turned the national liability debate into a live field test.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Choi begins with apology rather than defensiveness

The public bow acknowledges that teacher fear is reasonable, even before policy details are settled.

## Similar accidents have produced inconsistent outcomes

The absence of a clear standard makes every trip a professional gamble.

## Teachers are not asking to eliminate responsibility

They want responsibility distributed among the school, qualified staff, parents, and event organizers.

## The compromise is practical rather than ideological

One supervised trip allows the school to test whether outdoor education can continue under shared responsibility.

## The TRPA becomes part of the experiment

Hanrim and Sera will not merely investigate afterward; they will experience the demands of supervising children directly.
