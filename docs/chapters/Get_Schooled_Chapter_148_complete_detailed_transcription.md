# Chapter-level continuity notes

## G.Y. used the agency as a weapon

Yoon-kyeol sent the villa location because the agency’s raid would eliminate a rival organization and create a criminal power vacuum for G.Y. His “gift” was calculated cooperation, not civic responsibility.

## Yoon-kyeol is cautious despite his confidence

He wants Na’s death because it would make him G.Y.’s next president, yet he abandons the opportunity when the result is uncertain. His rule—only fight battles he can win—distinguishes him from impulsive gang leaders.

## Social media links the two halves of the chapter

The national organization used platforms to glamorize crime and recruit minors. The new offenders use the same mechanisms to convert assault into entertainment, attention, and settlement money.

## The livestreamers control both violence and narrative

They select lighting, direct the victim’s position, edit the event as a “fight,” and later claim unfair persecution. The audience is not incidental; the assault is designed for circulation.

## Na postpones G.Y., not abandons it

He concludes that Yoon-kyeol will reveal himself again. The immediate priority becomes a public school assault whose perpetrators are still broadcasting and escalating.
