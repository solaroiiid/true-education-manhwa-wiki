# Chapter-level continuity notes

## So-rin’s confession does not automatically produce remorse

She admits the original lie to escape Hee-soo’s anger, not because she has fully accepted the harm done to Soo-hyun. When sympathy messages arrive, she immediately builds a new story in which the agency and Hee-soo are responsible for everything.

## Her childhood explains attention-seeking without excusing it

So-rin grew up amid domestic violence and parental abandonment. Uploading the family fight produced the first large wave of strangers who expressed concern for her. From that point onward, attention became associated with safety, validation, and belonging.

## Soo-hyun values truth over theatrical punishment

She knows the apology is insincere. What allows her to sleep is the public correction of the false accusation, because the online judgment had become more painful than the original assault.

## The rooftop stream turns self-endangerment into performance

So-rin’s emotions may be real, but the chapter emphasizes the changing viewer count and her growing excitement. The danger and the attention reinforce one another in real time.

## The arc’s unresolved question

Na recognizes the pattern before the jump occurs, but the chapter ends with So-rin still on the ledge and the audience rapidly expanding.
