## Images 001–003 — Training under the woman she despised

Woo-chan’s mother supervises Jaeguk’s mother at the supermarket.

She says people often look down on one another through jobs, housing, and education even though everyone is simply trying to live.

Jaeguk’s mother hears the statement as an accusation.

Every instruction feels humiliating because she still believes a rental resident should not be above an apartment owner.

---

## Images 004–007 — The origin of her obsession with property

A flashback shows Jaeguk’s mother growing up in an old, worn villa.

At school reunions and adult gatherings, she felt judged for limited education, poverty, and an unimpressive address.

Her husband also earned modestly.

When her father-in-law died, an inheritance gave the couple enough money to combine with a mortgage and personal loans.

They purchased the Sunshine Land apartment.

The deed did more than provide housing. It transformed how she saw herself.

She could finally speak as an owner rather than someone embarrassed by a villa address.

---

## Images 008–010 — Falling prices turn confidence into panic

Interest rates rise and apartment prices fall.

Prospective buyers withdraw.

The monthly loan burden remains.

Jaeguk’s mother begins to resent the rental building because it gives her a visible target for everything she fears: that the expensive home will not prove she has escaped poverty.

She tells Jaeguk not to play with Woo-chan because the girl is “not our level.”

The child learns that friendship can threaten family status.

## Images 011–013 — She refuses to reconsider

At the supermarket, Jaeguk’s mother internally calls rental residents leeches and decides she will not sell the apartment or accept equality.

Then she sees Jaeguk injured again.

She assumes the transfer school has attacked him.

Daniel tells her to trust the child enough to ask before deciding the answer.

Jaeguk says the injury came while playing rather than from a serious assault.

His mother does not believe him at first.

---

## Images 014–017 — Jaeguk slowly joins the new class

The chapter shows Jaeguk’s early days at the new school.

Some classmates mock his old habit of ranking people and call him strange.

He tries to carry the Sunshine Land hierarchy into their games, but no one understands why apartment ownership should decide teams or friendships.

During soccer, he pushes himself into the group.

He falls and gets bruised.

Instead of treating him as contaminated, the other children help him up and continue playing.

His mother secretly watches and records, expecting evidence of abuse.

What she sees is ordinary rough play and gradual acceptance.

---

## Images 018–021 — A child adapts faster than an adult

Daniel asks Jaeguk what is different about the new school.

Jaeguk says no one there cares which building he came from.

The absence of the category allows him to become simply another boy.

His mother watches him laugh with classmates.

Her certainty weakens.

The chapter ends with her realizing that the son she believed she was protecting is more relaxed after leaving the status system she created.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Property became emotional armor

The apartment allowed Jaeguk’s mother to believe she had permanently escaped the shame of poverty. Any reminder of debt or rental housing therefore feels like an attack on her identity.

## Jaeguk’s behavior changes because the environment changes

Without classmates who understand and reward the housing hierarchy, his classist performance stops producing authority.

## The injury is deliberately ambiguous at first

His mother interprets a bruise through fear and prejudice. Watching the full scene reveals that children can be physically rough without creating a caste.

## The chapter separates economic anxiety from moral permission

Her fear of debt and falling prices is understandable. Teaching a child to despise another family is not.
