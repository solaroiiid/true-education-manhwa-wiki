# Strips 15–17 — The revised school-violence committee

Sang-wook has watched news about the revised Teachers’ Rights Protection Act.

The old school-run violence committee was abolished and replaced with a School Violence Countermeasures Deliberation Committee supervised by the Office of Education.

The new body includes lawyers and other experts.

Sang-wook initially believes this matters:

> “IT’S NOT AT SCHOOL!”

> “IT’S WORTH A TRY!”

He reports the abuse.

The outcome is devastating.

Only Hong Song-hak receives a five-day attendance suspension. The others are ordered to apologize and attend special education.

The disciplinary standards allocate points for:

- intentionality,
- seriousness,
- persistence,
- degree of reflection,
- degree of reconciliation.

A chart converts totals into measures ranging from written apology and school volunteer work to suspension, class change, transfer, or expulsion.

Song-hak’s conduct receives roughly twelve points out of twenty.

# Strips 18–19 — “Sixty out of one hundred”

> “I APOLOGISE. HEHEHEHE.”

Sang-wook interprets the decision:

> “12 OUT OF 20 POINTS…”

> “60 OUT OF 100.”

> “THAT WAS MY SUFFERING SCORE…”

> “SCORED BY ADULTS.”

He imagines victims throughout the country whose experiences are similarly reduced to administrative ratings.

> “FOR ADULTS THIS WAS A PROBLEM ONLY WORTH 60 POINTS…”

He had reported to the Teachers’ Rights agency before trying to die, but he assumes:

> “THERE’S NO WAY THEY’LL HELP A 60 POINT CASE…”

The committee’s attempt at standardized fairness has communicated that his suffering is partial and tolerable.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. “Fairness” becomes numerical abstraction

The committee’s rubric may be designed to produce consistency, but Sang-wook experiences it as adults assigning a percentage to his pain.

## 2. A written apology without changed power is another attack

Song-hak’s mocking delivery shows why reconciliation points cannot be measured from paperwork alone.

## 3. The bullying attacks family identity

Forcing Sang-wook to repeat obscenity about his mother crosses from physical dominance into an assault on his most intimate relationship.

## 4. Intervention changes his imagined future

Sang-wook does not instantly recover. He simply sees, for the first time, a future in which Song-hak may not control every return to school.
