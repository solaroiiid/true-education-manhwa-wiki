# Chapter-level narrative and visual notes

## 1. Chey’s public persona is constructed through contradiction

The opening narration presents Chey as both a public relief valve and a political aggressor. His press conference reinforces that duality. He officially rejects “corporal punishment,” yet repeatedly minimizes bruises and treats physical discipline as an acceptable cost of restoring authority. He defends freedom of the press while insulting reporters for questioning him. These contradictions are not concealed; he turns them into proof that he is supposedly more honest than conventional politicians.

## 2. The chapter reframes the earlier school arc as national policy

The student suicide and institutional corruption from the previous chapters are no longer treated as one school’s isolated failure. Chey uses them to justify the “Protected Education” policy nationally. The classroom television panels show that students are hearing his rhetoric directly, meaning the intervention program is becoming a public political project rather than a quiet emergency measure.

## 3. Ga-Yun humanizes both men without softening them

At the grave, Chey and Na remain abrasive: Chey kicks Na for calling him “Dad,” Na complains about the conference, and Chey grumbles about sunlight. Yet the carefully chosen macarons, maintained grave, and restrained discussion reveal a shared grief around Ga-Yun. The chapter explicitly identifies Na as her father. Chey’s line—“That’s a misery reserved for the living”—shows that his harsh philosophy is connected to unresolved loss, not merely political ambition.

## 4. The next school announces its problem visually before any exposition

The school exterior is peaceful, but the interior immediately contradicts it. Students do not merely bully one another privately; organized factions march through public corridors and fight in large numbers. Other students instinctively flee and describe the event as something recurring. The lack of visible staff intervention is as important as the violence itself.

## 5. Na’s final pose establishes his method

Na does not ask which gang is right. He sees the existence of the gang war itself as the assignment. His spread hands, relaxed posture, and pleased grin transform the chaotic corridor into a stage he is ready to enter. The cliffhanger promises direct intervention rather than investigation from a distance.
