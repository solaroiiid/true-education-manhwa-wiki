- Visual interpretation is separated from literal dialogue and captions.

## Chapter-level continuity and interpretation

### Season 2 begins by expanding the Agency
The first season ended with rumors that a new overseer would be hired. Chapter 112 immediately shows Han-rim and Hwa-jin conducting interviews, then reveals Lee Junbin already acting in the field. His introduction links institutional expansion with a new category of case: celebrity fandom, online reputation warfare, and school violence hidden beneath a marketable public persona.

### Fairy's omnipresence is part of the victim's trauma
Fairy's performance reaches the arena, city streets, phones, school gates, and online comments. The victim cannot simply avoid the bully after Yujung becomes famous; the face associated with her abuse is now celebrated everywhere. Her vomiting at an ordinary fan conversation shows how celebrity promotion can repeatedly reactivate private trauma.

### The article reproduces the structure of the abuse
At school, Yujung controlled the victim's expression and forced her to perform friendship. Online, fans again dictate what the victim is allowed to say, deny her account, and replace it with a story in which Yujung is the real victim. The fandom does not merely defend the idol—it recreates Yujung's power at a much larger scale.

### Beauty is treated as evidence of innocence
The hostile comments repeatedly argue that the accuser must be ugly, jealous, or socially inferior because Fairy is beautiful. The boys who confront Eunji say that “pretty girls don't curse at pretty girls,” turning appearance into a moral hierarchy. This allows strangers to treat violence against the accuser as defense of beauty rather than harassment.

### Lee Junbin's first act defines his practical approach
The interview montage includes ideologues, abusers, self-promoters, and timid applicants. Lee's qualifications are not explained in the interview room; instead, the chapter demonstrates them. He identifies the student, intervenes before an assault, ends the immediate threat efficiently, and presents official credentials only after she is safe. His methods are physical, but more controlled than the excesses associated with Hwa-jin or Han-rim.

### Naming inconsistency
The scanlation gives the victim at least two name forms—**Kim Eunjin** in the doxxing comment and **Kim Eunji** when Lee finds her. It also briefly appears to give Min Yujung's name to the transfer student during the flashback. These inconsistencies are preserved because the chapter does not provide enough unambiguous information to silently correct them.
