# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The principal’s hiring process reveals institutional vulnerability

Daniel is hired almost immediately because he offers to work without salary. The school values obedience and cheap labor more than verification, allowing an outsider to enter with only a flattering story about traditional culture.

## The students’ brightness is itself suspicious

The absence of visible bruises and the unusually polished behavior do not disprove the complaint. Daniel recognizes that a closed institution can train students to perform happiness and direct violence away from public spaces.

## Suchan has survived by moving upward

Minhye remembers him as the only compassionate child in the school. Three years later, he is a student leader trusted by the principal and obeyed by younger children. His status appears to have been purchased by adopting the institution’s cruelty.

## Abuse is disguised as hygiene and education

The aggressors frame the assault as making a child brush his teeth. This mirrors the school’s broader practice of converting sadism into etiquette, spiritual correction, or communal discipline.

## Daniel begins with observation rather than force

He does not expose his identity or intervene before understanding the hierarchy. His report to Na identifies the systemic mechanism: in an unchanged culture, victims are incentivized to become perpetrators because rank is the only available protection.

## The chapter’s central tragedy

Suchan’s transformation does not erase his victimhood, but it creates new victims. Daniel’s nonviolent philosophy will now have to confront someone whose survival strategy is inseparable from the power he abuses.
