# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The junior’s emotional breakthrough is being protected

Her admiration for Hanrim is not only about fighting skill. She has built her identity around rescuing others and has never experienced someone stepping in for her. The senior’s command to stay still therefore feels unfamiliar, comforting, and destabilizing.

## The chapter widens the case from a local gang to organized crime

The construction-site incident is tied to a nationwide alliance involved in kidnapping, fraud, loan-sharking, gambling, and recruitment of minors. The agency’s role becomes part of a broader joint investigation with police and prosecutors.

## The gang treats minors as labor and shields

The organization recruits through social media and “interviews,” then forces high-school students into criminal acts. The apparently eager student at the villa demonstrates how easily criminal ambition and adolescent bravado can be exploited.

## The national gang is internally unstable

Once police attention increases, the leaders immediately fight over revenue and territory. Their coalition is held together by profit rather than loyalty.

## The raid divides roles according to condition and method

Na keeps Daniel outside because Daniel refuses conventional violence and keeps the injured junior outside because she cannot safely participate. Both are assigned the practical task of stopping escapees, preserving their involvement without ignoring limitations.
