# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The real lesson is command discipline

The injured recruit’s instinct to protect a high-school student is not condemned. Her failure is acting without support, charging into a gang alone, and becoming another person who must be rescued. The senior’s new permission rule preserves moral initiative while placing it inside an accountable chain of command.

## Sera’s full-time status raises the stakes

The personnel document means she can no longer be treated as a temporary recruit who may simply be dismissed. The agency must either train her into a reliable officer or accept responsibility for the damage caused by her recklessness.

## The senior’s strength is technical rather than theatrical

Ham Bu-kwon is introduced through size, wrestling credentials, gang reputation, and national backing. She defeats him with positioning, timing, chin control, and two accurate strikes. Her closing advice identifies his mistake as basic fighting incompetence despite his power.

## The gang’s “national organization” is intimidation through scale

Ham Bu-kwon repeatedly invokes members across the country. The chapter visually counters that boast by reducing the organization’s strongest representative to an unconscious body in seconds.

## The injured colleague’s fear is reasonable

She has personally fought Ham Bu-kwon and knows his durability. Her shock is not cowardice; it establishes how far beyond ordinary gang fighters the senior’s ability actually lies.
