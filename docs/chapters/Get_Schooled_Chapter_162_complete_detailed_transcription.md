## Images 001–003 — Daniel explains why Jaeguk blended in

Daniel says Jaeguk initially tried to discriminate at the new school exactly as he had at Sunshine Land.

He called children rental bums and attempted to rank them by family assets.

The classmates found the behavior bizarre rather than authoritative.

Eventually he stopped because it brought no social benefit.

Daniel summarizes the result with a simple idea: if a child does not keep making himself a problem, he does not have to remain one.

Adults taught the prejudice; a different peer environment allowed the child to unlearn part of it.

## Images 004–006 — His mother tries to restore the old boundary

Jaeguk’s mother sees him playing with children she considers low-status.

She orders him not to associate with rental residents.

Daniel watches and realizes she still has not understood the lesson.

Gu Sera appears and comments that neither gentle explanation nor ordinary violence has changed the woman.

She says there are other methods: let a perpetrator experience the emotional and social consequences of the system she built.

Daniel’s expression shows discomfort with how far Sera may have gone.

## Images 007–010 — Sunshine Land removes visible segregation

The residents’ committee changes course.

The fence around the playground is removed or opened.

Building eligibility notices disappear.

Children from every building can use the facilities.

The rental building is repainted to match the rest of the complex, with costs divided through the appropriate management structure.

Representatives explain that discrimination damaged the apartment’s popularity and value.

Jaeguk’s mother is also forced to step down from her residents’ role because her behavior created public harm.

The complex erases some of the visual machinery that enabled the children’s caste system.

## Images 011–013 — Her financial position collapses

Jaeguk’s mother learns that Woo-chan’s mother has stable responsibility at the supermarket and that the supposed social hierarchy has no meaning outside the residents’ dispute.

She later finds a same-day quick-loan flyer.

The advertisement promises immediate money with minimal scrutiny.

The family needs funds to cover interest and prevent default.

She takes the loan.

## Images 014–017 — Six months later

A time jump shows the apartment entering auction.

Jaeguk’s family packs their belongings and moves back to a villa.

The status she used to insult others has disappeared.

Daniel says that without the private loan, the apartment might not have reached auction so quickly.

Jaeguk’s mother asks how he knows about the loan.

He sees the familiar flyer.

## Images 018–020 — Daniel suspects Sera

Daniel remembers Sera’s earlier comments about creating a lesson strong enough to wound.

He asks whether she placed the private-loan advertisements where Jaeguk’s mother would find them.

Sera does not give a direct answer.

The possibility changes the meaning of the outcome. The woman made the decision herself, but Sera may have deliberately placed a predatory option in front of someone she knew was desperate.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Children respond to incentives faster than adults

Jaeguk abandons discriminatory behavior when it no longer earns admiration. His mother continues because property status is tied to decades of shame and fear.

## The complex changes both rules and symbols

Opening the playground addresses access; repainting Building 103 addresses the visible badge that made rental children identifiable.

## Sera’s suspected intervention is ethically darker than Daniel’s

Daniel creates controlled educational experiences. Sera may have placed a destructive financial temptation in front of the target and allowed the consequences to unfold.

## The family’s fall reproduces the category it despised

They return to a villa and lose the owner identity. The chapter avoids saying poverty itself is punishment; the punishment is confronting the falseness of the hierarchy.
