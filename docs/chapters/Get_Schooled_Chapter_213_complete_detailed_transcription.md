# Get Schooled — Chapter 213
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Content note

The chapter contains false accusation, vigilante violence, suicidal behavior, public harassment, and discussion of whether young offenders can change.

## Strip 001 — Hansol insists the accusations are false

Song Hansol remains surrounded by Sera, former targets, and young people who once supported his “Second Company.”

He repeatedly insists that the criticism against him is not true.

Sera tells him that the problem was visible from the beginning: he concentrated on defeating people labeled as bullies but showed little interest in what victims actually needed afterward.

Hansol refuses to accept that his work was meaningless.

He says he made mistakes, but he also reached children the official TRPA left waiting.

The emotional balance of the room shifts when a larger group of his supporters arrives.

---

## Strip 002 — “I saved ten times more people than I hurt”

The young supporters identify themselves as people Hansol rescued.

Some say their abusers stopped only after he intervened. Others say teachers, schools, police, and the official agency had ignored their pleas.

They promised to protect him if the authorities ever came.

Hansol uses the crowd as proof of legitimacy.

> **I SAVED TEN TIMES MORE PEOPLE THAN I HURT.**

He argues that a few errors cannot erase every life he improved.

Sera answers that constant praise created the monster standing before them.

The supporters treated gratitude as permission for Hansol to decide guilt, punishment, and acceptable collateral damage by himself.

---

## Strip 003 — The falsely accused girl confronts him

A young woman in hospital clothes forces her way forward.

She is the patient who nearly died after Hansol accepted a false report and publicly branded her as a bully.

She shouts that he is not a hero.

Because Hansol beat her and displayed her punishment as proof of guilt, strangers flooded her phone with threats and humiliation.

No one believed her denials.

She eventually concluded that dying was the only way to make people consider that she might have been telling the truth.

Hansol tries to speak.

The girl cuts him off.

> **I ALMOST DIED BECAUSE OF YOU.**

Her presence prevents him from reducing his errors to an abstract percentage.

---

## Strip 004 — One innocent victim destroys the justification

Sera tells Hansol that the number of people he helped does not purchase the right to injure one innocent person.

Whether he rescued ten people or tens of thousands, the moment he punishes without sufficient investigation, he becomes another source of terror.

Hansol’s confidence finally collapses.

He kneels and apologizes.

The supporters who came to shield him begin turning away.

Some remain grateful for what he did, but they can no longer deny that his certainty nearly killed someone who had already been victimized.

---

## Strip 005 — The investigation’s conflicting figures

The case moves into public reporting.

Police and the agency review Hansol’s attacks.

One visible summary states that **7 of 54** people he labeled school-violence perpetrators were innocent or falsely accused.

Another news panel gives the figure as **7 of 31**.

The scanlation does not reconcile the difference, so both are preserved here as conflicting on-page reports.

What remains consistent is the conclusion: at least seven people were violently punished despite not being the offenders Hansol believed them to be.

Netizens who praised him immediately reverse direction.

Posts that once called him a savior now call him a psychopath, fraud, and monster.

The same crowd that treated his punishment as proof of guilt now treats the official investigation as permission to hate him.

---

## Strip 006 — Sera’s growth and the TRPA’s new criticism

Sera jokes that the case proves why undercover work matters.

Hanrim looks at her injured junior and notes that she has grown.

The victory does not protect the agency from criticism.

Experts and commentators connect the Second Company directly to the TRPA’s public history.

They argue that the official inspectors repeatedly presented physical punishment as heroic justice, then acted surprised when young viewers copied the violence without copying the investigation or legal responsibility.

The agency is accused of teaching an emotional equation:

> punishment equals justice.

The question becomes larger than Hansol.

What kind of behavior did the TRPA glamorize while trying to restore order?

---

## Strip 007 — A youth-rights attorney answers the cameras

A documentary crew interviews a neatly dressed, glasses-wearing lawyer and counselor who works with juvenile offenders.

He is asked whether repeat offenders and severely violent youths can genuinely be rehabilitated.

He rejects the phrase:

> **PEOPLE CAN’T BE FIXED.**

Used against young people, he says, it denies development before adulthood is complete and brands them for life.

A child repeatedly told that change is impossible may eventually accept the criminal identity society assigns.

He says:

> **HUMANS CAN CHANGE, AS LONG AS THEY’RE GIVEN THE RIGHT REASON.**

---

## Strip 008 — “Because I did”

The counselor finishes:

> **BECAUSE I DID.**

The polished adult image gives way to his past.

A younger version of the man stands among bloodied bodies with tattoos, a violent stare, and the posture of a delinquent accustomed to intimidation.

The contrast introduces him as someone who does not defend rehabilitation from theory alone.

He once belonged to the category the public now calls irredeemable.

The chapter ends by opening a new case about whether violent youth can truly change—and what society owes both their victims and their rehabilitation.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Hansol’s supporters are not simply foolish

Many were genuinely failed by official systems. Their loyalty reflects relief as much as manipulation.

## Arithmetic cannot justify unreviewed punishment

Hansol treats innocent victims as an acceptable error rate. Sera insists that each error is a life the vigilante has no authority to gamble.

## Public opinion repeats Hansol’s mistake

Online audiences move from worship to hatred with the same lack of investigation they once praised.

## The TRPA must answer for its public image

The agency distinguishes its force through investigation and responsibility, but the public mainly saw dramatic beatings.

## The new counselor embodies the rehabilitation argument

His adult work and violent past challenge the belief that every serious youth offender remains permanently unchanged.
