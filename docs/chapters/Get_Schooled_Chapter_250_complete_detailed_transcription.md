# Get Schooled — Chapter 250
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Scope

This transcription follows the **8 ordered story strips** embedded in the supplied Chapter 250 MHT, excluding its separate title/header image. Readable dialogue, combat, police contact, and the developing parental-responsibility experiment are preserved where legible.

### Content note

The chapter contains severe adult fighting, burns, broken hands, child abandonment, parental exploitation, and threats of further violence.

---

## Strip 001 — Na confronts Yunseong’s father

Na approaches Yunseong’s father outside the home.

He says that when a child repeatedly harms others, a guardian cannot simply declare the matter unrelated to him.

The father answers that victims may file a civil lawsuit.

He has little money and believes an unenforceable judgment cannot hurt him.

He assumes Na is another small-business owner victimized by Yunseong and tells him to take the complaint elsewhere.

Na places a hand on his shoulder.

He says the man has misunderstood the purpose of the visit.

---

## Strips 002–004 — The former boxer attacks first

The father demands to know whether Na is a gangster.

Na identifies the organization and tells him that the TRPA is examining whether the parent himself is sustaining Yunseong’s crime.

The father was once a boxer.

He throws the first punch.

Na allows several jabs and combinations to land while observing the technique.

The father boasts that a boxer’s jab begins everything that follows.

Na interrupts the rhythm with a lighter, briefly burning or startling him, then counters brutally.

The fight escalates.

Na crushes or badly injures the man’s hands and arms—the body parts the former boxer uses to dominate both strangers and his son.

He says a fighter who has forgotten what weakness feels like is pathetic.

The father falls, shocked that his athletic history cannot protect him.

---

## Strip 005 — Two responsibilities

Na identifies two separate forms of responsibility.

First, the father threw the opening punch and is accountable for the fight.

Second, he failed to control and protect his child.

The father responds with astonishing indifference.

He tells Na to take Yunseong away.

The agency may beat him, use him as a servant, or do whatever it wants.

The father speaks as though transferring ownership of an unwanted object.

Na is disgusted.

> **WHAT DO YOU THINK A CHILD IS?**

He strikes the man again.

The question is not rhetorical.

The experiment can succeed only if the father begins seeing Yunseong as someone for whom he remains responsible.

---

## Strip 006 — The warning

Na orders the father to keep Yunseong under control.

If the child commits another offense and the parent refuses to respond, Na will return.

Lee Jun-bin has witnessed part of the intervention.

He criticizes the brutality and says the method cannot become ordinary policy.

At the same time, he admits the case demonstrates why a parental-responsibility law may be necessary.

A parent who responds only to direct personal consequences will not change because society politely explains childhood development.

Na asks Jun-bin to cooperate.

They need one clear example showing whether pressure on the guardian can alter the child’s environment.

---

## Strip 007 — The father awakens injured and alone

The father wakes at home with both hands heavily bandaged or broken.

The apartment remains dirty and neglected.

Yunseong is gone.

The parent initially treats the encounter as an assault he can ignore once the pain fades.

Then the phone rings.

---

## Strip 008 — The police call again

Police say Yunseong was arrested at dawn for another theft.

The father must come to the station and take responsibility for bringing him home.

His first reaction is familiar.

He tells police to put the boy in jail or beat him.

Then he remembers Na’s face and warning.

The inspector’s shadow appears in his imagination.

The message is simple:

- when the child causes trouble;
- when the police call;
- and when an adult must bring him back—

the father will be that adult.

The chapter ends with the injured man forced to stand and leave the apartment.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Na’s intervention is deliberately punitive

It is not presented as a model of gentle family counseling. He creates an immediate consequence the father cannot evade through poverty or indifference.

## The father’s boxing identity mirrors his parenting

He relies on physical domination and abandons responsibility when strength does not solve the problem.

## Selling the child reveals deeper neglect

The parent would rather surrender Yunseong to strangers than change his own behavior.

## Jun-bin remains ethically uncomfortable

He supports legal accountability while recognizing that Na’s personal violence cannot substitute for a stable system.

## The first behavioral change is extremely small

The father has not become caring. He merely answers the police call because ignoring it now carries a consequence for him.
