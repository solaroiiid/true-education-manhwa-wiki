# Opening — Whale turns on his master

Whale reaches Son Hye-seung and strikes Koo Man-chun.

The village head cannot believe the servant he controlled would raise a hand against him.

Whale apologizes but says killing the teacher is wrong.

Koo Man-chun screams that Son Hye-seung nearly destroyed the village and that her mouth must be closed.

He orders Whale to throw her into the sea.

# Na lists the crimes

Na and Lim arrive.

Na sings and jokes while listing the offenses they have already observed:

- extortion,
- bribery of police,
- contracted assault,
- threats,
- attempted murder,
- and obstruction of public officials.

He says those are only the crimes visible to the agency. A real police investigation will reveal more.

Koo Man-chun recognizes them from news coverage and realizes they are from the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency.

Lim arrests him and says he will be handed over once the typhoon subsides.

# Whale still tries to protect Koo Man-chun

Koo Man-chun threatens Whale.

If he goes to prison, Whale’s bedridden father will stop receiving hospital payments.

Whale says he cannot allow the arrest because the village head sends his salary to the hospital every month.

Na asks whether Whale has personally visited or spoken to his father.

Whale says the man is in a coma and therefore cannot receive visitors.

Na becomes suspicious.

He asks Whale’s real name and identification details.

Whale says he is **Kim Sun-cheol**, but does not know his full resident-registration number.

# The background check

Na calls a police contact, Park Sang-ho, and requests a background check.

The result is devastating.

Kim Sun-cheol was reported missing fifteen years earlier.

He was only seventeen when he disappeared.

His father, Kim Nak-heon, died fourteen years ago—one year after the disappearance.

The father was processed as someone without family or friends.

There is no living hospitalized parent receiving Whale’s wages.

---

# How the village head enslaved him

A flashback shows the seventeen-year-old Sun-cheol asking for work.

His father was ill, and he believed he needed money for hospital bills.

Koo Man-chun recognized that the boy was physically strong, naive, and poorly educated.

He promised to transfer wages directly to the hospital.

Whenever Sun-cheol asked about money, the village head threatened him and told him not to worry.

For fifteen years, Sun-cheol worked from dawn until late at night without receiving wages.

He endured punishment happily because he believed every day of labor helped his father.

In reality, his father was already dead.

---

# Fifteen years collapse in five minutes

Sun-cheol refuses to believe the police.

Koo Man-chun finally admits the truth casually.

He says that even if the father is dead, Sun-cheol is alive because the village fed and housed him.

He argues that Sun-cheol cannot live without him.

The revelation destroys the moral structure around which Sun-cheol built his adult life.

Lim asks whether they should stop him from attacking the village head.

Na says they must.

Then he admits:

> “I DON’T WANT TO STOP HIM.”

The chapter ends as Sun-cheol turns his accumulated grief and rage toward the man who stole fifteen years of his life.

Koo Man-chun did not merely exploit poverty. He maintained control through a fabricated story about an ill parent.

## 2. Illiteracy and isolation preserve the lie

Sun-cheol cannot manage transfers, verify hospital records, or complete a basic identity check without outside assistance.

## 3. The village head calls captivity care

Food and lodging are presented as repayment for fifteen years of unpaid labor, echoing other exploiters in the series who rename control as protection.

## 4. Na’s reluctance to intervene is moral, not legal

He knows the assault must be stopped but understands why justice feels emotionally inadequate after such a revelation.
