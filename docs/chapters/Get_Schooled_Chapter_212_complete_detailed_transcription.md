## Strip 001 — Park Jiseon says she was the victim

One of the people assembled by Sera speaks first.

The young woman is identified through the report as **Park Jiseon**.

> **I’M NOT A BULLY.**

> **I’M THE VICTIM.**

She says she was bullied severely for approximately two years.

When she could no longer endure it, she reported the abuse to the TRPA.

For the first time, adults listened and recorded her story.

She believed help had finally arrived.

Then Hansol appeared.

He attacked her because someone else submitted a report naming **her** as the bully.

Jiseon repeatedly said he had the wrong person.

Hansol answered:

> **THAT’S WHAT THE BULLIES ALWAYS SAY. “I’M INNOCENT.”**

Her denial became another reason for him to believe she was manipulative.

## Strip 002 — The false report is read aloud

Sera displays the report Hansol accepted.

Its wording is approximately:

> **REPORTING PARK JISEON OF JUNGJIN HIGH.**

> **PARK JISEON BULLIED ME ALL THE WAY SINCE ELEMENTARY SCHOOL.**

> **SHE CALLED ME SMELLY AND MADE ME DO CLEANING DUTIES ALONE.**

> **WHEN I SAID I’D REPORT HER, SHE REPORTED ME AS THE BULLY.**

> **IT’S SO UNFAIR. PLEASE PUNISH HER.**

Jiseon explains that the report came from the person who actually bullied her.

The offender anticipated that Jiseon would seek help and filed a reversed accusation.

Hansol did not compare accounts, interview classmates, or examine school records.

He accepted the first emotionally convincing story and delivered punishment.

Sera gestures toward the room.

> **DO YOU SEE IT NOW? THEY’RE NOT THE BULLIES.**

## Strip 003 — More than half the reports require skepticism

Sera asks:

> **YOU KNOW WHAT HALF THE REPORTS TO THE TRPA ARE?**

She answers:

- pranks,
- exaggerated stories,
- demands for retaliation,
- malicious false reports,
- and conflicts where both sides omit important facts.

> **MEANING, MORE THAN HALF ARE PRANKS, EXAGGERATIONS, OR FALSE CLAIMS.**

The people in the room represent different types of Hansol’s mistakes.

Some were actual victims falsely reported by their bullies.

Some had no meaningful connection to the alleged incident.

One was identified because the reporter used the wrong name.

Another was not even attending school at the time of the supposed offense.

Sera asks whether this is what vigilante justice means:

> **GOING AROUND BEATING INNOCENT KIDS?**

Hansol laughs.

He asks how the official agency is any different when it also acts on reports and uses force.

Sera answers:

> **OF COURSE WE’RE DIFFERENT~ TO BE EXACT, THERE ARE 2 DIFFERENCES!**

## Strip 004 — First difference: investigation

The first difference is verification.

Sera says:

> **WITH 99.9% CERTAINTY, I CAN SAY THEY’RE NOT BULLIES~!**

Hansol immediately points out:

> **BUT 99.9% ISN’T 100%.**

Sera agrees that absolute certainty is rarely possible.

That is precisely why an investigator must collect as much independent information as possible before imposing harm.

She presents the findings gathered over five days:

- A witness confirms that Jiseon was bullied badly for two years.
- A classmate laughs at the idea that “Jungryul” is a bully and says he would lose a fight to an elementary-school child.
- One report confused **Juho** with **Panho**, the person witnesses identify as the actual delinquent.
- **Heetae** was away in field training at an electric company and was not even present at school.
- Another student admits the accusation was only a prank report.

> **HOW ABOUT IT? I SPENT 5 DAYS INVESTIGATING!**

Hansol had mocked the TRPA for taking too long.

The five days prevented several innocent people from being punished again.

## Strip 005 — Hansol treats error as a repair job

Hansol finally says:

> **FINE… I WAS WRONG. I’LL GO FIX IT NOW.**

He asks for the names of the real bullies.

He will beat them instead.

One of the victims immediately refuses.

She does not want another person attacked in her name.

Sera asks what Hansol plans to do for the innocent people he already injured.

He has no answer beyond apology and replacement violence.

> **DO YOU SEE WHY AN UNQUALIFIED PUNK LIKE YOU SHOULDN’T ACT SO RECKLESSLY?**

> **YOU CAN’T EVEN TAKE RESPONSIBILITY WHEN THINGS GO WRONG!**

An untrained vigilante can say:

> **OH? MY BAD~ I’M SORRY~**

but the victim continues living with hospitalization, humiliation, trauma, or suicidal thoughts.

## Strip 006 — Second difference: the true purpose of the violence

Sera says she now understands why Hansol worked so carelessly.

She remembers his expression while striking restrained or helpless people.

> **YOU WERE DEFINITELY ENJOYING IT!!**

> **HELPING VICTIMS WASN’T YOUR GOAL ANYWAY, RIGHT?**

> **YOU JUST WANTED A REASON TO BEAT SOMEONE UP!**

> **PRETENDING TO BE RIGHTEOUS JUST TO FEED YOUR FUCKING SADISM!**

Hansol attacks.

He says Sera is a close-range fighter and attempts to keep her at the distance where Gakcheomdo’s thrusting kicks are strongest.

Sera closes in before the leg can extend fully.

The fight spreads across the room while the assembled victims retreat.

---

## Strip 007 — Sera has adapted in one week

Hansol is shocked by Sera’s timing.

Only about a week has passed since their first fight, yet she now reads the angles and preparation of his kicks.

She avoids the direct thrusts, attacks the standing leg, and forces exchanges inside his preferred range.

Hansol says her improvement is unreasonable.

Sera jokes that she used a bit of a cheat but refuses to let “Empress Gu Sera” lose to some random man.

She says people might be more considerate if she were fighting a famous genius rather than an obsessive vigilante.

Hansol acknowledges that she possesses frightening talent.

The fight continues with neither willing to retreat.

## Strip 008 — Strategy gives way to direct force

Hansol changes his rhythm and attempts another rapid piercing kick.

Sera decides she has spent enough time analyzing him.

> **SCREW STRATEGY~ IMMA JUST SMASH THROUGH HEAD-ON…**

She charges directly through his attack.

Hansol admits:

> **OKAY~ I ADMIT YOU’VE GOT SOME TALENT.**

Sera answers:

> **CUZ I’M A SUPER-GENIUS.**

The chapter ends mid-fight, with the inspector attempting to break the Second Company’s authority physically after dismantling its moral argument.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## False reports are not edge cases

The agency expects large numbers of prank, retaliatory, exaggerated, and malicious submissions. Investigation is the central work, not an optional delay.

## Hansol confuses correction with responsibility

Beating a different person does not repair the bodily and psychological harm inflicted on someone innocent.

## The first difference is procedural

The TRPA can also be wrong, but it has a duty to gather records, testimony, and corroboration before acting.

## The second difference is motivational and accountable

Sera argues that Hansol’s pleasure in domination—not victims’ safety—has become the true engine of the Second Company.

## A victim may reject retaliatory violence

Hansol assumes every harmed person wants the offender beaten. The assembled witnesses show that some want safety, recognition, and prevention instead.

## Sera’s combat adaptation mirrors the investigation theme

She studies the technique, tests evidence, and changes her approach rather than relying on immediate certainty.
