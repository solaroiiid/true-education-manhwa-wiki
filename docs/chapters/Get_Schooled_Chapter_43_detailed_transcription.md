## Strip 008 — A child shows her dress

A girl proudly displays a dress or photograph of clothing her father bought.

Other children respond positively, calling it pretty or complimenting her.

## Strip 009 — Mandatory confession

Yang tells the class that judging or praising a person by appearance reinforces discrimination.

She asks children who made the compliment to raise their hands or admit fault.

The children become quiet and uncertain.

Na raises a hand with exaggerated enthusiasm.

> “VERY GOOD.”

His tone is visibly sarcastic.

---

## Strip 010 — Na distinguishes praise from contempt

Na argues that discrimination based on appearance should disappear, but a compliment is not automatically an act of looking down on someone else.

He challenges the assumption that every evaluation of appearance creates hierarchy.

Yang insists that appearance-centered praise trains girls into prescribed femininity.

---

## Strip 011 — Individual taste

Na replies that wanting to dress attractively or enjoying praise can also be an individual preference.

He asks the child directly whether she likes being called pretty.

She answers that she does.

Na’s argument is not that beauty standards are harmless. It is that the teacher cannot protect individuality by erasing the child’s own stated preference.

---

## Strip 012 — Warning against forced uniformity

Na says that suppressing individual tastes in the name of equality can itself become authoritarian or fascistic.

The children listen closely, and several visibly agree.

Yang’s face tightens because the class is responding more naturally to the open question than to her predetermined answer.

---

## Strip 013 — Textbook occupations

Yang changes the subject to occupations and opens a textbook.

She presents images in which the pilot, doctor, or police officer is male while the teacher is female.

She calls the illustrations a gender stereotype and says the Ministry of Education should correct them.

The lesson now moves from compliments to representation in employment.

# Chapter-level observations

### 1. Observation replaces accusation

Na gives Yang an opportunity to demonstrate that the first lesson was misunderstood. Her repeated behavior supplies stronger evidence than an immediate confrontation would have.

### 2. The key dispute is not equality versus discrimination

Na and Lim do not oppose equality. They oppose a system in which the teacher predefines every correct conclusion and treats dissent as proof of immorality.

### 3. Lim’s answer refuses tokenism

Her military experience supports women’s competence while rejecting the idea that women must have a decorative or separate “female role.”

### 4. Children respond to genuine questions

Once the students are permitted to answer from preference and experience, the classroom becomes more animated. Yang experiences this liveliness as a loss of control.
