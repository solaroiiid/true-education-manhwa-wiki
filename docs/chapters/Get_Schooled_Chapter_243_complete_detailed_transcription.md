## Strip 001 — The TRPA reviews twenty-three complaints

At the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency office, Hanrim presents the written follow-up report from the team’s recent field trip.

> **WE RECEIVED A TOTAL OF 23 FORMAL COMPLAINTS FOLLOWING THE FIELD TRIP.**

At least ten of them appear to defy ordinary common sense.

Na Hwa-jin says physical danger and legal liability are major burdens, but malicious bad-faith complaints are what truly break a teacher’s spirit.

Someone suggests forwarding the pattern to the special task force formed by the Minister of Education so that malicious complaints can be addressed alongside school violence.

Sera is baffled.

> **WHY DOES THE SCHOOL HAVE TO ACCOMMODATE COMPLAINTS THAT SOUND LIKE TOTAL NONSENSE?**

She imagines simply telling the complainant to stop talking nonsense and go to sleep.

Hanrim answers that she has seen similar cases before. Schools comply because the alternative can threaten the entire institution.

## Strip 002 — One parent can overwhelm an entire school

Na explains the usual escalation.

If a school rejects a malicious complaint, the parent does not necessarily stop. The complaint is sent simultaneously to:

- the local education office;
- the Ministry of Education;
- national petition systems;
- journalists and television programs;
- police;
- and any other institution capable of creating pressure.

The parent then frames the school’s refusal as proof of corruption or concealment.

Administrators become consumed by survival and eventually accept demands simply to prevent the controversy from spreading.

Na says spiteful parents often become obsessed with revenge and systematically file reports against individual teachers across every available government channel.

The weapon teachers fear most is a report for:

> **CHILD ABUSE**

A teacher receives a call from a police investigator.

The complainant claims that the teacher threw a desk, frightened students, and emotionally abused a child.

The teacher stares at the phone.

> **YOU’RE SAYING I’VE BEEN REPORTED FOR CHILD ABUSE…?**

## Strip 003 — Choi Kyung-uk becomes the fifth homeroom teacher

The teacher introduces himself:

> **MYEONJEONG ELEMENTARY SCHOOL — CLASS 5-2**

> **CHOI KYUNG-UK**

He is the fifth homeroom teacher assigned to the class during the same school year.

The previous teachers were removed, transferred, or placed on medical leave one after another because of relentless complaints from a single parent.

Class 5-2 has become known as a graveyard for teaching careers.

The vice principal begs Kyung-uk to accept the class because no one else will.

Watching the administrator break down in tears, Kyung-uk cannot refuse.

The student at the center of the crisis is **Lee Jinyoung**.

---

## Strip 004 — Jinyoung terrorizes classmates

Jinyoung is every bit as uncontrollable as the rumors suggested.

He disrupts lessons, ignores instructions, threatens classmates, and physically attacks weaker children.

Because Kyung-uk already knows the mother’s reputation, he walks on eggshells and avoids any discipline that might provoke another complaint.

That caution leaves the other students unprotected.

During one violent incident, Jinyoung grabs and attacks a crying classmate.

Children beg the teacher to stop him.

Kyung-uk knows that physically grabbing a student may itself be framed as abuse.

He therefore slams his hand against the chalkboard to create a loud shock.

> **THAT IS ENOUGH! STOP IT RIGHT NOW!**

Jinyoung freezes.

The victim is able to escape.

---

## Strip 005 — The mother turns the intervention into abuse

Jinyoung’s mother later describes the incident as though the teacher threw classroom furniture at her son.

Kyung-uk repeatedly explains that he struck the chalkboard to stop Jinyoung from hurting another student.

The mother says the result matters more than the teacher’s version.

She claims Jinyoung is experiencing severe post-traumatic stress:

- hospital treatment;
- nightmares;
- screaming in his sleep;
- and emotional injury caused by the teacher.

She asks whether Kyung-uk understands that this qualifies as emotional child abuse.

That is why he now sits in a police interview room.

The chapter shifts to the teachers who held the class before him.

---

## Strip 006 — Kim Kyung-a refuses to reveal a school record in advance

The fourth-grade homeroom teacher introduces herself:

> **KIM KYUNG-A — FIVE YEARS OF TEACHING EXPERIENCE**

Jinyoung’s mother called the school constantly and appeared in person to criticize tiny details.

One demand crossed a clear professional line.

She ordered Kyung-a to reveal what would be written in Jinyoung’s permanent school record before it was finalized.

The mother claimed she had a “bad feeling” that something unfavorable was being inserted.

Kyung-a refused.

> **UNDER NO CIRCUMSTANCES AM I PERMITTED TO DISCLOSE THE CONTENTS OF A STUDENT’S RECORD TO A PARENT AHEAD OF TIME.**

She called the demand a serious violation of teachers’ rights and told the mother to leave.

That refusal became the catalyst for a concentrated campaign.

---

## Strip 007 — 164 calls and twelve school visits

Over approximately three months, Jinyoung’s mother called the school **164 times** and visited **twelve times**.

The complaints multiplied tenfold after Kyung-a refused the school-record demand.

Unable to endure the stress, Kyung-a applied for medical leave.

On the day her leave was approved, she returned to collect her belongings and encountered Jinyoung and his mother in the hallway.

Despite the hostility between the adults, Kyung-a tried to offer a civil goodbye.

> **TAKE CARE, JINYOUNG.**

> **I’M SORRY I WASN’T ABLE TO SEE YOU THROUGH TO THE END OF THE YEAR.**

She meant the words sincerely and felt guilty about leaving a student behind.

The farewell was later reframed as another violent or prohibited encounter involving a teacher already accused of child abuse.

Kyung-a spent the following year moving between police interviews, prosecutors, and education authorities.

She was officially cleared only the previous month.

> **BUT… DOES THIS NIGHTMARE EVER ACTUALLY END?**

## Strip 008 — Lee Jin-sol and the “Referee Bear”

The second-grade homeroom teacher introduces herself:

> **LEE JIN-SOL — FOURTEEN YEARS OF TEACHING EXPERIENCE**

At that age, Jinyoung was restless but outwardly cute and charming.

During one class he repeatedly played with a plastic water bottle and disrupted everyone with loud noises.

Jin-sol called him to the front, warned him, and wrote his name on the board beside a cartoon character called the **“Referee Bear.”**

She explains that teachers once used lighthearted visual systems to list children who repeatedly talked during class.

For writing his name beside the cartoon, she was reported for child abuse.

Sera cannot understand how the act could possibly qualify.

---

## Strip 009 — The cartoon becomes “deep-seated emotional harm”

A child-protection agency issued a formal opinion stating that placing Jinyoung’s name beside a red-card-style cartoon could stigmatize him, create deep shame, and damage emotional development.

The act was therefore categorized as direct emotional harm.

Jin-sol received:

- a deferred prosecution or suspended indictment;
- a formal reprimand from the education office;
- and a civil claim for approximately **₩3 million** to cover therapy for Jinyoung’s alleged post-traumatic stress.

She believed the matter had ended.

Three years later, another child-abuse report was filed concerning a different incident from the same second-grade period.

She is still being called to the police station.

> **WHEN ON EARTH WILL IT END…?**

The imagery darkens as she wonders whether it will end only with her death.

---

## Strip 010 — Why teachers are defenseless

Na says these cases reveal why teachers remain almost defenseless against malicious complaints.

Teachers have committees and professional-rights procedures intended to protect their authority.

A parent can neutralize those protections by filing an emotional child-abuse report.

Six years earlier, after a child-death case shocked the country, the Special Act on the Punishment of Child Abuse Crimes was revised rapidly.

The revision strengthened necessary child protection.

It also left the definition of emotional abuse broad and ambiguous.

The displayed comparison suggests reports involving teachers rose from an average of roughly **thirty cases** to more than **one thousand**.

A law intended to protect children became, in some cases, a weapon against legitimate teaching.

## Strips 011–012 — Someone must enter Class 5-2

The TRPA originally visited the school to study elementary-school complaint systems.

The team now learns that Class 5-2 cannot find a sixth homeroom teacher.

Leaving the class without stable guidance means:

- Jinyoung remains undisciplined;
- classmates remain exposed;
- and every new teacher becomes the next target.

Someone must serve as interim homeroom teacher.

Na answers:

> **UNDERSTOOD.**

A tall black-haired man enters the classroom under a teacher identity.

Jinyoung’s mother confronts him immediately.

> **ARE YOU THE NEW HOMEROOM TEACHER?**

> **YOU DO KNOW WHY I CAME, RIGHT?**

The chapter ends as the agency places one of its own inspectors directly inside the class that destroyed five previous teaching assignments.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The chapter distinguishes ordinary complaints from weaponized complaints

The problem is not criticism itself. It is repeated multi-agency escalation intended to punish teachers who refuse a demand.

## Child-protection standards developed for homes are being applied to classrooms

Conduct considered routine discipline can be interpreted through standards designed for abusive family environments.

## Every teacher’s career is damaged differently

One is investigated for stopping an assault, another is driven onto medical leave, and another receives prosecution and civil liability for a cartoon discipline system.

## The victimized classmates remain almost invisible

Fear of the parent makes teachers less able to restrain Jinyoung, allowing his violence toward other children to continue.

## The TRPA chooses infiltration rather than another external investigation

The next chapter places Daniel inside the classroom to create a record of what happens when a teacher refuses to be frightened away.
