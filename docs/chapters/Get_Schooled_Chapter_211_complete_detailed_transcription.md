## Strip 001 — The official TRPA becomes the target of public anger

The TRPA team arrives at the hospital where Sera is being treated.

Reporters immediately surround the inspectors.

> **ARE ALL TRPA MEMBERS HERE?**

They ask whether the agency created the so-called Second Company through its own violent example and whether the public should trust the official inspectors after they tried to arrest a vigilante whom many victims consider a rescuer.

News coverage describes the Second Company as one of the hottest topics online.

A poll conducted by a channel with roughly two million subscribers shows overwhelming support for the vigilante:

- approximately **85.7 percent** support,
- approximately **14.3 percent** oppose.

A legal commentator argues that the crisis was predictable.

The TRPA marketed itself as an institution of heroic, extralegal punishment. Young people then copied the visible violence while ignoring the investigations, authority, and responsibility behind it.

The public now asks why the copy should be condemned when the original agency built its reputation through similar spectacles.

## Strip 002 — Vice Minister Park Daecheon suspends field activity

With Minister Choi Gang-seok away in Australia for an Asia-Pacific education conference, Vice Minister **Park Daecheon** takes charge.

He summons the entire TRPA team.

> **VICE MINISTER OF EDUCATION PARK DAECHEON**

Park says the Ministry is being overwhelmed by complaints, press calls, and accusations that the agency encouraged vigilantism.

He orders:

> **UNTIL THEN, YOU’RE ALL ON PROBATION!**

> **NO FIELDWORK, JUST STAY HERE AND CLEAR YOUR BACKLOG!**

The Second Company investigation will be left to police.

Park says the inspectors ran wild because they believed the Minister would protect them from every consequence.

Until Choi returns and the situation is reviewed, none of them is to conduct an independent operation.

## Strip 003 — The team debates the chain of command

The inspectors are furious.

They ask whether a direct agency under the Minister must obey the Vice Minister when the Minister is absent.

Na says the answer is yes.

He compares the situation to a military chain of command: when the commander is unavailable, the deputy’s lawful order remains binding.

Someone suggests calling Choi in Australia or quietly giving the police the information they have gathered.

Na refuses to openly disobey.

The agency cannot defend its legitimacy by ignoring its own command structure the moment an order becomes inconvenient.

For now, they will remain in the office and process the backlog.

## Strip 004 — Sera remembers letting Hansol go

In the hospital, Sera reads online comments.

Users say public confidence in the official TRPA has reached the gutter and ask why the agency handcuffed the person who actually helped victims.

Sera remembers meeting Hansol before she formally understood who he was.

She had found him beating a school bully.

At the time, Hansol appeared to have a reason and the punished student was clearly an offender.

Sera stopped the beating and made him promise not to repeat it.

She let him leave.

Later, he attacked her with a stun gun and nearly killed a restrained student.

> **IF ONLY I HADN’T LET HIM GO…**

She blames herself for giving his violence time to grow into the Second Company movement.

## Strip 005 — Sera catches the falsely accused girl

Near midnight, Sera leaves her hospital room while still connected to an IV stand or medical cart.

She hears movement in the corridor and follows it.

The young woman attacked by Hansol—the patient who insisted she was falsely accused—has left her bed.

The girl is moving toward a dangerous height or rooftop access point.

Sera grabs her before she can fall or jump.

The two struggle while the injured inspector tries to pull her away from the edge.

The girl finally collapses and cries:

> **I’M NOT A BULLY…**

> **WHY WON’T ANYONE BELIEVE ME?**

Sera asks:

> **WERE YOU ATTACKED BY “2ND COMPANY”?**

The girl tells her everything.

Hansol accepted a false report, beat her, and allowed the public to treat the punishment itself as proof that she was guilty.

## Strip 006 — Five days later, Sera creates bait

Five days pass.

Sera anonymously submits an urgent tip designed to attract Hansol.

The report claims an abandoned or suspicious commercial building is being used for illegal gambling and martial-arts activity. Young people are allegedly losing money, stealing from their parents, and being trapped in debt.

The location is presented as a perfect target for a vigilante seeking fast, dramatic punishment.

Hansol responds almost immediately.

He enters the building expecting offenders.

Instead, Sera waits inside.

She asks why he came and whether he expects her to arrest him.

Hansol says he also has business with the official agency.

## Strip 007 — Hansol asks to be recruited

Hansol makes an extraordinary proposal:

> **RECRUIT ME OFFICIALLY.**

His popularity is increasing while the official TRPA’s public approval is collapsing.

> **MY POPULARITY’S RISING WHILE YOURS IS FALLING…**

> **WOULDN’T HIRING ME FIX EVERYTHING?**

He says the Second Company proved that victims want faster and harsher intervention.

If the agency accepts him, it can inherit his supporters and stop appearing hostile to the people it claims to protect.

Sera realizes the livestream campaign, public confrontations, and attacks were not merely spontaneous vigilantism.

They also formed Hansol’s application for authority.

She smiles.

> **FINE. LET’S START THE INTERVIEW.**

---

## Strip 008 — The interview room is full of Hansol’s victims

Sera opens the inner doors.

The room contains people Hansol attacked, including hospitalized victims and students connected to reports he handled incorrectly.

Some were genuine offenders.

Others were falsely accused, mistakenly identified, or attacked without sufficient investigation.

Sera asks the first interview question:

> **WHAT DO ALL THESE PEOPLE HAVE IN COMMON?**

Hansol looks across the room.

She then asks:

> **WHO’S REALLY THE ONE ABOUT TO LOSE THE PUBLIC’S TRUST?**

The chapter ends with Hansol facing the human record hidden behind his popularity statistics.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Public approval becomes a political weapon

The Second Company’s support makes every official action against Hansol look like institutional jealousy or self-protection.

## Park Daecheon’s order is procedurally legitimate

The team may dislike it, but Na refuses to preserve the agency’s authority through direct insubordination.

## Sera’s regret is personal and operational

She once treated Hansol as a misguided victim acting out. Letting him go allowed the violence to become a movement.

## The falsely accused girl changes the investigation

Until Sera hears her testimony, the danger of Hansol’s lack of verification remains abstract. The suicide attempt makes the cost immediate.

## Hansol wants recognition more than partnership

He presents popularity as proof of qualification and assumes public enthusiasm should convert private violence into official authority.

## Sera’s “interview” tests responsibility

The central question is not how many bullies Hansol punished. It is whether he can answer for every person harmed when his certainty was wrong.
