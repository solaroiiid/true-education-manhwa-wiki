# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The lesson reproduces the mother’s system instead of merely describing it

Na does not ask her to imagine Hyunmin’s exhaustion. He imposes the same schedule, locks her in the same study space, controls when she may stop, and makes escape conditional on performance. The tactic is intentionally harsh because verbal evidence—including Hyunmin’s overdose and self-harm—failed to move her.

## The target continually shifts

She is promised release at an average score of 30. Once she reaches it, Na changes the target to 50. This mirrors the unstable reward structure Hyunmin lived under: achievement never produced freedom because every success became the baseline for the next demand.

## Hyunmin’s notes expose terror, not laziness

His hidden writing does not express indifference to the exam. It expresses fear of failure, longing for one free day, fading confidence, and uncertainty that medical school is even his dream. The mother initially sees the notes, but quickly converts them into another motivational project.

## Her knife attack proves Na’s warning

After a relatively short period under forced study, the mother becomes violent toward the person controlling her. Na uses that reaction to make the consequence explicit: a child trapped for years could eventually retaliate against the parent who built the prison.

## “Support” has become coercion

Na repeatedly says he is helping, rewarding, encouraging, and acting for her good. His cheerful mimicry exposes how benevolent language can conceal domination when the supposed beneficiary is denied rest, choice, privacy, and the right to stop.
