# Get Schooled — Chapter 245
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Scope

This transcription follows the **34 ordered story segments** embedded in the supplied Chapter 245 MHT, excluding its separate chapter-header image. Readable dialogue, administrative notices, parenting material, school-entry rules, police measures, and major actions are preserved where legible.

### Content note

The chapter contains physical assault, educational neglect, coercive parenting, malicious complaints, police separation of a child from a parent, and possible loss of parental rights.

## Segments 001–004 — A direct act of violence

Daniel remains bruised after Han Sunyeong’s attack.

She orders Jinyoung to stop cleaning and says she will take care of everything.

Daniel tells her that if she had waited only a few more seconds, she would not have created a far more serious issue.

Sunyeong says a parent cannot tolerate emotional cruelty toward her child.

Daniel compares the conduct:

- a structured consequence intended to teach a classroom rule;
- and a mother openly assaulting a teacher in front of her son.

> **WHICH ONE DO YOU THINK IS MORE HARMFUL TO THE CHILD’S EMOTIONS?**

Sunyeong calls her attack self-defense.

Daniel treats it as evidence.

## Segments 005–009 — The long-awaited child

Sunyeong narrates her history.

After four years of marriage and approximately seven and a half years of infertility treatment, she finally gave birth to Jinyoung.

She regarded him as the most precious child in the world.

Because conceiving him had been so difficult, she vowed that he would receive exceptional love and grow with high self-esteem.

She read parenting books, listened to experts, and studied harder than anyone.

While Jinyoung remained fully within her control, she believed the education plan worked.

The moment outside teachers began correcting him, she interpreted every disagreement as interference with her child.

---

## Segments 010–013 — She mistakes obstruction for devoted parenting

Sunyeong says she fought hard to protect Jinyoung from adults who could not distinguish discipline from emotional abuse.

She repeatedly called schools to “guide” teachers using the parenting knowledge she had accumulated.

The school eventually classified her conduct as an infringement of teachers’ rights.

She received notice requiring roughly **fifteen hours of special education** through a parent-awareness or teacher-rights program.

She was offended that a school could order education for a parent merely because she “expressed a few opinions.”

Sunyeong declined every request.

Nothing effective happened.

No fine arrived.

No criminal punishment followed.

The teachers became more cautious.

She concluded:

> **SCHOOLS CAN NEVER WIN AGAINST PARENTS.**

---

## Segments 014–018 — The new complaints office blocks her

Sunyeong calls the school and demands the immediate replacement of Daniel as homeroom teacher.

A voice from a newly established complaints office answers instead of the vice principal.

The Ministry of Education selected Myeonjeong Elementary as a pilot site because malicious complaints had pushed the staff close to collapse.

Parents can no longer contact teachers directly.

All complaints must pass through the office.

The staff member tells Sunyeong:

> **PLEASE STOP TALKING NONSENSE, DRINK A GLASS OF WATER, AND THINK ABOUT IT RATIONALLY.**

If she calls again about the same issue, her number will be blocked.

Sunyeong is outraged that a school can refuse to relay a parent’s demand.

---

## Segments 019–022 — She is barred from the school

Sunyeong goes to the campus herself.

The gate remains closed.

A security officer asks which student she represents.

When she identifies herself as Jinyoung’s mother, he says she has been blacklisted for malicious complaints.

Under the Elementary and Secondary Education Act, the principal controls facility access.

Unapproved visits can be restricted.

Because Sunyeong has already been formally identified as someone who infringed teachers’ rights, her request to enter is denied.

Forcing the gate may result in legal action.

She leaves furious.

---

## Segments 023–025 — Jinyoung disappears from school

One week later, Daniel reminds the class about seasonal influenza and hygiene.

Jinyoung’s seat is empty.

He has not attended school for a full week.

Daniel wonders whether the absence is another form of protest against the school.

The worst possibility is confirmed.

Sunyeong is keeping the child home because she refuses to accept Daniel as homeroom teacher.

She treats compulsory education as leverage in an adult dispute.

---

## Segments 026–029 — Sunyeong organizes a Ministry protest

Sunyeong orders graduate students to create protest signs.

The signs accuse the Ministry of failing children and demand intervention from the education office.

She believes Daniel must be a spy sent by a teachers’ organization to persecute her family.

She directs the students to accompany her to the Ministry.

> **GOVERNMENT EMPLOYEES LIKE YOU CAN’T WIN AGAINST THE PEOPLE’S WILL!**

Before she can begin the protest, officers from the Women and Youth Investigation Division approach.

They say a report for child abuse has been filed against her.

---

## Segments 030–032 — Emergency separation

Police execute emergency protective measures under Article 12 of the Special Act on Child Abuse Crimes.

Sunyeong screams when officers move Jinyoung away from her.

She calls it kidnapping.

The officers call it separation.

A child may be separated from an allegedly abusive parent for up to seventy-two hours while authorities assess immediate safety.

Sunyeong demands the identity of the person who reported her.

The answer points back toward the homeroom teacher and the school’s documented evidence.

---

## Segments 033–034 — Educational neglect

Daniel explains the basis of the report.

Keeping a child subject to compulsory education out of school for no special reason may constitute:

> **EDUCATIONAL NEGLECT**

Sunyeong insists the absence was a protest against an unqualified teacher.

Daniel says that, in the United States, withholding education because a parent is angry with school administration is treated seriously.

The parent is using the child like private property and taking away the child’s independent right to an education to satisfy adult emotional needs.

Possible consequences include:

- fines;
- detention;
- extended protective orders;
- and, if the parent’s presence is found deeply toxic to the child’s welfare, loss of parental rights.

Sunyeong goes pale.

> **FORFEITURE OF MY PARENTAL RIGHTS…?**

The chapter ends with the law she treated as an unlimited weapon now examining her own behavior toward Jinyoung.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Sunyeong’s love becomes possession

She believes protecting Jinyoung means preventing any outside adult from correcting him, even when the correction protects other children.

## Earlier teacher-rights procedures lacked enforcement

Ignoring mandatory education taught Sunyeong that official warnings had no consequence.

## The complaints office protects direct teaching relationships

It does not prohibit legitimate complaints. It prevents a parent from contacting and harassing teachers repeatedly in real time.

## Compulsory education belongs to the child

The school absence is not merely a parent’s personal boycott.

## The reversal is legal rather than fabricated

Daniel documents assault and prolonged unjustified absence, conduct that child-protection systems can evaluate through direct facts.
