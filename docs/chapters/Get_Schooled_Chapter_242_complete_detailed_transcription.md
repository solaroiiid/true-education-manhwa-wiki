# Get Schooled — Chapter 242
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Scope

This transcription follows the **13 ordered story strips** embedded in the supplied Chapter 242 MHT, including the short final strip that closes the chapter before the scanlation credit. Readable dialogue, complaint examples, agency identification, and major actions are preserved where legible.

### Content note

The chapter contains parental harassment of teachers, allegations of child abuse, a locked complaint room, discussion of roadside toileting and food complaints, and harsh verbal confrontation.

## Strips 001–002 — Sera defends the teacher

The angry mother continues gripping the homeroom teacher.

Sera pulls her away and asks what exactly the teacher was supposed to do.

The child ran into the road.

A safety-company employee or other field-trip adult confirms that the teacher’s intervention prevented a serious accident.

The mother still treats the firm grab and shouted warning as unacceptable.

Sera says the teacher acted in an emergency, not as an abuser.

The mother threatens complaints and legal action.

Hanrim and Sera escort the teacher back toward the school while the parent continues shouting.

The successful excursion now feels like another demonstration of why teachers avoid trips.

---

## Strips 002–004 — Sera establishes a complaints office

Back at school, Sera notices that the teachers’ room has a small, unpopular lounge or consultation room nearby.

Every complaint call routed to the school usually reaches a teacher’s desk.

Sera announces a new arrangement.

For the next week, she will operate a dedicated school-complaints office and take every grievance before it reaches the teachers.

She leaves a phone and sits alone in the windowless room.

The calls begin immediately.

Parents complain about matters including:

- a teacher photographing field-trip activities;
- a meal not matching a child’s exact preference;
- water not being supplied at a specific moment;
- and children being told to wait or solve small problems themselves.

Sera answers each call, records the statement, and invites the parent to continue.

---

## Strips 005–006 — One week of impossible demands

Sera hears an astonishing range of complaints.

One parent says a packed meal had sauce on the side when the child prefers it poured on top.

Another says a teacher should have run to a shop to buy a replacement drink after a child forgot to pack water.

One mother complains about the handling of an emergency roadside bowel movement.

A teacher had shielded the child with a jacket and allowed the child to relieve themselves because no bathroom was available.

The caller labels the event humiliating or abusive.

Sera begins enjoying the sheer absurdity of the claims.

Hanrim sympathizes with the teachers and wonders how staff endure such conversations daily.

After one week, the same parents arrive together, furious that the person answering the phone did not automatically accept their accusations.

They demand the responsible discipline teacher.

Sera leads them into the complaints room.

---

## Strips 007–008 — The parents are locked inside

Sera welcomes the group cheerfully.

She identifies herself as the temporary complaints-office employee.

She says every grievance filed during the week has been processed before reaching a teacher.

Since most consisted of insults, irrational demands, or requests that teachers perform impossible personal services, she threw them away.

The parents demand to see the principal.

They discover the room’s door will not open.

Sera produces her official identification.

> **I’M GU SERA, FIELD SUPERVISOR WITH THE TEACHERS’ RIGHTS PROTECTION AGENCY.**

She says the agency is conducting aggressive on-site audits after the recent court rulings and instability in school environments.

The parents wanted a resolution.

The Ministry has provided one efficiently.

Not one of them may leave until the complaints have been examined properly.

---

## Strips 009–010 — Sera questions the callers one by one

Sera begins with the parent who complained about the emergency roadside bowel movement.

The parent says the child came home crying because other students saw enough to identify the child as a girl.

The teacher had removed a jacket and used it as a shield because no restroom existed.

Sera asks what alternative the caller expected:

> **LET THEM POOP THEIR PANTS?**

She says the teacher prevented a far worse humiliation.

The next parent complained that a teacher took fewer photographs of her child.

Sera asks whether a teacher supervising dozens of children should prioritize safety or function as a personal photographer.

Another caller criticizes food portions and sauce placement.

Sera asks whether the school should conduct an individualized culinary survey and prepare every meal according to one child’s preference.

The parent whose child ran into traffic appears next.

She says she secretly followed because news of a previous field-trip death frightened her.

Sera asks why the woman assaulted the teacher who saved the child and threatened to make the rescuer pay for therapy.

---

## Strips 011–013 — The right to educate includes discomfort

The parents become louder.

They ask whether Sera understands how sensitive children are.

Sera briefly apologizes sarcastically and says they may be right.

Perhaps the logical solution is to eliminate every field trip, sports day, excursion, and special event.

Children can travel only between home and school, with no unpredictable experience at all.

The parents are disturbed by the conclusion.

Sera says that constant protection does not prepare a child for society.

School is often a child’s first encounter with rules, frustration, peers, and outcomes that do not match personal preference.

Adults also hear things they dislike and experience bad days.

> **GET A GRIP, LADIES!**

She says education cannot occur if every unpleasant feeling is treated as institutional abuse.

Protecting children from real danger is necessary.

Requiring every teacher to eliminate discomfort, inconvenience, embarrassment, and personal responsibility is not protection.

The chapter closes with Sera’s warning:

> **IF YOU KEEP CODDLING YOUR CHILDREN LIKE THIS, YOU’LL BE RAISING FUTURE IDIOTS!**

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The complaints office protects teachers’ working time

Sera demonstrates how much professional energy is normally consumed by demands unrelated to education or safety.

## Several complaints contain a real emotion beneath an irrational demand

Parents fear humiliation, exclusion, and injury, but convert those fears into impossible standards for teachers.

## The emergency-toilet example illustrates constrained choices

No available option was ideal. The teacher chose the action most likely to prevent greater harm.

## Sera’s “abolish everything” proposal follows the parents’ logic

A world with zero risk, embarrassment, or discomfort would also contain almost no extracurricular education.

## The right to education is not the right to total control

Children need protection from abuse and severe danger, but they also need ordinary exposure to inconvenience, boundaries, and shared social life.
