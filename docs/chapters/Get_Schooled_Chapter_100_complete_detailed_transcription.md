# The baseball life that ended

A flashback shows the president as a child and teenager playing baseball.

He initially preferred batting, then became a pitcher in high school and threw exceptional balls.

His seniors hated facing him.

Despite the talent, he never entered a proper game.

He believed his family could not provide the money needed to bribe the principal or school authorities.

His legitimate baseball life ended before it began.

The disappointment did not excuse his later crimes, but it became part of the grievance and superiority he carried into violence.

# Ga-yoon’s belief about education

The violence is intercut with a conversation from Na’s military years.

A colleague or counselor tells Ga-yoon that some people cannot be fixed and that the violent student she worries about will not change no matter what she says.

He advises her to follow professional advice and stop stressing over the case.

Ga-yoon becomes angry.

She says she is not a naive child. She understands that a person cannot be transformed by a few words.

But if teachers begin with the belief that change is impossible, what happens to the people who could have changed?

> “IF THAT’S THE CASE, WHY DO TEACHERS EXIST?”

She says that even if the student will not change easily, realizing that someone is looking out for him may drive him in that direction.

The philosophy is the foundation of the agency’s work and the reason her death wounds Na so deeply.

# Jang Kwon-hyuk becomes proof

Kwon-hyuk continues trying to stop Na.

He remembers his first legitimate salary and his mother’s happiness that he had come to his senses.

He says that without Na and the agency, he probably would have fallen completely into the gambling organization’s schemes.

Kwon-hyuk tells Na that he is not proof of an “unfixable person.”

He is proof of a person being fixed.

> “TEACHER, THE THINGS YOU DO…”

> “ARE MUCH MORE AMAZING THAN YOU THINK.”

If the agency falls apart because Na loses himself to revenge, all of that work becomes meaningless.

Kwon-hyuk’s words reach him where force cannot.

# Arrests and resignation

The headquarters is filled with unconscious gang members.

Lim arrives after securing the distributor side of the operation and says Na did good work.

She addresses him as *sunbae-nim*.

Na, still emotionally shaken, tells her not to call him that.

The scene moves to the agency office.

Education Minister Chey Kang-suk holds an envelope or written document and asks Na:

> “WHAT’S THE MEANING OF THIS?”

The document is Na’s resignation.

The chapter ends before his full explanation.

He can distinguish discipline from vengeance in most cases, but this one destroys that boundary.

Na is not simply ashamed of the fight. He fears that remaining an inspector may eventually make the agency responsible for an irreversible personal act.
