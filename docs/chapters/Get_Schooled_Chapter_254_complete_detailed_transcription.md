A call from local police or hospital staff confirms the outcome of the confrontation.

The national discussion continues while the family used as its example enters another institutional process.

## Strip 002 — Public opinion turns Yunseong into proof

News and online commentary spread details of the attack.

Some users say the father acted only because he feared Na and the proposed law.

Others argue that the child putting his father into a coma proves the country needs a Parent Responsibility Act immediately.

Na visits or speaks with the injured father.

He says years of abuse and neglect have returned to the parent through the child.

That history does not erase Yunseong’s responsibility for the final assault.

Considering the weapon and severity, Yunseong may receive a serious No. 9 or No. 10 juvenile disposition.

## Strip 003 — Forgiveness cannot erase everything

The father asks whether the victim’s forgiveness can reduce the outcome.

Na explains that the wishes of a direct victim may matter in the handling of certain offenses.

However, forgiveness is not a magic cancellation.

If the father genuinely wants the relationship repaired, he must continue trying after the court process rather than using one statement to make the case disappear.

Internally, the father still imagines beating Yunseong with his own hands.

His desire to forgive and his desire for revenge coexist.

The child is sent to juvenile detention.

## Strip 004 — The national threshold remains fourteen

Na reflects:

> **PEOPLE CAN CHANGE…**

> **BUT IS IT REALLY THAT HARD…?**

One month later, a government-led public deliberation process concludes.

A social-dialogue council holds its final plenary session and votes to maintain the current age threshold of fourteen.

The official recommendation does not lower the exemption age.

Inside a juvenile detention center in Gyeonggi Province, children celebrate the decision crudely.

They joke that they can continue exploiting the law for another year.

An officer quiets the room and tells Lee Yunseong that his parents have arrived for a visit.

## Strip 005 — Yunseong’s mother returns

Yunseong is dragged or escorted into the visiting room because he refuses to go willingly.

His mother is waiting.

> **YUNSEONG… IT’S BEEN A WHILE.**

She says she is sorry for coming so late and remarks that he has grown.

Yunseong erupts.

He says she abandoned him in hell with his father and now appears as though a reunion can repair it.

> **I DON’T NEED A MOM OR A DAD, SO JUST GET OUT OF MY LIFE!**

He strikes the barrier or struggles against staff.

The mother cries.

Outside, the father asks whether she is truly prepared to handle the trauma and rejection.

She says Yunseong’s condition is entirely her fault.

When he leaves detention, she will take him in and raise him herself.

For the first time, both parents are beginning to name responsibility rather than simply transferring the child.

## Strip 006 — A broader Parent Responsibility Act is proposed

The public deliberation’s recommendation to maintain fourteen does not end legislative action.

One year later, following sustained public pressure, the National Assembly advances a bipartisan parental-responsibility proposal.

The visible statutory material describes goals such as:

- protecting and educating minors;
- clarifying guardians’ obligations;
- preventing juvenile crime;
- and restoring family educational functions.

Provisions include mandatory family education orders, administrative fines for refusal, and stronger criminal liability when a guardian’s actions or neglect materially enable a minor’s serious violent crime.

The proposal associated with Lee Jun-bin and the Yunseong case becomes the catalyst for a national legal framework.

The chapter does not claim the law will repair every family.

It establishes that guardians cannot remain legally invisible while their conduct fuels serious harm.

## Strip 007 — Song Hansol receives a visitor

The story shifts to **Song Hansol**, the former self-proclaimed TRPA Second Company vigilante.

He is serving a No. 9 juvenile protective disposition.

A visitor sits across from him.

The man says his own family was not wealthy, yet the settlement money and a lawyer from a major firm helped reduce his outcome to juvenile detention rather than ordinary prison.

Hansol realizes the visitor played a role in arranging that support.

The visitor says he did not act from kindness.

He asks Hansol directly:

> **WHAT DO YOU THINK OF THE AGENCY?**

Hansol’s expression darkens.

He calls the TRPA a group of disgusting hypocrites.

He says he volunteered to help them, only to be discarded over a few mistakes.

He feels pathetic for ever admiring them.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The threshold debate and parental law produce different outcomes

The country keeps the age of criminal responsibility at fourteen while separately expanding guardian accountability.

## Yunseong’s mother does not receive instant forgiveness

Accepting responsibility means enduring rejection and continuing after detention, not demanding emotional closure.

## The father remains internally conflicted

His behavior has changed more than his instincts.

## The legislative result is presented as prevention

The law aims to force adult intervention earlier, before a child’s conduct reaches the level seen in Yunseong’s case.

## Hansol becomes vulnerable to recruitment through humiliation

His belief that the agency betrayed him makes him useful to an organization seeking a skilled, publicly recognizable enemy of the TRPA.
