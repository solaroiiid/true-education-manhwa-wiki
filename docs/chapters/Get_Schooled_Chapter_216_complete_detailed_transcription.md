## Strip 001 — Park Kyungpil gives Jun-bin an ordinary home

The chapter returns to Lee Jun-bin’s probation period under Park Kyungpil.

Jun-bin expected another institution.

Instead, Kyungpil brought him into a private home.

There was:

- an ordinary bed,
- home-cooked food,
- no constant surveillance,
- and relatively few restrictions on leaving.

Kyungpil handed him his first allowance:

> **₩50,000**

There were no hidden conditions.

Jun-bin, accustomed to adults who either feared, punished, or used him, did not understand the kindness.

The man caring for him was also the father of the boy he had assaulted.

That contradiction forced Jun-bin to imagine a life not entirely defined by his worst act.

- Changsung’s clothing,

## Strip 005 — Na shares a cigarette with him

Na finds Changsung outside smoking.

> **STILL SMOKING?**

Changsung braces for another lecture.

Na lights a cigarette of his own.

He says he never managed to quit, so he cannot honestly order Changsung to stop.

Instead, they speak about the larger burden.

People will continue judging Changsung.

Some will never believe him, regardless of how he lives.

Na says the only available answer is to keep showing the better side until enough time has passed that the pattern becomes harder to deny.

Changsung says he accepts the burden created by his past.

## Strip 006 — Jun-bin’s conditions for rehabilitation

During an interview or podcast, Jun-bin describes three conditions necessary for youth rehabilitation.

The first and most important is environment.

Children are still developing and often reach absolute conclusions quickly. In a violent or hopeless environment, a temporary crisis can feel like an entire future.

The second is encouragement.

A young person must hear:

> **YOUR LIFE ISN’T OVER.**

Routine support—food, sleep, schoolwork, a stable home, and adults who return—is not a miraculous special treatment.

It is the basic structure children require.

Jun-bin says statistics show that even children considered “good” can deteriorate under sustained neglect and violence.

## Strip 007 — The old gang invites Changsung back

Changsung encounters former delinquent associates.

They mock the rehabilitation story.

They call him the old loan-shark punk and say the false accusation was probably another plan.

They invite him to return to the group.

The chapter reflects on punitive public rhetoric.

Calls to abolish juvenile protections or punish every offender as permanently criminal may satisfy anger, but they can also push recoverable youths back toward the identity society expects.

Branding produces a vicious cycle:

- the young offender is denied work and belonging,
- former criminal associates remain the only welcoming group,
- and relapse is then treated as proof that rehabilitation was always impossible.

Changsung attempts to walk away.

The group tears into him verbally and physically.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Kyungpil’s method supplied what Jun-bin had never experienced

Accountability remained, but ordinary home life gave the delinquent an alternative model of adulthood.

## The cleared allegation does not prove complete reform

Na distinguishes innocence in one case from certainty about a person’s entire current life.

## Intelligence makes Changsung harder to evaluate

He committed organized crimes carefully. A genuine relapse may not look like an impulsive, easily disproven assault.

## Forgiveness requires social participation

An individual can choose to change, yet employers, neighbors, and peers determine whether a lawful identity remains available.

## The final fight preserves ambiguity

Changsung resists returning to the gang, then uses the same overwhelming violence associated with his former self.
