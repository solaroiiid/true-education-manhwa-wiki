## Strip 001 — Why keep helping youths who relapse?

The chapter returns to the older adult who supervised young Lee Jun-bin.

Jun-bin asks what the man receives from helping offenders.

The supervisor says it is simply his personality.

He compares rehabilitation to cleaning.

Some people tolerate a dirty room indefinitely. Others see one clean spot and feel compelled to clean the entire house.

A familiar, stable home can help young people from broken families recognize a different life.

Jun-bin calls the belief naïve.

The supervisor admits that many children he cared for returned to handcuffs.

He has worked as a probation officer for approximately fifteen years and knows that support does not guarantee change.

Jun-bin asks why he has not stopped.

The man laughs instead of answering immediately.

## Strip 002 — Jun-bin fights while Changsung is ordered to stay still

Back in the industrial yard, Jun-bin tells Changsung not to move.

Changsung has only recently completed probation. Joining the fight may produce a new offense and additional punishment.

Jun-bin takes responsibility for the confrontation himself.

The gang charges with bats and fists.

Changsung protests that one adult cannot defeat the entire group alone.

Jun-bin calmly says he will be fine.

His first exchanges prove that the confidence is not empty.

## Strips 003–004 — A lawyer who can actually fight

Jun-bin drives through the attackers with direct punches, throws, and evasive footwork.

Changsung is stunned that “Jun-bin hyung” possesses genuine fighting skill.

The gang tries surrounding him and attacking from behind.

Jun-bin continues moving through the group, though he also absorbs several hits.

A senior gang member mocks the wild style and asks whether Jun-bin learned from action movies as a teenager.

Jun-bin knocks down the remaining attackers.

Only the recruiter or leader remains standing.

The leader panics because the staged situation has moved far beyond what he expected.

Changsung suddenly attacks the leader.

The move reveals that Changsung knows far more about the meeting than he admitted.

## Strip 005 — Na and Jun-bin discuss “monsters”

After the violence, Changsung is confronted about whether he was cooperating with the gang.

He answers that Jun-bin has already understood.

A separate conversation begins between Jun-bin and Na Hwa-jin.

They discuss the limits of rehabilitation.

The TRPA handles cases so extreme that ordinary common sense often feels inadequate.

Jun-bin argues that children deserve opportunities rather than permanent labels.

Na says that principle may apply to ordinary or specific cases.

Some offenders appear to exploit the goodwill of every person who tries to help.

The question is whether Changsung is one of those “monsters.”

## Strip 006 — Changsung says much of the reform was true

Changsung asks whether Jun-bin’s faith was part of the plan from the beginning.

He says most of what he told Jun-bin was true.

He genuinely tried to change because Jun-bin was the first adult who approached him closely and consistently.

He did not want to disappoint him.

The criminal offer disrupted that effort.

Changsung had already made up his mind when he touched the signing money.

The chapter begins exposing a plan within the apparent relapse.

## Strips 007–008 — “This is who I am”

Changsung says everything fell apart when he saw the money.

He insists that a person’s true nature cannot change easily.

> **SO YEAH, THIS IS WHO I AM.**

He tells Jun-bin to give up on him and reform other youths instead.

Jun-bin asks him to calm down and speak honestly.

Changsung attacks, demanding that Jun-bin stop treating him gently.

He says one final thing:

> **DON’T YOU THINK YOU’RE BEING TOO ARROGANT—**

> **TRYING TO CHANGE ANOTHER HUMAN AS A MERE HUMAN YOURSELF?**

The chapter ends with their faces inches apart: Jun-bin bloodied but steady, Changsung furious and frightened that the adult may still refuse to abandon him.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The probation officer accepts a low success rate

He does not believe every youth changes. He believes abandoning the attempt guarantees that none will.

## Jun-bin’s physical competence supports his rehabilitation work

He can protect a youth from criminal recruiters without requiring the youth to violate probation again.

## Changsung’s confession contains contradictory truths

He did care about Jun-bin and also planned to use that care.

## “Monster” is a conclusion the chapter delays

Na has seen offenders manipulate adults repeatedly, while Jun-bin keeps looking for the part of Changsung that still responds to trust.

## Changsung attacks the philosophy because he fears its failure

If Jun-bin admits people cannot be changed, Changsung no longer has to face the possibility that he personally chose not to change.
