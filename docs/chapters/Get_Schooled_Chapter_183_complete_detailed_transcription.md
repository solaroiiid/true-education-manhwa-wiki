## Strip 001 — Daniel stops the underpass ambush

The chapter resumes inside the underpass where Giryong’s kickboxing seniors have surrounded Sera.

Daniel enters the fight before the attackers can turn their ambush into evidence against her.

He intercepts two men at once, catching or controlling their heads and necks before driving them down. Another attacker swings from the side, but Daniel blocks without losing his balance.

One of the masked fighters shouts:

> **WHO THE HELL ARE YOU?! SHOWING UP OUT OF NOWHERE!!**

Daniel does not answer with a threat. He begins identifying them.

He names **Lee Sangguk**, a local kickboxing welterweight amateur with a record of roughly six matches, three wins and three losses.

He then identifies **Kim Dalyoung**, a lightweight amateur with two recorded matches and two losses.

The accuracy alarms them. Daniel knows not merely that they train, but the divisions and records connected to their ambitions.

## Strip 002 — A professional future is placed against one night’s violence

Daniel says both men appear to be working toward professional careers.

That makes the current situation more dangerous for them than they realize.

Helping a school bully ambush civilians, participating in an armed group attack, and being publicly identified by the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency could become permanent “dirty laundry.” Even if they avoid a severe criminal sentence, the scandal may follow every future match, gym contract, and licensing decision.

He asks whether the attack is worth becoming the obstacle that destroys their own careers.

Daniel offers a simple choice: if they truly do not care, the fight can continue.

The fighters immediately step back. They announce that trained athletes should not be attacking civilians and carry their injured companions away.

Their sudden moral realization is plainly self-preservation, but the immediate danger ends without Daniel needing to beat every person unconscious.

He then turns toward Giryong.

> **NOW’S YOUR TURN.**

## Strip 003 — Giryong tries to reclaim the victim role

Giryong asks who Daniel is.

Daniel introduces himself formally:

> **TEACHERS’ RIGHTS PROTECTION AGENCY, FIELD INSPECTOR.**

> **MY NAME IS DANIEL HYUN.**

Giryong recognizes the institution he previously contacted.

He immediately tries to reposition himself:

> **MISTER, PLEASE LISTEN TO ME!**

He claims he only wanted the unfair committee ruling corrected and says Jungtaek is still the true offender.

Daniel cuts him off:

> **ENOUGH. THE INVESTIGATION IS ALREADY OVER.**

Daniel removes or adjusts the disguise he used at school, revealing his familiar silver hair and real appearance.

Giryong realizes that the recently assigned student-guidance teacher was also an undercover inspector.

Sera mocks his shock. She says Daniel never drops character and calls Giryong a pathetic drama performer for continuing the lie after the entire trap has collapsed.

Giryong then understands that “transfer student” Sera was also part of the agency.

## Strip 004 — One week later: the administrative hearing

A caption moves the story forward:

> **ONE WEEK LATER**

Yu Jungtaek’s challenge to the original school-violence ruling proceeds through an administrative hearing.

Kim Giryong gives a final argument.

He holds up his bandaged wrist and shouts:

> **I’M TELLING YOU! HE’S THE ONE WHO ASSAULTED ME!!**

His strategy remains the same. He insists that he has nothing to lose and therefore continues presenting himself as the victim.

The agency submits the underpass recording.

The video contains Giryong’s own words about:

- reporting first,
- manufacturing a victim narrative,
- using injuries as evidence,
- extorting Jungtaek,
- and taking advantage of Jungtaek’s knife attack.

The room can now compare his calm written claim with his unguarded conduct.

## Strip 005 — Giryong is forced to answer the same questions Jungtaek faced

Giryong argues that the video was manipulated.

He says his attack on Jungtaek was self-defense because Jungtaek tried to stab him.

The committee asks for evidence supporting that specific claim.

Giryong erupts:

> **WHY DO I HAVE TO PROVE IT WHEN I’M THE VICTIM?!**

The question is almost identical to the frustration Jungtaek expressed when the school demanded proof of years of secret bullying.

Giryong is now trapped inside the procedural logic he used as a weapon.

His assertions are no longer accepted automatically because the direct recording contradicts his credibility.

The hearing finds that he bullied Jungtaek, extorted money and valuables, and knowingly filed a false counter-report.

The original “mutual assault” conclusion is overturned.

Giryong receives a **Level 7 disciplinary action: expulsion**.

## Strip 006 — Jungtaek calls it “breaking even”

Giryong walks through the school hallway carrying his belongings. He uses crutches or a brace because of the injuries from the underpass confrontation.

Jungtaek passes him.

Giryong asks bitterly:

> **HAPPY NOW?**

He says Jungtaek suffered enough to want to kill him, while Giryong is merely receiving punishment that the system already considers appropriate.

Jungtaek smiles.

> **I AM JUST BREAKING EVEN~**

> **I HAD A GREAT TIME!**

The cheerful wording is unnerving. The agency has corrected the false ruling, but Jungtaek’s anger and knife attack have not disappeared simply because Giryong was exposed.

## Strip 007 — The school punishment is not the end

Police officers arrive immediately.

They arrest Giryong for conduct beyond the school committee’s authority, including assault and extortion.

He protests that the disciplinary hearing was supposed to end the matter.

Sera and Daniel watch him being taken away.

Sera says he is still delusional. If Giryong had admitted the truth and reflected before the evidence became overwhelming, he might have avoided expulsion or reduced the criminal consequences.

Instead, he repeatedly redefined every setback as another stage of “winning.”

Sera remarks that students can rationalize themselves into believing they have won even while every institution is closing around them.

She then reminds Daniel of their earlier argument over which boy was the real bully and demands lunch as her prize.

She wants black-bean noodles.

Daniel says they never formally made a bet, but agrees to buy lunch as recognition of her self-declared “mental victory.”

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Daniel protects the fighters’ future instead of merely defeating them

He uses their professional ambitions as leverage. The method ends the immediate violence and makes retreat feel safer than continued loyalty to Giryong.

## Giryong experiences procedural reversal

The same demand for evidence that once trapped Jungtaek now exposes Giryong’s lies.

## Correcting the ruling does not heal Jungtaek automatically

Jungtaek’s smile and “breaking even” line show that formal vindication cannot erase the psychological damage or his own serious knife attack.

## Criminal and school consequences are separate

Expulsion addresses the educational setting. Extortion and assault still proceed through police investigation.

## The new arc turns discipline into online entertainment

The campaign begins with property damage because it is easy to imitate, film, and present as a joke. Its organizer is testing how many strangers can be persuaded to attack the agency for attention.
