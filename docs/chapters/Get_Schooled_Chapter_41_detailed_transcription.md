# Chapter-level observations

### 1. Eun-ha wins because she paid attention

The match exposes an asymmetry. Eun-ha knew every element of Soo-yeon’s game because admiration made her study. Soo-yeon’s contempt prevented her from understanding Eun-ha.

### 2. Forgiveness does not erase consequences

Eun-ha’s desire to preserve Soo-yeon’s talent does not persuade Lim to overlook the abuse. The chapter distinguishes personal forgiveness from institutional accountability.

### 3. Athletic performance is defined as relational

Lim argues that a professional’s work is not isolated individual excellence. It exists to move an audience and depends on teammates who remain whole enough to participate.

### 4. The next arc shifts from physical violence to ideological coercion

The elementary-school material uses morally righteous language but reproduces the same power pattern: one authority decides who is “bad,” isolates the target, and teaches everyone else to participate.
