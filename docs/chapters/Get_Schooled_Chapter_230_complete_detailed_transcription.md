## Strip 001 — An idol photocard and the proxy-payment advertisement

A teenage fan celebrates a new limited-edition idol release.

Only around ten thousand photocards are available, so obtaining one appears extremely difficult.

She says she will run to a convenience store early the following morning.

Friends show her a card already purchased through a youth transit or payment card.

The teenager asks her mother for an advance on her allowance.

The mother refuses and says the girl must wait until the normal date.

The disagreement becomes loud and comic on the surface, but the girl is desperate not to miss the limited release.

Later, while lying in her room, she sees an advertisement for a:

> **PROXY PAYMENT SERVICE**

The advertisement promises immediate purchases through transit-card payment, with limits reaching several million won after photo verification.

It also lists high fees:

- approximately 25 percent interest for smaller transactions;
- and extreme late charges that may increase by tens or hundreds of thousands of won per hour.

The friendly design hides the scale of the risk.

---

## Strip 002 — The first purchase feels harmless

The girl obtains the photocard and boasts to friends.

When they ask whether she stole money from her mother, she jokes that a rich boyfriend bought it.

Narration explains the proxy-payment system.

The company purchases an item for the customer.

The customer repays the price plus a substantial fee.

For a small purchase under approximately ₩50,000, the teenager assumes the extra amount is manageable.

She hands over personal information and a photograph despite feeling that the request is suspicious.

The convenience overwhelms the discomfort.

She no longer has to wait for allowance dates, save gradually, or accept that limited goods may sell out.

The system turns immediate desire into immediate debt.

---

## Strip 003 — The concert ticket

The girl learns that her favorite group, **BEST**, is holding a concert in Busan.

She calls it a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.

Ordinary tickets are sold out and ticket resellers are charging enormous prices.

The proxy-payment representative suggests buying a VIP seat.

The base price is around **₩210,000**, with fees raising the immediate obligation to approximately **₩250,000**.

The girl hesitates because it equals more than a month’s allowance.

The representative says she could sell personal belongings at a pawn shop and recover the money later.

The girl agrees.

At almost the same time, her family enters a financial panic connected to missing money or a relative who has disappeared and stopped answering calls.

The girl realizes she may not be able to rely on her parents for help.

---

## Strip 004 — Debt grows while she works

The payment company begins sending threatening messages.

A holiday or grace period passes.

The company demands approximately **₩500,000 including late fees**, then escalates further.

Messages insult her and threaten her family.

The collector says that if the debt cannot be covered, they can use her photograph or other personal material.

Only then does she understand what she has entered.

She takes every part-time job she can find:

- delivery riding;
- restaurant or food work;
- convenience-store shifts;
- warehouse labor;
- and multiple back-to-back jobs after school.

She works two or three shifts, barely sleeps, and survives on energy drinks.

She sends payments continuously.

---

## Strip 005 — The debt increases faster than she can repay it

Collectors confront her in person and mock the small payments.

They say the debt is “drowning” while she sends pocket change.

She manages to repay roughly **₩1.2 million** over about a month.

By then the total demand has grown to approximately **₩7.2 million**.

The collectors order her to provide her father’s bank information or identity details.

They also threaten to make her strip and send selfies.

She says the debt feels like a swamp from which death may be the only escape.

Meanwhile, the concert she bought the VIP ticket to attend begins.

The idols perform under bright stage lights.

The girl stands alone near the river rather than inside the venue.

---

## Strip 006 — “My life was worth ₩200,000”

The girl remembers reading an article warning teenagers about proxy-payment services.

Online comments blamed the children for being stupid and immature rather than blaming the predatory lenders.

That public reaction helped convince her she could not tell anyone.

She asks whether she is really worse than the people tearing her life apart over the original **₩200,000-scale purchase**.

Collectors call and threaten to kill her.

One message says:

> **TAKE MY CALL.**

The river moves below.

She thinks:

> **MY LIFE WAS WORTH… 200,000 WON.**

She leans over the railing.

Before she can jump, a rough-looking man grabs her by the hair.

He says the situation almost went badly, not because he wants to save her, but because a dead debtor cannot be exploited.

## Strip 007 — Sera tracks Seo-ah’s phone

The collector confirms the girl’s name as **Seo-ah**.

He says she failed to answer and that it was fortunate they tracked her phone location.

A disguised Sera arrives wearing a wig, glasses, and body padding intended to make her resemble a vulnerable teenage debtor.

She announces herself as connected to the TRPA.

While preparing the disguise, she complains about the wig and padding, then becomes comically distracted by the physical transformation.

Once she reaches the collectors, the humor ends.

She beats them down.

Sera says people who threaten children and their families do not deserve to collect anything.

She throws cash onto one injured man and tells him to call his boss immediately.

---

## Strip 008 — The G.Y. Busan branch chief

The cash lands in a bathroom filled with money.

A massive bald man answers a restricted-number call while sitting in a bathtub surrounded by banknotes.

A caption identifies him:

> **G.Y BUSAN BRANCH CHIEF — JUNG WONSUL**

He calmly asks:

> **WHAT IS IT?**

The chapter ends by revealing the person receiving the money extracted through the proxy-payment operation.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The product is framed as payment rather than lending

The term “proxy payment” makes the transaction appear like a convenience service and obscures the predatory debt structure.

## Small purchases normalize the system

The first fee seems survivable, encouraging a larger concert-ticket transaction.

## Shame isolates the victim

Public comments blaming teenagers make disclosure feel more dangerous than continued payment.

## The collectors turn information into collateral

Family bank details, identity documents, location tracking, and sexualized images become leverage.

## Sera’s disguise is part of a larger plan

She is not only rescuing Seo-ah. She is forcing the cell-based collectors to lead the agency toward Wonsul.
