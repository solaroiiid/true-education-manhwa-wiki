# Strip 2 — Domestic violence interrupted

After the recurring fiction disclaimer, the story opens inside a dim apartment.

A young woman with long brown hair and large black glasses kneels on the floor. Her face is bruised and wet with tears. She clasps her hands toward a muscular, pale-haired man standing over her in a sleeveless shirt.

She pleads:

> “OPPA, I’M SORRY…”

> “I WON’T DO IT EVER AGAIN.”

> “OPPA, PLEASE…”

The man’s eyes are narrowed and furious. Before the confrontation continues, a small boy appears in the doorway.

> “M… MOM?”

The woman immediately turns and lunges toward him.

> “MIN-SEO!”

The child has witnessed enough to understand that his mother is in danger.

# Strips 3–7 — A strained family breakfast

The next morning, Min-seo sits at the table with his parents.

He says brightly:

> “MOM, YOU CAME HOME LATE~”

His mother answers:

> “YEAH~ I WON’T BE LATE AGAIN…”

> “I’M SORRY, MIN-SEO.”

She places his favorite egg roll on the table.

The scene initially resembles an ordinary family breakfast, but the dialogue preserves the household’s tension. When Min-seo eats messily, his mother warns him that his father hates it. The father unexpectedly dismisses the concern:

> “IT’S ALRIGHT. KIDS USUALLY EAT LIKE THAT…”

The mother asks whether he is going to work dressed as he is. She reminds him that it is Sunday and encourages him to take Min-seo outside.

The father agrees that the park would be pleasant because someone recently bought Min-seo a radio-controlled car.

The family’s gentleness feels provisional. Everyone is watching the father’s mood.

# Strip 8 — The “mister next door”

A loud voice calls from the hallway:

> “DONG-CHULSSS~!”

The visitor tells Go Dong-cheol to come outside as soon as he finishes eating.

> “I BROUGHT SOMETHING REALLY NICE TODAY!”

> “YOU’LL REGRET IT IF YOU DON’T COME!”

Dong-cheol reacts with suspicion and irritation.

The next panel shifts to a construction or interior-renovation site. The promised “nice” thing is not recreation—it is a job.

# Strips 9–11 — Special Purpose Interior

Several muscular workers in camouflage trousers, white shirts, and black berets stand in formation.

Their leader shouts:

> “YOU’VE JUST BECOME AN INTERN FOR OUR SPECIAL PURPOSE INTERIOR!”

> “WE SINCERELY WELCOME YOU!”

Dong-cheol stares at them, bewildered.

> “WHAT IS THIS ALL OF A SUDDEN…?”

The work crew immediately applies military discipline.

The first rule is to keep communication short and simple, and to end sentences formally. Dong-cheol is ordered to answer loudly:

> “YES, SIR!”

The foreman internally notes that Dong-cheol looks like a punk and gives a bad impression, but he cannot reject a request from Captain Na Hwa-jin.

A caption identifies the leader:

> “LEE JIN-CHEON  
> (SPECIAL FORCES RESERVE LIEUTENANT)”

Jin-cheon explains that every member of the crew served in special forces. The workers openly wonder whether Dong-cheol can endure their pace.

The job is therefore both employment and supervision. Na has placed Dong-cheol among disciplined adults who will not be intimidated by him.

# Strips 12–15 — Min-seo’s temporary happiness

At the construction site, Dong-cheol is ordered to move faster. Despite the rough introduction, he begins keeping up.

Elsewhere, Min-seo plays with an expensive red radio-controlled sports car.

Another adult asks whether his father bought it.

Min-seo answers:

> “IT WASN’T DAD BUT THE MISTER NEXT DOOR!”

> “I REALLY LIKE THE MISTER NEXT DOOR!”

He then says something more important:

> “SINCE THE MISTER FROM NEXT DOOR CAME, DAD HASN’T HIT ME ANYMORE AND BECAME NICE.”

> “I’VE BEEN VERY HAPPY LATELY!”

> “I WISH IT WAS LIKE THIS EVERY DAY!”

His mother agrees that she is relieved Dong-cheol has become kinder.

The child’s happiness confirms that the neighbor’s presence is functioning as a deterrent. It also reveals that Dong-cheol previously hit Min-seo directly.

# Strips 15–17 — Dong-cheol earns a day’s pay

At the end of the shift, Lee Jin-cheon tells Dong-cheol:

> “YOU DID A BETTER JOB THAN I EXPECTED!”

He orders him back by 8 a.m. the following morning.

Another worker observes that Dong-cheol’s stamina may be poor, but his method remains clear even when exhausted. From his technique, they infer that he spent at least two or three years on construction sites.

Dong-cheol is paid roughly 180,000 won for the day.

The amount surprises him. He jokes that if he had been paid like this before, he might not still be living the way he does.

He says he will buy chicken on the way home, remembering how happy he was when his own father brought chicken during childhood.

The scene briefly allows him pride in useful work rather than dominance over his family.

# Strips 18–20 — Teenage parenthood

A coworker asks whether Dong-cheol previously worked in interior construction.

Dong-cheol rejects the suggestion but says he spent about three and a half years doing manual labor.

He explains that he was a high-school senior while Min-seo’s mother was in her second year when she became pregnant.

She tried to conceal her stomach, but he eventually found out.

> “BUT THIS FUCKING COUNTRY THINKS IT’S A FUCKING CRIME TO HAVE A BABY.”

Both teenagers were expelled from school and thrown out of their homes.

Dong-cheol remembers promising:

> “IT’S OKAY! I’LL TAKE RESPONSIBILITY FOR THIS!”

He admits that, at the time, the promise made him feel impressive and adult.

In reality, he worked hard during the day and discovered that supporting a child while trying to continue education was nearly impossible.

The chapter does not present hardship as an excuse for abuse, but it shows the resentment from which his violence grew.

# Strips 20–22 — “Mistake”

Dong-cheol sees or remembers peers enjoying ordinary youth while he works.

He says:

> “WHY SHOULD I LIVE LIKE THIS…”

He resents that he could not study, attend university, or enjoy his twenties like everyone else.

> “IF I DIDN’T MAKE THAT ‘MISTAKE’…”

A coworker immediately challenges him.

> “WHAT DO YOU MEAN BY ‘MISTAKE…’”

> “DON’T EVER SAY THAT IN FRONT OF YOUR KID.”

The coworker explains:

> “FROM THE PARENTS’ POINT OF VIEW, KIDS CAN BE SEEN AS MISTAKES…”

> “BUT FOR THEM, PARENTS ARE FATE.”

The line reframes the relationship from Min-seo’s perspective. Dong-cheol may experience fatherhood as an unwanted life event, but Min-seo has no alternate parent or childhood to choose.

# Strips 23–24 — Dong-cheol claims there is a reason

The coworker says that no matter how difficult life becomes because of a child, hardship cannot justify being emotionally overwhelmed and violent toward that child.

Dong-cheol replies defensively:

> “MISTER, I KNOW THAT YOU THINK OF ME AS TRASH, BUT…”

> “WHY I’M TAKING CARE OF MY KID…”

> “THERE’S A REASON FOR EVERYTHING!”

He faces the reader with a cigarette between his lips.

> “EVEN NOW, I…”

> “FUCKING LIVE WITH MY FAMILY!”

A dark transition returns to the apartment. The red radio-controlled car Min-seo adored is broken and discarded in a trash container.

A black-edged narration balloon repeats:

> “WHY I’M TAKING CARE OF MY KID…”

> “THERE’S A REASON FOR EVERYTHING.”

The broken toy turns his claim into a warning rather than a defense. Something inside the family remains concealed.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Employment is used as behavioral containment

Na does not simply tell Dong-cheol to stop being violent. He places him in structured work under adults physically capable of enforcing boundaries.

## 2. Min-seo describes safety as a recent change

His happiness is inseparable from the statement that his father no longer hits him. The chapter refuses to let the warm breakfast and toy erase the prior abuse.

## 3. Dong-cheol’s resentment is understandable but not exculpatory

Teenage parenthood, expulsion, poverty, and lost opportunity explain why he feels trapped. The coworker correctly identifies that none of them transforms Min-seo into a legitimate target.

## 4. The broken RC car foreshadows another perpetrator or another cycle

Dong-cheol insists there is a reason he remains with his family. The chapter ends before revealing that reason, showing only that Min-seo’s new source of joy has already been destroyed.
