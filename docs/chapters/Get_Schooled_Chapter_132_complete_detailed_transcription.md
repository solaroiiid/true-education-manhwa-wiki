# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The natural setting is part of the control system

The mountains, streams, and distance from town initially appear therapeutic. The same remoteness removes phones, witnesses, transportation, and any realistic route to help. Beauty becomes camouflage for confinement.

## The school punishes disclosure more harshly than abuse

The principal sees Minhye’s wounds and does not merely dismiss them. He beats her unconscious and withholds food and water. The institution’s central rule is therefore secrecy, not etiquette.

## Suchan’s sacrifice is deliberate

He never planned to escape because he believes he has nowhere to return. His role in the attempt is to guide Minhye to the road and draw the searchers back toward himself. The violence he receives is the price of her survival.

## Minhye’s parents unknowingly reinforce the abusers

By apologizing to the principal, they confirm the school’s claim that Minhye is merely disobedient. The chapter shows how respectable language about discipline and character training can persuade parents to ignore direct testimony.

## The complaint reaches the agency only years later

Minhye remains haunted because Suchan is still enrolled. Her request is not revenge for the past alone; it is an urgent rescue appeal for someone she believes is still being tortured.

## Daniel’s first test begins at the gate

The chapter ends before entry is granted. His nonviolent philosophy will have to operate inside an isolated institution whose staff control communication, redefine abuse as education, and treat outsiders as intruders.
