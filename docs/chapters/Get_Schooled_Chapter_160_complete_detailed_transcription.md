### Content note

The chapter contains class-based bullying, debt anxiety, public exposure, and financial distress.

## Images 001–003 — Jaeguk enters a new school

Daniel personally takes Jaeguk to the transferred school.

He tells the boy that adults have dragged children into a conflict that should never have belonged to them. Jaeguk remains angry, but Daniel says the transfer gives him a chance to experience a classroom that does not already know his apartment rank.

Jaeguk introduces himself to the new class.

The students immediately ask the same kinds of questions he once used against Woo-chan:

- Where does he live?
- How large is the apartment?
- What car does his family own?
- Is the home purchased or rented?

Jaeguk says he came from Sunshine Land and tries to emphasize the prestigious apartment name.

Someone asks whether he lived in the rental building.

The word **“rental”** turns toward him like an insult.

---

## Images 004–006 — His mother sees the cost of ownership

At home, Jaeguk’s mother receives loan-payment notifications.

The visible amounts include approximately **₩1.86 million** and **₩543,000**, representing mortgage and other monthly debt obligations.

The apartment that gave her confidence is largely financed.

Jaeguk returns with bruises.

His mother immediately assumes Daniel deliberately sent him somewhere he would be bullied and storms out to confront the agency inspector.

She does not first ask Jaeguk what happened.

---

## Images 007–009 — Daniel waits behind the school fence

Jaeguk’s mother arrives at the playground or school gate shouting for the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency man.

Daniel meets her through a fence.

She accuses him of using her son as revenge.

Daniel produces the apartment property-registration documents and financing information.

He says the purchase was made with roughly an **80 percent debt ratio**, and relative to the current market price the burden is now close to **90 percent**.

He calls her a maximum-leverage buyer: someone whose sense of ownership is far greater than the actual equity she controls.

---

## Images 010–012 — Her own campaign damaged the property

Daniel explains that her public agitation against the rental building did not protect Sunshine Land’s value.

It advertised conflict, exclusion, and instability.

Prospective buyers withdrew after learning that one building was intentionally marked as rental housing and that residents were fighting over playgrounds and schools.

Her attempt to increase the distinction between owners and renters made the entire complex less desirable.

Daniel tells her that “owner” and “poor person” are not fixed human identities. Debt can erase the distinction very quickly.

---

## Images 013–015 — The news arrives

Reporters and cameras appear.

A broadcast describes a controversy in which a resident of an owner-occupied unit called rental residents parasites and pressured the complex to segregate children.

The report explains that Sunshine Land contains both privately purchased and public-rental buildings.

Jaeguk’s mother realizes that her own words and actions have become evidence in a national story.

Daniel says:

> **WHAT YOU DID TO RAISE THE APARTMENT’S VALUE ONLY LOWERED IT.**

The children she treated as stains did not cause the financial damage. The organized discrimination did.

---

## Images 016–018 — The family can no longer maintain its lifestyle

At home, Jaeguk’s mother stares at the bills.

Her husband says the payments cannot continue on one income and that she will have to work.

She reacts as though employment itself is a loss of rank.

The chapter then moves to a supermarket.

She begins a job wearing the store uniform, struggling to accept instructions from people she once would have classified as beneath her.

A senior employee named **Jo Suyoung** is introduced.

---

## Images 019–021 — Woo-chan’s mother is her manager

Jaeguk’s mother is brought to the floor manager.

The woman turns around wearing glasses and a red supermarket polo.

She is Woo-chan’s mother.

She smiles professionally:

> **PLEASURE TO SEE YOU AGAIN.**

The reversal is immediate. The “rental mother” whom Jaeguk’s mother described as irresponsible and inferior is experienced, employed, and responsible for training her.

Jaeguk’s mother freezes.

---

## Images 022–023 — Status collapses outside the apartment gate

Woo-chan’s mother begins explaining the work.

Jaeguk’s mother cannot rely on building number, property title, or residents’ committees inside the supermarket.

Competence determines authority.

The chapter ends with her horrified expression as she realizes that the person she dehumanized now controls whether she succeeds at the job her family needs.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Ownership is revealed as heavily financed

Jaeguk’s mother built a moral hierarchy around an apartment that the bank effectively owns to a large degree.

## Daniel does not deny the bullying risk

He transfers Jaeguk knowing that a new environment may challenge him. The chapter leaves open how much of the boy’s injuries came from actual targeting and how much came from ordinary play.

## Public exposure attacks the value she was protecting

The apartment controversy becomes a market liability. Discrimination is not only immoral; it is economically self-destructive.

## The workplace reversal is not revenge by Woo-chan’s mother

She behaves professionally. The humiliation comes from Jaeguk’s mother’s own assumptions about who should have authority.
