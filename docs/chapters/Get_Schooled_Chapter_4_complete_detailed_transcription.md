### Transcription conventions

- **Dialogue / narration** reproduces the readable English scanlation wording, including awkward grammar, punctuation, capitalization, and line-break quirks where they affect delivery.
- **Visual description** records composition, movement, facial expression, body language, setting, and significant background information.
- **SFX** identifies prominent Korean sound lettering and describes the visible action it accompanies. A precise English equivalent is not invented where the lettering is untranslated.
- **Interpretive notes** are explicitly labeled. They are used only where staging, an obscured object, or a character’s intention is not stated outright.
- The injured brown-haired student who carries out the knife attack is not clearly named in this chapter, so he is referred to as **the injured student** rather than assigned an identity.

# Chapter-Level Observations

## The knife attack is exposed as coerced theater

The chapter immediately clarifies the cliffhanger from Chapter 3. The injured student did stab Na, but only shallowly and without an intent to kill. Na recognizes this from the depth, angle, and hesitation of the strike. Instead of treating the student as the central enemy, he identifies him as another frightened victim being used by Jun-hyung.

## Na’s counseling method combines intimidation with a route out

Na is physically frightening: he controls the knife hand, slams the blade into the table, and threatens to demonstrate who is scarier. Yet every intimidating act points toward the same objective—getting the student to stop sacrificing himself for his abuser. He treats the wound in front of him, explains the legal stakes, listens to the history, and then physically accompanies the student when he confronts Jun-hyung.

## Dae-seok’s punishment expanded from school to family

The flashback makes clear that Dae-seok’s “offense” was defending a teacher from Jun-hyung. The retaliation then operates on multiple levels: direct assault, a digital order to ostracize him, public humiliation, systematic participation by other students, economic punishment of his father, and institutional suppression after his death. The bullying is therefore not depicted as a private dispute; it is a miniature authoritarian system.

## Jun-hyung’s control depends on inherited power

Jun-hyung repeatedly invokes his father’s assistant, political office, and ability to ruin careers. His followers obey because the threat extends beyond school discipline. The final news report attacks the foundation of that authority. Na does not merely beat Jun-hyung’s followers; he removes the external protection that makes the followers believe resistance is impossible.

## The injured student’s punch mirrors Dae-seok’s original intervention

Dae-seok once punched Jun-hyung to protect a teacher and was destroyed for it. The injured student later punches Jun-hyung after learning that Na will stand beside him. The visual echo is deliberate: the same act that once led to isolation now becomes the beginning of collective resistance because an adult authority finally refuses to abandon the student afterward.

## Translation and lettering notes

- “Aren’t you cute?” is preserved literally, although the visual tone is mocking rather than affectionate.
- “At the other end of the knife” appears to mean the person holding it.
- “An entire cast system” likely intends **caste system**; the scanlation wording is retained in the dialogue section.
- “The next person in the attendance” refers to the next selected victim in the school’s informal outcast rotation.
- Large Korean SFX throughout the chapter mark footsteps, grabbing, doors, trembling, strikes, impacts, phone alerts, and bodies hitting the ground. They are described contextually rather than assigned unsupported word-for-word English translations.
