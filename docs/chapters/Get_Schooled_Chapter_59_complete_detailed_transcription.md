# Strips 002–003 — A filmed apology and private rage

# Strips 004–8 — Hostile eye contact becomes retaliation

At school, students stare at Song-hak’s swollen face and laugh that he was beaten by a female teacher.

Song-hak directs a murderous glare toward Sang-wook.

Lim notices instantly.

> “WHAT ARE YOU LOOKING AT?”

She reminds him that he apologized and promised not to repeat the violence.

> “YOUR EYES ARE FULL OF HOSTILITY?”

She asks whether they need to perform the second lesson again.

Song-hak protests that he cannot even look at Sang-wook now.

Lim answers that a perpetrator does not have the right to direct such hostility toward the victim.

> “A HOSTILE SIGNAL LIKE THAT IS ALSO A FORM OF RETALIATION!”

She creates a new rule. Because Song-hak and the victims remain in the same school, he must walk with his head lowered and his eyes on the floor.

> “YOU’RE ASHAMED OF YOURSELF… AND CAN’T RAISE YOUR HEAD UP…”

> “ISN’T THAT PERFECT FOR A CRIMINAL LIKE YOU?”

Song-hak asks whether she is joking.

# Strips 009–11 — The school sees his fear

Lim studies his rigid posture and tense expression.

She predicts that his expression will eventually become sincere.

The following day, students notice the posture and understand why he is afraid to raise his head.

They openly discuss how he tried to retaliate against Sang-wook after the committee decision and how the agency beat him.

Students call him a criminal and say the committee should have expelled him.

> “KEEP YOUR HEAD DOWN UNTIL YOU GRADUATE, YOU CRIMINAL BASTARD!”

For years, Song-hak controlled what everyone could say about him. Lim has reversed that social pressure.

# Strips 012–15 — Students become the surveillance network

Song-hak finally explodes and shouts at the mocking students.

They immediately take photographs.

> “HEY! HONG SONG-HAK RAISED HIS HEAD!”

> “HURRY AND SEND IT TO THE TEACHER’S AUTHORITY!”

Other students join in, telling him that a criminal should not raise his head.

Lim reviews the flood of information.

She says she had wondered how she could verify every rule violation, but Song-hak’s own history has made the other students eager to report him.

When he complains that walking like this is impossible and “too much,” Lim reminds him that without the rule he would continue glaring at and frightening the victims.

She presses the training baton against his head and mentions the place where she previously stepped on him with her heel.

> “KARMA~ JUST ACCEPT IT.”

# Strips 016–17 — Song-hak announces that he will quit

Lim gathers the bullies for the third period of special education and says there will be a special guest.

She tells them to warm up their voices after staring at the floor all day.

Song-hak interrupts:

> “I’LL QUIT SCHOOL.”

He believes the agency came because Sang-wook was dissatisfied with the violence committee’s decision. He says he will give up as demanded.

> “DID I PAY TO GET A 5 DAY SUSPENSION?”

He says he is ready to leave school, take the GED, and abandon college.

He thinks resignation from school removes him from Lim’s jurisdiction.

# Strips 18–20 — The former victims challenge him

Song-hak leaves the classroom and says that no one should act as though they know him if they meet outside.

A group of boys blocks the hall. He recognizes students he previously treated as different categories of entertainment—the “loser,” “money guy,” and “toy trio.”

They ask whether he remembers them.

Song-hak orders a heavyset student out of the way and says that because he quit school, they can meet outside school without restrictions.

The student slams him down.

Song-hak screams that the boy is insane and that the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency cannot protect him now.

Lim arrives and pins Song-hak’s face to the floor with the end of her training weapon.

> “I THINK YOU’RE MISTAKEN.”

# Strips 21–22 — Dropping out does not erase the crime

Song-hak protests:

> “I’M NOT A STUDENT ANYMORE!”

Lim compares his logic to a drunk driver claiming that punishment no longer applies because he promises not to drive again.

> “SCHOOL VIOLENCE IS DEFINITELY A CRIME!”

> “OF COURSE CRIMINALS NEED TO GET EDUCATED!”

Whether he pays a fine or drops out, he remains responsible for completing the special-education program.

> “UNTIL THE TRAINING IS OVER, I WON’T LET YOU ESCAPE!”

The chapter ends with Song-hak physically trapped beneath the rule he tried to avoid.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Apology without changed conduct is evidence, not reconciliation

The filmed apology reassures Sang-wook, but Song-hak’s first private action is to threaten him by name.

## 2. A hostile look matters in context

Ordinarily, eye contact is not a crime. Here it is a signal from a known perpetrator to a frightened victim immediately after formal reporting.

## 3. The student body becomes willing to oppose him

Lim’s punishment works partly because many students have accumulated their own reasons to resent Song-hak.

## 4. Quitting school cannot retroactively erase school violence

Song-hak treats membership in the school as the source of accountability. Lim treats the harm itself as the source.
