# Reader page 2 — Hong Song-hak’s suspension ends

After the recurring fiction disclaimer, **Hong Song-hak** stands in a school office facing a gray-haired male teacher.

The teacher holds a document and says:

> “YOUR SUSPENSION HAS ENDED TODAY.”

He gives Song-hak a guide for the required special-education class.

> “HERE’S THE GUIDE FOR THE SPECIAL EDUCATION CLASS.”

The teacher warns that another summons will not be treated lightly:

> “IF YOU DO IT ONE MORE TIME AND GET SUMMONED, THERE WON’T BE A SECOND CHANCE ANYMORE!”

Song-hak reads the paper with a flat expression, then closes his eyes and smiles broadly.

> “DON’T WORRY, TEACH~”

The cheerful answer is performative. The following panels immediately show that the suspension produced no remorse.

Outside, Song-hak and several followers surround two kneeling students. The victims clutch their heads while the group makes a spectacle of frightening or striking them.

A jagged balloon repeats the kind of phrase Song-hak used before the disciplinary committee:

> “I WON’T BULLY OTHERS ANYMORE.”

Song-hak stands behind the group smoking, amused rather than corrected.

The series logo follows, turning the false statement into the chapter’s opening irony.

The scene cuts to Na Hwa-jin’s temporary apartment. Na sits at a low folding table in a black T-shirt, drinking from a red bottle while speaking by phone.

A voice asks:

> “DONG-CHUL? WHO’S THAT?”

Na answers vaguely:

> “ANYWAY, I’VE SENT SOME OF THE SERIOUS CASES TO YOUR EMAIL.”

> “SORRY TO BOTHER YOU DURING YOUR VACATION… I DON’T KNOW WHAT TO CHOOSE. CAN YOU PICK FOR ME?”

> “EVEN SATAN WILL CRY FACING THESE KIDS!”

The multiple images of Lim represent her rapid reactions to the dossier rather than several different women.

# Reader page 4 — Na explains the agency’s larger purpose

Na tells Lim:

> “YOU DECIDE.”

He reminds her that she is officially still on leave from the military but is also an authorized supervisor in the Teachers’ Rights Protection Bureau.

> “OFFICIALLY, YOU ARE STILL ON A BREAK FROM THE MILITARY…”

> “AND YOU’RE AN OFFICIAL SUPERVISOR FOR THE TEACHERS RIGHTS PROTECTION BUREAU, SO YOU SHOULD BE ABLE TO CHOOSE AND JUDGE IT BY YOURSELF.”

He advises her that their work is not only about finding individual students or faculty members who are suffering.

> “NO MATTER HOW POWERFUL YOU ARE, YOU AND I ARE ONLY SUPERVISORS… RELIEVING THEIR SUFFERING IS VERY INSIGNIFICANT.”

The statement does not dismiss the victims. Na means that rescuing one person at a time cannot change the conditions that keep reproducing similar cases.

> “SO OUR JOB IS BEYOND JUST RELIEVING THEIR SUFFERING, THAT IS TO INFORM AND PUBLICISE VARIOUS EVENTS THAT TAKE PLACE AT SCHOOLS.”

He concludes:

> “IT’S THE REAL PURPOSE OF THE BUREAU TO MAKE PEOPLE REALISE WHAT THE PROBLEM IS.”

Lim is being asked to choose a case whose handling can expose a broader social misunderstanding.

> “MAKE PEOPLE REALISE WHAT THE PROBLEM IS?”

The scene shifts to a large school under a clear blue sky.

> “THIS BLOODY SCHOOL VIOLENCE…”

The bottom of the page begins showing a heavyset student being strangled, transitioning from the abstract question directly into Song-hak’s behavior.

# Reader page 6 — The “fainting game”

Song-hak has his arm locked around a heavyset boy’s neck from behind.

Several followers hold phones toward them. One records video while another runs a timer.

They shout:

> “40 SECONDS!”

> “PLAYER HONG SONG-HAK!”

> “WILL HE REACH THE 1 MINUTE MARK AGAIN THIS TIME?!”

The framing imitates a sports broadcast. The victim’s oxygen deprivation is treated as competitive entertainment.

The student’s eyes roll upward and his body goes limp. Song-hak releases him, letting him collapse face-first among the others.

> “PLAYER HONG SONG-HAK! A NEW RECORD!!”

The official time is announced:

> “51 SECONDS!”

A follower says Song-hak is twelve seconds faster than his earlier attempt.

Song-hak stretches or scratches his head casually.

> “FUCK~ I JUST WORKED OUT AND SLEPT FOR A WEEK AT HOME.”

The suspension has functioned as rest and training rather than punishment.

A voice approaches, calling Song-hak terrible.

# Reader page 7 — The clique laughs about the committee

A long-haired female student points toward the unconscious victim.

> “HE FAINTED JUST AS SOON AS I COME?!”

> “YOU’RE SUCH A JERK~ HOW CAN YOU DO THAT?!”

Her apparent concern immediately turns into mock affection for the victim, whom she calls her “precious sofa” because his body is soft enough to sit on.

She crouches or perches on him while teasing Song-hak.

She asks whether he has anyone backing him and expresses surprise that Sang-wook’s persistence resulted in only five days of suspension.

> “SANG-WOOK WAS SO PERSISTENT! BUT YOU ONLY GOT 5 DAYS??”

> “I DEFINITELY THOUGHT YOU WERE GETTING EXPELLED~”

Song-hak explains that he simply attended the violence committee.

He boasts that his father mocked or coached him, and that he kept repeating the approved phrases.

He demonstrates physically, clenching a fist while reciting:

> “I WON’T DO IT AGAIN. I’LL REFLECT ON MYSELF.”

The group laughs. The apology language has become part of their comedy routine.

# Reader page 13 — Why Lim chose this case

Na speaks with Lim by phone during the earlier case-selection conversation.

> “WHY DID YOU CHOOSE THAT?”

Lim answers:

> “I THOUGHT ABOUT IT ALL DAY LONG…”

> “THIS IS SIMILAR TO STEALING A BICYCLE.”

Na repeats the comparison with surprise.

Lim explains that Korea is known as a place where a bag may remain untouched even if left on the street, yet bicycles are frequently stolen.

> “BUT THERE ARE LOTS OF STOLEN BICYCLES, RIGHT?”

> “IT’S BECAUSE ONLY BICYCLES HAVE A SPECIAL PERCEPTION.”

> “THE PERCEPTION THAT STEALING DOESN’T CAUSE MUCH TROUBLE…”

She concludes:

> “THE SAME GOES FOR SCHOOL VIOLENCE.”

The explanation is cross-cut with Lim driving a full-force punch into Song-hak’s face.

His cheek collapses around her fist, blood sprays outward, and his body whips sideways.

The theory is that weak or symbolic consequences create a social perception that the offense is minor.

The special education is designed not only to hurt Song-hak, but to destroy the expectation that violence leads to trivial consequences.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The five-day suspension functions as a reward

Song-hak treats the period at home as time to sleep and exercise. Because his victim missed school for at least as long, the punishment communicates that the aggressor’s comfort matters more than the victim’s recovery.

## 2. Scripted remorse becomes a survival technique

Song-hak knows the phrases adults want to hear and repeats them without changing his conduct. The committee measures the performance of reflection rather than the presence of reflection.

## 3. The clique converts policy language into comedy

“Mental and physical damage,” “serious issue,” and “I’ll reflect” are recited as punch lines. The adults’ official language has lost all moral force.

## 4. Lim chooses a perception problem

Her bicycle-theft analogy does not claim that every bicycle theft or bullying case is identical. It identifies the social belief that a specific offense is easy, ordinary, and unlikely to produce meaningful consequences.

## 5. The intervention is designed for public meaning

Na’s advice explains why Lim chooses a visible, notorious bully rather than merely helping one hidden victim. Breaking Song-hak’s impunity in front of his followers and classmates changes what the surrounding school believes is possible.

## 6. The fainting-game cliffhanger bridges directly into Chapter 57

Chapter 57 opens after Lim has continued the special-education games. Song-hak’s followers awaken bruised and describe being forced to play rock-paper-scissors, while Song-hak’s loss of status spreads through the school.
