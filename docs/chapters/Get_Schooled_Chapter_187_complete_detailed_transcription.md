## Strips 005–006 — Protection cannot cover every moment

The police can issue smart watches, patrol near homes, and monitor travel to and from school.

They cannot place officers beside every student during breaks, classes, clubs, and ordinary daily movement.

The agency previously helped more than eighty victims, while only four field inspectors are immediately available.

Hanrim then points to the deeper damage.

The agency’s report board has received no new submission for an entire week.

It previously received dozens of reports every day.

Daniel says he sensed the fear while undercover.

Students whisper that reporting to the TRPA means becoming the next target.

Some ask:

> **HOW DO WE TRUST THE TRPA NOW…?**

Sera asks what they should do if no victim will approach them.

Na makes a decision:

> **WE WILL PAUSE ALL OF THE CURRENT MISSIONS AS OF NOW.**

Sera asks whether the agency is going to disband.

Na answers by ordering the four inspectors to go directly to the former victims’ schools and protect them.

Hanrim protests that four people cannot cover more than eighty students.

Na says they must still do it.

> **KIDS GOT HURT BECAUSE OF US.**

> **WE NEED TO SAVE AS MANY AS WE CAN.**

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The campaign’s primary success is psychological

The attackers cannot physically reach every former victim, but the threat alone nearly stops new reports.

## Police secrecy has structural limits

A victim’s identity may be hidden in formal records while remaining obvious to classmates and local communities.

## The agency’s earlier relationships become emergency infrastructure

Former enemies, rescued students, family members, gym leaders, and military contacts expand the protection network beyond the four inspectors.

## Mari mistakes victimhood for helplessness

Some former victims are physically formidable. Others have allies. The wanted posters do not produce uniformly easy targets.

## Fear filters rather than eliminates reporting

The remaining reports come from children whose situations are severe enough that danger after reporting feels preferable to silence.

## Na uses the diversion against G.Y.

While Mari tracks the inspectors through public posts, Na traces the campaign back toward the organization and begins a direct raid on its villa.
