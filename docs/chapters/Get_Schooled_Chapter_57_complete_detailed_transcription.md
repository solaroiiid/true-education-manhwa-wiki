# Strips 2–4 — Lim Han-rim is recognized at school

After the fiction disclaimer, students gather around the aftermath of a confrontation.

A boy asks:

> “WHAT THE HELL IS THIS BITCH?”

Other students recognize the red-haired woman:

> “HOH? ISN’T THAT THE TEACHERS’ RIGHT PROTECTION AGENCY?”

> “OH MY GOD! YOU’RE RIGHT! IT’S THE RED HAIRED LIM HAN-RIM!”

They assume she has come for Hong Song-hak.

> “I KNEW THEY WOULD COME ONE DAY BECAUSE OF HIM!”

Song-hak is irritated that the agency includes a woman and appears unaware of Lim’s public record.

He mutters about the school-violence committee and teacher authority, then directs his anger toward Lee Sang-wook.

> “LEE SANG-WOOK, YOU BASTARD…”

Song-hak sees Sang-wook’s report—not his own violence—as the cause of his humiliation.

# Strips 4–7 — Song-hak underestimates Lim

Song-hak asks what Lim is holding and dismisses her.

He says he might have taken the long-haired Na seriously, but Lim is:

> “JUST A FUCKING PIECE OF SHIT.”

The series title logo interrupts the confrontation.

Afterward, Song-hak’s internal reaction becomes confused. He believes he can escape if he wants to, yet part of the restraint or impact feels unexpectedly intense.

> “FEELS FUCKING GOOD?”

> “IS THIS THE REWARD…?”

The scanlation turns his disorientation into a crude joke about discovering an unwanted new sensation.

Lim looks down at him with detached contempt.

# Strips 8–10 — The followers wake up bruised

Hong Song-hak regains consciousness.

His followers call:

> “HONG SEONG-HAK! ARE YOU DEAD?”

When he opens his eyes, the boys laugh at one another’s swollen faces.

They explain that while he was unconscious, they had to play rock-paper-scissors under Lim’s supervision.

The losers were struck.

> “WHAT KIND OF A GIRL’S PUNCH HURTS SO BAD?”

One says he thought his soul left his body.

They are especially terrified that other students took photographs and circulated them.

Someone explains:

> “SHE USED TO BE A SPECIAL AGENT…”

Song-hak curses them for losing to a woman.

The boys joke about being strangled and discovering a “whole new world,” but the humor cannot conceal that their aura of invincibility has collapsed publicly.

# Strips 10–12 — Song-hak becomes a “lame duck”

A student shows the group chat used by the second-year students.

Song-hak had not realized they maintained a space outside his control.

Messages and images show classmates celebrating the bullies’ defeat.

Song-hak asks:

> “I FEEL LIKE SHIT NOW! IS THIS WHAT THEY CALL A LAME DUCK??”

His followers agree that everyone seems happy.

One says that, at this rate, Sang-wook—the person who called the agency—will be happiest of all.

Song-hak imagines Sang-wook smiling.

> “WOOK BEING HAPPY…”

The thought enrages him more than suspension, police, or public embarrassment.

# Strips 13–15 — Sang-wook leaves the hospital

Sang-wook is discharged or preparing to return to school.

Someone tells him he could have remained hospitalized longer, but he says there is no longer anything to worry about.

> “I CAN GO TO SCHOOL TOMORROW.”

He says he needs to buy a few things and tells his companion to go home first.

A message from **Sunjung** reads:

> “Sang-wook, I’m sorry and thank you…”

> “How did you get the courage, I…”

Sang-wook responds in person or by message:

> “NO, SUNJUNG, YOU DID WELL TOO.”

> “IT’S ALL OVER NOW.”

> “LET’S GO TO SCHOOL WITH A SMILE FROM NOW ON…”

The exchange suggests that Sunjung also helped report or corroborate the violence.

For the first time, Sang-wook imagines returning to school without Song-hak controlling the ending.

# Strips 16–18 — Song-hak ambushes the victim

A voice calls mockingly:

> “WOOGI~ WOOGI~ SANG-WOOK~!”

Song-hak appears and imitates the hopeful conversation.

> “LET’S GO TO SCHOOL WITH A SMILE FROM NOW ON??”

> “HEHEHEHE, LOOK AT THIS IDIOT.”

He asks whether Sang-wook and “that piggy bastard” are filming the ending of a movie.

> “I GUESS YOU STILL DON’T KNOW ME?”

Song-hak says the school-violence committee and police do not matter.

> “IT DOESN’T MATTER IF I GET EXPELLED OR GET SENT TO PRISON, BUT…”

What he cannot tolerate is Sang-wook believing he can be happy after opposing him.

He notices Sang-wook’s injured or bandaged hand and mocks it as a new greeting.

The encounter confirms Lim’s concern: a short suspension can be followed by immediate retaliation against the reporter.

# Strips 19–20 — Lim interrupts the attack

Song-hak attacks Sang-wook.

The panels show sudden movement, a raised arm or improvised strike, and a violent impact.

Lim enters between them and counters. Song-hak’s head snaps back.

She then presses the elevator button with complete calm.

The elevator doors open while Song-hak is still recovering from the blow.

Her timing suggests she followed Sang-wook because she expected retaliation.

# Strip 21 — The warning arrived on the first day

Lim faces Song-hak in front of the elevator.

> “I THOUGHT YOU’D RETALIATE,”

> “SO I CAME TO WARN THE VICTIM…”

> “BUT I NEVER IMAGINED THIS WOULD HAPPEN ON THE FIRST DAY.”

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Public humiliation destroys Song-hak’s real asset

His physical strength matters less than the belief that opposing him is hopeless. Photographs of his defeated group break that belief across the school.

## 2. Retaliation is motivated by the victim’s happiness

Song-hak can imagine prison or expulsion. What he cannot endure is Sang-wook returning with dignity and relationships intact.

## 3. Lim treats retaliation as predictable

She does not assume the committee decision concluded the case. She follows the victim because the period immediately after reporting is dangerous.

## 4. The “three points” joke attacks the committee’s logic

Lim reduces Song-hak’s attempted surprise assault to a small score, echoing the administrative point system that previously reduced Sang-wook’s suffering to twelve out of twenty.
