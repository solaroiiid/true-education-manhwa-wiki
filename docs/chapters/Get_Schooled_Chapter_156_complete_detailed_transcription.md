# Get Schooled — Chapter 156
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Scope

This transcription follows all **24 ordered reader images** in the uploaded Chapter 156 archive. Readable dialogue, school labels, family-status questions, and narration are preserved where legible.

### Content note

The chapter depicts class discrimination among children, housing stigma, exclusion, and coercive classroom discipline.

---

## Images 001–003 — Daniel recognizes the expression of contempt

Daniel remembers racist abuse from his childhood in the United States.

The chapter focuses not on a specific slur but on the faces of the people who used it: the certainty that someone else was dirty, lesser, and naturally excluded.

He finds Woo-chan crouched and crying after the playground confrontation.

The insult **“filthy bum”** is explained through the apartment complex’s class structure. The children are not accusing her of homelessness. They mean that her family lives in a public-rental unit rather than an apartment purchased on the private market.

Daniel realizes that housing tenure has become a hereditary caste in the children’s minds.

---

## Images 004–007 — Woo-chan remembers moving to Sunshine Land

A flashback begins when Woo-chan is five years old.

Her family moves into the large **Sunshine Land** apartment complex. She quickly becomes friends with four neighborhood children, including **Jaeguk**. At kindergarten age, the children run, play, and visit one another without asking who owns which home.

The differences become visible in elementary school.

Jaeguk arrives with the newest smartphone. Other children discuss expensive trips, including a family vacation to Hawaii. Woo-chan cannot participate in the same conversations.

The decisive change occurs when the apartment buildings are repainted. Most buildings receive the new exterior design. Building **103**, the rental building, remains an older pink color.

The color makes every child’s housing status readable from a distance.

---

## Images 008–011 — Friendship turns into hierarchy

The children repeat adult language:

- Their parents own their homes.
- Woo-chan’s family lives there cheaply.
- Rental residents are “bums.”
- Owner-occupied households are the real residents.

The old friendship becomes a hierarchy.

At school, students organize themselves through a game or custom translated as **“folding.”** They compare:

- family car,
- parents’ income,
- whether the home is registered under the family’s name,
- school awards,
- smartphone model,
- and apartment building.

A child folds or lowers a finger for every status marker they lack.

Woo-chan always reaches the bottom.

Her position is not based on her behavior, grades, or kindness. The class has made her parents’ assets into a score attached to her body.

---

## Images 012–015 — Daniel becomes temporary homeroom teacher

Daniel enters the classroom as a temporary homeroom teacher.

The students are immediately curious about the foreign-looking teacher with green eyes, but Daniel’s attention goes to the seating arrangement.

The desks reveal the same residential hierarchy. Children from desirable buildings sit together. Woo-chan is isolated or placed where no one wants to sit.

Daniel identifies Jaeguk and Woo-chan as a pair and tells Jaeguk to move beside her.

Jaeguk refuses.

He says his mother has already spoken with the regular homeroom teacher and arranged that he not sit beside the rental-building child.

Daniel takes Jaeguk’s wrist before he can simply walk away.

---

## Images 016–019 — Daniel establishes classroom authority

Jaeguk protests that an adult cannot touch him and repeats that his mother already decided the matter.

Daniel answers that a student who refuses a teacher’s legitimate classroom instruction does not deserve to remain in class.

His wording is severe:

> **FOLLOW THE TEACHER’S INSTRUCTIONS OR LEAVE THE CLASSROOM.**

The room goes quiet.

Daniel does not argue about whether Jaeguk personally likes Woo-chan. He first removes the child’s assumption that parental status can override a teacher’s authority inside the class.

---

## Images 020–024 — The choice is made public

Daniel gives Jaeguk a visible choice in front of everyone:

- sit where instructed,
- or leave.

Jaeguk looks around for support. The classmates who normally reinforce the hierarchy avoid eye contact.

Woo-chan remains tense, aware that being seated beside her is being presented as a humiliation.

Jaeguk finally moves, but he threatens both Woo-chan and Daniel with consequences from his mother.

The chapter ends with Daniel watching him closely. He has changed the seat, but the social system that made the seat intolerable remains intact.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Building color operates like a uniform

Leaving Building 103 pink makes rental status permanently visible. The architecture performs discrimination before anyone speaks.

## “Folding” converts family wealth into peer authority

The children have built a game that appears objective because it uses countable possessions. In reality, it teaches them to rank classmates by circumstances no child controls.

## Jaeguk’s refusal is inherited behavior

He does not merely dislike Woo-chan. He believes an agreement between adults has made him exempt from sitting near her.

## Daniel begins with enforceable equality

He cannot instantly change Jaeguk’s values, but he can prevent the classroom from officially reproducing the apartment hierarchy.
