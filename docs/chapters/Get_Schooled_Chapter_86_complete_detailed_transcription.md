# Opening — False complaints are different

Lim clarifies that the criminal-law warning is primarily caution.

Reasonable questions and complaints remain legitimate.

Malicious false complaints are different.

The agency came to Hyunjung Elementary because parents accused Mrs. Choi of abusing children and abusing her authority.

After investigating the class, students, committee records, and school conduct, the agency found no student harmed by Choi.

If the final result confirms her innocence, the attempt to end her career through lies will be treated as a malicious complaint and pursued without mercy.

Woo-jin’s mother rejects the result and accuses the agency of failing to investigate properly.

---

# The mother tries to mobilize the neighborhood

She posts that the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency is openly taking the teacher’s side and threatening parents with criminal punishment.

She announces a plan to take mothers and civic groups to the Ministry of Education.

Some users support her.

Others begin questioning the story.

Parents from the same school say their children never described Choi as violent. One commenter says the posts read like unrealistic fiction and that the author appears to be the person creating trouble.

Woo-jin’s mother is startled that the community is no longer moving as a single block behind her.

She privately fears prison, a fine, her husband discovering the lie, and the family being unable to remain in the neighborhood after their reputation collapses.

They cannot simply move because the apartment cost a fortune.

---

# Mrs. Choi is cleared

The agency tells Choi that the investigation found she did absolutely nothing wrong.

She may return to work the following day.

Na and Lim explain that more than half of elementary teachers report similar parent harassment.

They are considering a criminal case not only for Choi’s personal revenge, but to change an atmosphere in which parents can indiscriminately attack teaching rights without consequences.

Choi admits that she feels anger and wants the mother punished.

Then she asks about Woo-jin.

He is twelve, approaching puberty, and not a bad child despite one fight with a classmate.

A criminal case against his mother may injure him and destabilize his home.

Choi asks the agency to let the matter go for his sake.

---

# A transactional apology

Woo-jin’s mother visits Choi.

She says she admits she went too far because of her child and apologizes.

The apology immediately becomes a bargain.

She asks Choi to admit one thing in return: that she sided with Sung-min during the school-violence committee and acted against Woo-jin.

If Choi acknowledges that accusation, the mother will admit that she became angry and made excessive complaints.

Choi refuses.

She submitted the committee opinion to the Ministry, and the records prove that the claim is false.

---

# The final offer

The agency offers Woo-jin’s mother a path away from criminal charges.

She must publicly admit that she lied and apologize to the school.

The mother refuses.

Public admission would tell the entire neighborhood she was wrong.

She says she would rather see how far Choi’s “hypocrisy” goes.

---

# Lim’s final day in class

Lim completes her temporary teaching assignment.

Most students applaud and thank her. They say she was clumsy but improved and made the class fun.

Woo-jin suddenly insults her.

He says she cannot teach, does not know mathematics, and should remain home.

Mrs. Choi orders him to apologize.

Woo-jin refuses and asks what anyone can do when he is only telling the truth.

The chapter ends with the child beginning the provocation his mother designed.
