# Reader pages 3–4 — The agency’s youth center

# Reader page 5 — Why the center matters

Na and Lim discuss the broader pattern.

Most runaways do not leave home intending to become criminals.

The immediate problem is that there is nowhere safe to remain.

Once food, sleep, and education are available, children can begin planning a future rather than choosing between exploitation and return to abuse.

Within fewer than three days, the agency has recovered more than half the children from the helper network.

Na says the next step begins when the remaining youths are found.

# Reader pages 12–13 — Yeri rejects the false choice

Yeri steps between the leader and the rescued youths.

The woman argues that there is no safe place outside abusive homes and exploitive helper houses.

Yeri answers that there really is one.

The agency center behind her is the proof.

The chapter ends as parents begin responding to the leader’s messages and the shelter prepares for a confrontation over who has the right to decide where the children live.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The helper leader depends on the absence of public shelter

Her business works only because children believe the alternatives are home, street, or exploitation.

## 2. The center changes behavior through material safety

Food, sleep, study, and freedom from immediate parental return persuade the runaways more effectively than threats.

## 3. Park Seol-young remains trapped by family information

Even a senior participant in the network can be controlled through the threat of being returned to an abusive adult.

## 4. The next conflict is legal rather than hidden

Once parents are contacted, the agency must defend the children’s right to remain away from home against formal parental authority.
