# Reader pages 2–3 — Thirteen days earlier

# Reader page 4 — Park Eun-ha’s athletic beginning

A height measurement identifies:

> “179 CM, PARK EUNHA.”

Someone comments that Eun-ha grew four centimeters since the previous year and asks why she does not work out.

Eun-ha narrates that she was already 175 cm tall in her first year of middle school. Coaches visited her house almost every day.

She tried several sports—baseball, volleyball, and judo—before choosing basketball.

There was one specific reason basketball became her main sport.

# Reader page 5 — Kang Soo-yeon, the admired ace

Eun-ha remembers **Kang Soo-yeon**, one year above her.

Soo-yeon dominated middle-school basketball through talent, especially a perfect one-handed shooting form compared to men’s play.

Eun-ha dreamed of wearing the same jersey as her.

When she finally enters high school, Eun-ha introduces herself as a freshman, says she is 186 cm tall, and is willing to play any position.

Her excitement is genuine. She has worked for two years to enter the prestigious women’s-basketball school where Soo-yeon plays.

# Reader page 6 — The sports-club hierarchy

Eun-ha quickly learns that sports-club life is not easy.

Morning practice, afternoon practice, and repeated conditioning fill every day.

Freshmen are yelled at for minor issues under the label of “hardening training.”

A short-haired freshman named **Kang Ja-yeon** is treated differently.

# Reader page 7 — Ja-yeon’s protection

Ja-yeon is Soo-yeon’s younger sister.

Although both girls are freshmen, Ja-yeon is exempted from collective punishment because her sister is the team ace.

Eun-ha initially envies her.

She tells herself that harsh discipline is simply part of being on the team and that she can endure it as long as necessary.

The unequal exemption does not yet look like the beginning of systematic abuse.

# Reader pages 8–9 — Ja-yeon turns Eun-ha into a servant

Eun-ha and Ja-yeon become roommates.

Ja-yeon proves demanding and governed entirely by mood.

She repeatedly makes Eun-ha switch beds despite both being acceptable.

She orders Eun-ha to buy two Häagen-Dazs ice creams and sanitary wipes.

She refuses to do her own laundry and calls Eun-ha whenever she wants chores completed.

At first, Eun-ha obeys several times.

Eventually she refuses to go to the store.

Ja-yeon erupts:

> “YOU FUCKING BITCH, GET GOING!”

The confrontation becomes physical.

# Reader page 10 — Eun-ha fights back

The two girls grapple.

Eun-ha’s body and balance reveal that she has experience beyond basketball.

Her narration says that before basketball, the sport she practiced most was judo.

Ja-yeon is shocked that resistance comes so easily.

Eun-ha’s refusal should have ended an unreasonable demand. Instead, it triggers retaliation through the team hierarchy.

# Reader page 11 — Soo-yeon protects her sister

The captain confronts Eun-ha over an improperly cleaned shower.

Eun-ha says she was not assigned the shower that week.

> “THIS WEEK WAS JA-YEON…”

Soo-yeon says Ja-yeon claimed Eun-ha was responsible.

> “YOU MEAN MY SISTER’S LYING?”

Soo-yeon attacks or strikes her before allowing an explanation.

The captain’s authority converts her sister’s private lie into an official team judgment.

# Reader page 12 — Basketball exclusion

Eun-ha describes the price of touching the ace’s sister as “beyond belief.”

From that day onward, teammates stop passing her the ball.

She becomes isolated during training.

Her only permitted role is massaging Soo-yeon and Ja-yeon.

The punishment continues after the school year changes.

Days and weeks pass in which she cheers and serves others without being allowed meaningful contact with the basketball.

The sport she chose because of admiration is taken away while she is forced to remain physically present beside it.

# Reader page 13 — Why she cannot simply quit

People tell Eun-ha it would be easier to leave basketball.

She explains that quitting is not easy after building her life around sports since middle school.

Then the school announces good news:

> “KANG SOO-YEON AND KANG JA-YEON HAVE BEEN CHOSEN FOR THE ASIAN GAMES NEXT YEAR.”

The team is ordered to congratulate them.

Eun-ha watches the sisters receive a perfect future while other athletes abandon their dreams because of bullying.

# Reader page 14 — Failed disclosure and suicidal despair

Reporters arrive to cover the successful sisters.

A victim attempts to reveal the truth but cannot complete the disclosure.

Afterward, the bullying becomes so severe that Eun-ha reaches the point of suicide.

Her last hope is the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency.

Before that hope can arrive openly, Ja-yeon yells at her for failing to pass on a notice that the new coach would arrive thirty minutes early.

The pattern remains intact: any administrative detail can become a reason to attack the isolated player.

# Reader page 15 — The original coach is suddenly absent

Team members assemble and discuss the coach.

They say she was injured suddenly over the weekend, possibly after falling while drinking.

The sisters hope the substitute will not care about what the team does.

> “WE’RE ON THE NATIONAL TEAM. WHAT’S SHE GONNA DO?”

Their confidence rests on status. They believe no school employee will risk the careers of two nationally selected players.

# Reader page 16 — Lim Han-rim becomes substitute coach

A new figure enters the gym.

Lim wears sports clothes and carries herself with military directness.

> “I’LL BE YOUR SUBSTITUTE COACH.”

> “LET’S GET ALONG WELL FROM NOW ON.”

Ja-yeon privately tells her sister she probably does not need to worry about the small new coach.

The chapter ends on Lim’s calm face and the sisters’ dismissive confidence, setting up the collision between team authority and the agency’s field inspector.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Eun-ha’s admiration intensifies the betrayal

Soo-yeon is not merely an abusive captain. She is the player whose skill originally gave Eun-ha a basketball dream.

## 2. Ja-yeon’s privilege begins with a small exemption

Being excused from freshman discipline seems minor, but it teaches the entire team that the captain’s sister exists outside ordinary accountability.

## 3. Isolation is athletic violence

Preventing Eun-ha from touching the ball attacks her development, identity, and future just as directly as physical assault.

## 4. National selection protects the abuse

The sisters believe athletic value makes them institutionally untouchable. Their success becomes a reason adults and teammates avoid confronting them.
