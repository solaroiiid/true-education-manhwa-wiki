# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Na’s cover protects Daniel from immediate dismissal

By pretending to verify a support project, Na makes Daniel appear financially valuable to the school. The principal’s instant flattery confirms that material benefit, not educational philosophy, determines whom he tolerates.

## Daniel separates explanation from absolution

He believes Suchan’s violence has a motive beyond ordinary status-seeking, but explicitly rejects sympathy that erases victims. Understanding the causal system is necessary because the adult institution appears to be producing aggressors deliberately.

## The principal selects children unlikely to be defended

His admission that most residents are effectively abandoned is central. The school’s isolation and brutality are sustainable because parents, authorities, or communities are unlikely to investigate injuries or believe complaints.

## Labor is disguised as collectivist education

Digging a decorative pond benefits the school while being described as cooperation and discipline. The children are motivated not by shared purpose but by Suchan’s threat that the slowest worker will be buried.

## Medical neglect is part of the philosophy

The principal refuses hospital care for a severe ankle injury, then deliberately strikes Suchan in the same area. Pain is treated as a teaching instrument, and seeking outside treatment would expose the school to scrutiny.

## Daniel identifies the hierarchy of responsibility

Suchan remains accountable for abusing younger students. However, the principal actively rewards, organizes, and models violence. Daniel’s final decision is to direct the harshest consequence toward the adult who created and maintains the system.
