## Images 004–006 — Hanrim requests an official review

Hanrim asks whether the school has a culture of looking down on substitute teachers.

She notes that other temporary teachers appear to have experienced similar rights violations.

> **WOULDN’T IT BE GOOD TO INVESTIGATE USING THIS OPPORTUNITY AND CORRECT THE STUDENTS’ HABIT OF INFRINGEMENT OF TEACHERS’ RIGHTS?**

The head teacher explodes:

> **WHAT, YOU LITTLE…**

> **HOW DARE A BRAT LIKE YOU TALK BACK TO ME…**

Hanrim states formally:

> **I REQUEST A REVIEW BY THE TEACHERS’ RIGHTS PROTECTION COMMITTEE.**

Lee Seoni rushes in and pulls her away while the administrator shouts.

On the rooftop, Lee Seoni gives Hanrim a drink and says the committee will not convene.

Hanrim replies that all teachers have rights.

Lee Seoni says she will go speak to the administrators herself.

## Images 007–009 — The previous substitute teacher’s case

Lee Seoni explains that temporary teachers are non-regular workers, not permanent staff.

A flashback introduces **Yoo Changjin**, a ten-year veteran substitute teacher.

He was liked by students and performed classes and duties as well as any permanent teacher.

During the previous year, however, second-year high-school boys began testing him.

Anonymous posts circulated among students identifying which teachers were substitutes.

They mocked him:

> **I HEARD YOU’RE A SUBSTITUTE!**

> **YOU’RE A MERE PART-TIMER. SHOULD YOU BE SAYING SHIT TO PRECIOUS STUDENTS LIKE US?**

The harassment escalated from jokes to physical abuse, insults, and humiliating classroom behavior.

Changjin documented the incidents and requested a Teachers’ Rights Protection Committee meeting.

The principal asked why he needed to make such a big issue over students playing around.

Another official said:

> **YOU’RE A SUBSTITUTE, SO JUST FOCUS ON TEACHING. TALKING ABOUT RIGHTS? AS IF YOU HAVE ANY.**

The principal lied that substitute teachers did not qualify under the relevant education law.

In reality, the educational administration manual and Teaching Rights Act include substitute educators.

The principal’s approval never came through, so the committee never convened.

Changjin’s contract was not renewed, and he left the school.

## Images 010–012 — Students learn that substitutes cannot fight back

After Changjin’s departure, certain students become completely uncontrollable around temporary teachers.

They smoke in front of them, walk out of class, throw objects, and openly say:

> **YOU’RE A DAMN PART-TIMER. STAY A DAMN PART-TIMER. DON’T OVERDO IT.**

The problem is not only individual cruelty.

The students watched the school refuse to protect Changjin.

They learned that a substitute can be assaulted without consequences.

Lee Seoni summarizes the only practical survival strategy:

> **STAY SILENT AND ENDURE IT.**

A sudden movement interrupts the rooftop conversation.

A student grabs or hooks Hanrim’s feet and trips her.

She falls violently while Cheolyong and his friends laugh.

## Images 013–015 — Why substitute teachers remain silent

Hanrim says:

> **HE GRABBED YOUR FEET AND TRIPPED YOU…**

> **THAT’S ASSAULT.**

Lee Seoni answers that she already knows.

Hanrim asks why she will simply accept it.

Lee Seoni smiles sadly:

> **IN THIS WORLD, THERE ARE PEOPLE WHO CAN’T SURVIVE IF THEY DON’T GET MONTHLY INCOME EVEN FOR ONE MONTH.**

> **WE GOTTA MAKE A LIVING, SO WE DON’T HAVE A CHOICE, DO WE?**

She explains:

- schools do not renew temporary teachers who cause conflict with students,
- rumors spread and make employment at another school difficult,
- contracts last only six months to a year,
- and even if the agency proves discrimination, the school can say the contract simply ended.

Help from the TRPA would not automatically protect the victim’s livelihood.

Hanrim finally understands why almost no substitute teachers report abuse.

> **THIS IS DEFINITELY DIFFERENT FROM ANYTHING I’VE HANDLED BEFORE.**

Even punishing Cheolyong and his friends might leave Lee Seoni unemployed.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The school’s reaction proves Hanrim’s hypothesis

A substitute’s single “accidental” injury produces immediate administrative fury. Repeated student abuse produces excuses, silence, and threats against the teacher.

## Changjin’s case taught the students institutional immunity

The boys know substitutes are identifiable, temporary, and dependent on contract renewal. Their power comes from watching adults abandon an earlier victim.

## Lee Seoni’s silence is economic, not moral

She recognizes assault clearly. She cannot risk missing even one month of income or being blacklisted through informal rumors.

## Hanrim separates the protected victim from the complainant

If Hanrim’s undercover identity files the case, Lee Seoni can remain outside the official conflict. The tactic attempts to expose the system without sacrificing the teacher who depends on it.

## The new male figure signals escalation

His language matches the agency’s formal mission, but the chapter withholds his complete introduction. His arrival turns Hanrim’s improvised violence into the beginning of a documented Teachers’ Rights Protection case.
