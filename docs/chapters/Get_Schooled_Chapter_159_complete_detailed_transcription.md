## Images 001–002 — Sera confronts Jaeguk’s mother

The chapter opens outside the elementary school beneath a promotional line for HiveToon.

An adult woman with her hair tied up and round glasses recoils with a cry:

> **ACK!**

She appears to have been struck or sharply stopped by **Gu Sera**, who is wearing her purple varsity jacket.

The woman screams:

> **HOW DARE YOU HIT ME?! I’LL CALL THE POLICE!!**

Sera answers without fear:

> **SURE, GO AHEAD. WE’LL SEE WHO SUFFERS AT THE END.**

She presents the situation as a legal contest. On one side is a civil servant from the **Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency** carrying out official duties. On the other is a parent who entered the school, interrupted class, and violated a teacher’s rights.

Sera adds that the agency is supervised by the Ministry of Education and implies that Jaeguk’s transfer cannot simply be reversed through a parent’s threats.

The mother’s outrage turns into calculation. Sera has already prepared several possible bureaucratic responses on her phone—appeals to the Ministry, questions about the legality of the transfer, and complaints about the agency’s authority.

Sera later says she followed the prepared script exactly and expects the parent to understand the warning.

Jaeguk’s mother does not reflect on her own behavior. She immediately concludes:

> **RIGHT… IT MUST BE THAT SUBSIDIZED RENTER’S DOING.**

She believes Woochan’s family reported her.

## Images 003–005 — Rumors spread through the classroom

The series title card appears.

Daniel dismisses the class:

> **THAT’S IT FOR TODAY’S CLASS. BE CAREFUL ON YOUR WAY HOME.**

Students begin leaving in pairs.

Two girls whisper that **Jaeguk transferred schools**. They assume the reason must be that Woochan reported him and describe Woochan as frightening.

A quieter student remains behind and approaches Daniel:

> **AH, TEACHER… I HAVE SOMETHING TO TELL YOU…**

Before the student can explain, Sera enters and loudly reminds everyone that class is over.

She says she saw fifth period was the last scheduled class and urges Daniel to leave quickly.

Daniel senses the student was about to reveal something important:

> **AH, PLEASE WAIT A MINUTE. HE WAS JUST…**

The student retreats:

> **I-IT’S NOTHING…**

Daniel watches the child leave with concern.

## Images 006–009 — Sera resents being treated as Daniel’s assistant

Outside school, Daniel thanks Sera for arriving early.

He explains that he requested assistance from the team leader but did not expect Sera to be sent specifically.

A flashback to the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency office shows Sera protesting the assignment:

> **THIS ISN’T RIGHT! WHY SHOULD I GO HELP HIM?!**

She complains that Daniel arrived later than she did and should therefore be below her in seniority.

Hanrim or another senior calmly points out that Daniel is currently acting as an elementary-school teacher. Elementary teachers must handle all subjects, so an additional capable person can help.

Sera is horrified:

> **A-ALL SUBJECTS?**

The senior tells her to switch roles with Daniel if she believes she can manage the classroom herself.

Sera tries to reframe the relationship:

> **I MAY BE YOUR ASSISTANT— I MEAN HELPER,**

> **BUT WE’RE STILL PEERS AND EQUALS, GOT IT? I’M NOT UNDER YOU!**

Daniel says he had not thought about it that way.

When he suggests returning to the agency after only twenty minutes, Sera refuses and says she will stay to monitor the situation a little longer.

Daniel looks down the street and reacts:

> **ARE YOU CRAZY?!**

A crowd has gathered around the apartment-complex fence.

## Images 011–015 — Owner-residents block the rental residents

At the apartment entrance, older residents argue in front of a tall green fence.

A grandfather shouts at his granddaughter or younger relative for speaking disrespectfully:

> **GRANDPA, WHAT DID WE DO WRONG FOR YOU TO BE SAYING THAT?**

> **WHAT DID YOU DO? WHAT A LOAD OF BULLSHIT!**

He points at the barrier and demands to know how residents can be locked away by the fence.

Other rental residents insist they are not criminals. They explain that there is a side entrance behind Building 103, but the route is inconvenient and humiliating.

An owner-resident woman in a yellow patterned dress dismisses their complaints:

> **WOW~ YOU SURE HAVE A LOT OF DEMANDS~**

She says the private owners pay for road and garden maintenance and asks why rental residents contribute so little.

> **YOU PEOPLE ONLY PAY RENT EQUAL TO ONE MONTH’S MAINTENANCE FEE. YOU SHOULD BE THANKFUL JUST TO BREATHE HERE.**

She accuses them of wanting all the benefits without paying the price and concludes:

> **THIS IS WHY POOR PEOPLE ARE HOPELESS.**

The confrontation becomes personal when **Woochan’s mother**, a woman with glasses and tied-back dark hair, steps forward.

She asks why the owners are doing this. Not long ago, they locked the playground and made the children cry; now they are also blocking the entrance.

The yellow-dressed woman recognizes her:

> **OH~ WOOCHAN’S MOM?**

She claims the owners never intended to go this far, but says the rental residents are “making” them angry.

Then she asks whether Woochan’s mother called the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency.

She argues that the playground closure was necessary because the rental residents’ children discriminated against—or caused trouble for—the owners’ children. Her logic reverses the actual power relationship.

Her expression becomes openly hateful:

> **ARE YOU TRYING TO BRING THE DAMN PROPERTY VALUE IN THE AREA?!**

She calls the rental residents nuisances who merely exist, warns that their presence will reduce future housing prices, and tells them to live separately.

## Images 016–017 — Daniel reveals that he is both an inspector and a resident

Daniel and Sera reach the crowd.

Daniel says Woochan’s mother had nothing to do with the agency’s arrival.

He identifies himself:

> **NO ONE CALLED THE TEACHERS’ RIGHTS PROTECTION AGENCY. I’M A MEMBER OF THE AGENCY.**

He adds that he raised the issue personally as a resident of the apartment building.

The owner-residents are stunned.

The yellow-dressed woman remembers that Daniel said he lived there and immediately concludes that he must also be a subsidized renter:

> **MAKES SENSE. WITH A GOVERNMENT SALARY, A RENTAL IS ALL YOU CAN AFFORD~**

She then argues that this is not a school, so the agency has no authority over the fence dispute.

She gives Daniel an ultimatum: cancel Jaeguk’s school transfer and the owners may “consider” removing the fence.

Daniel smiles and tells her she has mistaken the order of events.

## Images 019–020 — Daniel reverses the ultimatum

Daniel answers:

> **IF YOU REMOVE THAT FENCE, I’LL THINK ABOUT JAEGUK’S TRANSFER.**

He also orders the residents to make the playground open to every child.

He explains:

> **WE CAN’T HAVE CHILDREN FEELING DISPARITY AND DISCRIMINATION FROM SUCH A YOUNG AGE.**

The owner-resident woman scoffs that subsidized renters expect no consequences despite contributing less money.

She agrees to continue the confrontation:

> **FINE~ LET’S SEE WHO WINS~**

Sera quietly asks Daniel whether she can run over and smack the woman on the back of the head.

Daniel refuses the simple beating.

> **PHYSICAL PAIN FROM VIOLENCE DOESN’T TEACH YOU ANYTHING.**

He says that if they want to teach the woman a lesson, a merely “tame” method resembling violence will not work.

A white SUV drives away.

## Images 021–022 — Jaeguk’s mother sees Daniel with her son

Inside the car, Jaeguk’s mother complains that canceling the transfer is not enough. She wants the rental residents removed entirely.

Her internal monologue is contemptuous. She tells herself the poor residents may blame her now, but will soon begin resenting the agency as well. In her view, poor people always blame one another, which is why they never make progress.

The SUV stops near the residential area.

She warns Jaeguk:

> **DON’T GET CLOSE WITH THE KIDS HERE. THEY’RE NOT ON THE SAME LEVEL AS US!**

She says they will return home and decide how to pressure the teacher further.

Then she notices something through the car window.

Daniel is standing beside—or physically guiding—Jaeguk near a school boundary.

Her expression becomes panicked:

> **WHAT THE—! WHY IS HE OVER THERE?!**

> **WHAT’S HE TRYING TO DO TO MY SON?**

The chapter ends with Daniel looming over Jaeguk, setting up the transfer-school lesson continued in Chapter 160.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The chapter fills the missing bridge between Chapters 158 and 160

Chapter 158 ended with the transfer plan. Chapter 159 shows the residents’ fence protest, Daniel’s condition for reconsidering Jaeguk’s transfer, and Jaeguk’s mother discovering Daniel with her son.

## The apartment conflict extends beyond school seating

The owner-residents control physical access to the playground, entrances, roads, and gardens. Housing discrimination has become an organized community policy rather than isolated children’s insults.

## Sera and Daniel represent different disciplinary instincts

Sera wants immediate physical retaliation. Daniel argues that pain alone will not change the owner-resident’s worldview and begins designing a lesson based on social experience and reversed power.

## Jaeguk’s mother believes financial contribution determines human entitlement

She repeatedly converts maintenance fees and property values into permission to exclude children and families from shared life.

## Daniel’s identity collapses her categories

He is simultaneously a government official and a resident she assumes is subsidized. She cannot dismiss rental residents as powerless outsiders once one of them holds official authority.
