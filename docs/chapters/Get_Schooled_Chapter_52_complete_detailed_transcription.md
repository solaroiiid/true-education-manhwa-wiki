After the fiction disclaimer, the construction-site conversation continues.

# Strips 3–6 — The RC car breaks

At home, Min-seo begins screaming and crying.

His red radio-controlled car has crashed into a wall and broken.

An adult says that crying is useless, but another points out that Min-seo did not intentionally do anything wrong.

Dong-cheol calms him:

> “STOP CRYING.”

> “I’M WORKING NOW, SO I’LL BUY YOU A BETTER ONE LATER.”

> “I BROUGHT CHICKEN, SO JUST EAT THIS FOR NOW.”

The father’s response is patient and materially reassuring. For the moment, the intervention appears to be working.

# Strips 7–9 — The family briefly resembles its past

Min-seo’s mother asks:

> “OPPA, YOU’RE WORKING NOW…? DID YOU GET A JOB?”

She is shocked that he earned approximately 180,000 won in a single day.

Dong-cheol says he will work again the next day and tells her to send Min-seo to daycare.

His body aches from labor, but she smiles and says:

> “OPPA, IT FEELS JUST LIKE THE OLD DAYS.”

Dong-cheol quietly agrees:

> “YEAH, LIKE THE OLD DAYS…”

The scene suggests the period before resentment and violence overtook their relationship.

# Strips 10–12 — Daycare conflict over toy cars

At daycare, children play with toy vehicles.

Min-seo makes engine noises while moving a forklift. Another child tells him the noise is wrong for that kind of machine.

A teacher or caregiver comments that Min-seo has changed recently and seems relieved. She wonders how his father managed it.

The children then argue over a toy police car.

One child refuses to trade and says Min-seo can keep playing with the forklift.

When Min-seo asks again, the other boy says:

> “I HEARD YOUR DAD WORKS AT A CONSTRUCTION SITE?”

> “YOU CAN PLAY WITH THE FORKLIFT FOREVER!”

The remark makes the father’s occupation into an insult.

Min-seo attacks him with a toy.

The sudden violence echoes the aggression he has watched at home.

# Strips 13–18 — Adults judge Min-seo and his mother

Min-seo’s mother is called to the daycare.

The other child’s parent reacts as though Min-seo is already destined for crime.

> “IS HE GOING TO BE A CRIMINAL WHEN HE GROWS UP?!”

She emphasizes that he struck another child with an object rather than his bare hand.

Min-seo’s mother bows and says it is her fault for not paying attention.

The other woman notices how young she looks and asks a cruel question:

> “DID YOU MAKE A MISTAKE AND GIVE BIRTH?”

A staff member tries to move the conversation away from personal attack.

She hesitantly explains that children exposed to domestic violence are more likely to show aggression toward peers.

> “SO I HOPE YOU DON’T BLAME MIN-SEO THAT MUCH…”

Min-seo’s mother insists:

> “HIS FATHER’S KIND NOW…”

The teacher thanks her and asks Min-seo:

> “ISN’T THAT RIGHT, MIN-SEO~ YOUR FATHER’S KIND THESE DAYS?”

# Strip 19 — The child sees a different danger

A dark close-up shows Min-seo’s mother smiling unnaturally.

A black balloon reads:

> “BECAUSE YOUR DAD IS NICE!”

The next panel isolates Min-seo’s terrified face.

> “YOU BECAME MEAN?”

The child’s reaction reveals that the household violence has not ended. Its direction has changed.

The father’s recent restraint has removed one source of violence, but Min-seo now fears his mother.

# Strips 20–21 — Na’s undercover leave ends

Na sits barefoot among moving boxes in the neighboring apartment, smoking while taking a phone call.

An angry superior shouts:

> “HEY, YOU BASTARD! WHEN ARE YOU COMING BACK?!”

Na says he still has a week remaining on his leave.

The caller reacts to the fact that he intended to use the full two-week vacation, then revokes the rest and orders him back the next morning.

Na complains:

> “HOW CAN THERE BE SUCH AN EVIL COMPANY?”

> “CAN’T A CIVIL SERVANT TAKE A PROPER VACATION?”

He lights another cigarette and decides there is no need to say goodbye to the family.

His thought continues:

> “IF HE KNOWS I’M GONE…”

> “DONG-CHEOL MIGHT BECOME DONG-CHEOL AGAIN…”

Na has been living next door and arranging the construction job as a surveillance intervention.

# Strips 22–24 — The hidden family emergency

A heavy crash sounds from the neighboring apartment.

Na’s expression changes instantly. He hears Dong-cheol scream:

> “YOU FUCKING BITCH!!”

Na rushes through the door.

Inside, Dong-cheol is attacking Min-seo’s mother. Min-seo lies motionless in the foreground, visibly injured or unconscious.

The shouting escalates into murder-suicide language:

> “YOU’LL DIE HERE TODAY!”

> “AND I’LL DIE TOO!”

> “FUCK! LET’S ALL TAKE MEDICINE RIGHT NOW!!”

> “AND JUST DIE!”

The chapter ends on Min-seo’s small body while the adults’ violence continues behind him.

Na’s fear that Dong-cheol would relapse appears correct at first glance, but the precise cause of the confrontation remains unresolved until the next chapter.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Temporary calm did not repair the family system

Employment and surveillance reduced Dong-cheol’s violence, but neither addressed the mother’s trauma, the couple’s mutual aggression, or Min-seo’s learned fear.

## 2. The daycare incident is a behavioral echo

Min-seo uses a toy as a weapon after being humiliated about his father. The chapter links this reaction to domestic violence without reducing the child to a future criminal.

## 3. The parent meeting reproduces social contempt

The other mother criticizes Min-seo’s violence while humiliating a young mother for teenage pregnancy. Adult cruelty is presented as respectable only because it is verbal.

## 4. Min-seo recognizes the cycle before the adults do

“Because your dad is nice, you became mean” captures the child’s experience: one parent’s restraint has left the other parent’s abuse exposed.
