# Opening — The agency goes undercover

Lim Han-rim’s red hair and public appearances have made her recognizable.

Na Hwa-jin explains that the island is a closed society whose police, officials, and local workers are connected to the village.

The agency therefore cannot arrive openly as public investigators.

Lim dyes or conceals her hair, wears glasses, and takes the alias **Na Han-rim**, a newly appointed physical-education teacher.

Na presents himself as her older brother, a tired city worker accompanying her on a rural break.

The cover story gives both investigators a reason to enter the village without announcing the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency.

---

# First contact with village authority

The village elders welcome the newcomers with false warmth.

They ask why two people arrived when only one teacher was expected.

Lim introduces herself and Na casually explains that he came along to rest in the clean air.

The village head separates them.

Lim is told to report to the school principal, while Na is taken on a tour and instructed in village rules.

The first rule is familiar: outsiders are expected to pay a village development fee.

Na is told that because he will stay for two years, he should become a resident and get along with everyone.

The amount demanded is approximately five million won.

Na refuses.

He says the village does not work through legitimate contracts and he will not hand money to someone he just met.

When an elder threatens him for speaking rudely to a man old enough to be his father, Na jokingly complains that stress is making his hair fall out.

He then stops joking and physically intimidates the man.

---

# Na’s gangster cover becomes useful

The villagers conclude that Lim’s “brother” is a gangster.

Na tells them that if he refuses to become a resident, he will take responsibility for himself.

The elders warn that they will not help him if trouble occurs.

Na’s refusal is intentional. He wants the village to treat him as a dangerous outsider so their hidden enforcement system reveals itself.

The village head tells his men not to react too quickly. Catching whoever photographed or reported the village is more important.

---

# Lim meets the principal

At school, Lim introduces herself as the new teacher.

The principal comments that young female teachers often cry when appointed to isolated places.

Lim says she has traveled widely since age twenty and is accustomed to rural assignments.

The principal praises the island’s scenery and generosity.

Lim immediately notices him smoking indoors and politely points out that it is prohibited.

He tells her not to worry about small rules.

Her only duty, he says, is to obey the village elders and never complain about what they do.

He mentions that another Seoul teacher failed to adapt and disappeared.

The warning confirms that the school administration is part of the village hierarchy.

---

# Na finds Kyu-jeong’s family

Na follows the address provided in Kyu-jeong’s report.

He finds villagers attacking or threatening the family.

They accuse Kyu-jeong of stealing a golden toad from the village head’s house and causing an elder to collapse.

The parents say they do not know where their son is. They have spent three nights unable to sleep from worry.

They explain that Kyu-jeong carried the demanded development fee to the village head and never returned.

The villagers deny receiving the money and prepare to assault the family.

Na intervenes.

A man asks what authority he has.

Na says he came to enjoy fresh air but has become angry.

He offers two options: accept five million won, or receive five million won’s worth of hospital treatment.

---

# Na interrogates the local enforcer

Na overpowers the man and demands information about Son Hye-seung.

He applies a painful joint lock and refuses to release him until he answers.

The villager calls him a gangster.

Na observes that stealing money and abusing power are not very different from the criminal behavior he is pretending to represent.

When asked who he really is, Na answers through the cover story:

> “MY SISTER’S BROTHER IS A GANGSTER.”

The ridiculous phrasing keeps the villagers focused on the false identity rather than the agency.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Undercover work is necessary because formal systems are compromised

The village’s public employees and school officials are socially tied to the same local hierarchy.

## 2. Na weaponizes the village’s expectations

They assume a rough-looking city man must be a gangster. That assumption gives him freedom to provoke and investigate without revealing government authority.

## 3. The principal teaches submission rather than education

His main instruction is not about students. It is that a teacher must obey elders and overlook violations.

## 4. Kyu-jeong’s disappearance is reframed as theft

The village constructs a criminal accusation against the missing victim so the family appears responsible for its own persecution.
