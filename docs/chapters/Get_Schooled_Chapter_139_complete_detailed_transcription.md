## Reader Images 004–006 — Daniel killed his father

Na asks directly:

> **YOU SHOT YOUR OWN FATHER…? NO… WAY… HE’S DEAD…**

Daniel answers:

> **NO. HE DIDN’T DIE.**

He explains that the shooting happened while defending his mother. In the state where he lived, the incident was not treated as a serious crime because the circumstances were considered self-defense.

> **HOWEVER, MY CIRCUMSTANCES WERE CONSIDERED, AND IT WAS RECOGNIZED AS SELF-DEFENSE…**

Afterward, Daniel believed his mother could finally escape his father.

> **AFTERWARD, MY MOTHER COULD LEAVE THAT DEMONIC FATHER, BUT…**

> **IF I HADN’T DONE IT, SOMEDAY MY MOTHER AND I MIGHT HAVE BEEN KILLED BY MY FATHER…**

Yet the rescue destroyed their relationship. Daniel remembers sitting at dinner with his mother in silence. She would not look at him and eventually stopped speaking to him.

> **FROM THAT TIME ON, MY MOTHER**

> **NEVER SPOKE TO ME AGAIN.**

Daniel does not regret stopping his father, but he wonders whether there was another way—one that could have saved his mother without making her fear or distance herself from him.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Daniel’s nonviolence is not based on never using force

He shot his abusive father to protect himself and his mother. His philosophy comes from living with the aftermath: the action may have been legally justified and necessary, but it did not repair the family or remove the trauma.

## The “other way” Daniel seeks is restorative

Daniel does not regret saving his mother. He wonders whether violence alone can ever produce safety without leaving fear, alienation, or new harm behind. The agency gives him cases where he can test that question.

## Administrative skill is part of field competence

Daniel’s ability to restore and organize the deleted reports proves that the agency needs more than fighters. His quiet preparation prevents public or bureaucratic failure without requiring force.

## Sera’s insecurity is social as well as professional

She fears losing the agency because her old community appears trapped in cycles of low expectations, bravado, compensation scams, and violence. Becoming a regular employee represents escape from that identity.

## The alumni gathering mirrors Dowon Gyeongdang’s hierarchy

The men boast about hurting others, exploiting accidents, and intimidating weaker people. Sera recognizes the same coercive culture she is supposed to oppose, but her immediate response is still physical aggression.

## The cliffhanger tests whether Sera can distinguish intervention from self-validation

The tattooed man is clearly violent, but Sera approaches while desperate to prove her worth. The next chapter must show whether she protects victims proportionately or turns the encounter into a performance of dominance.
