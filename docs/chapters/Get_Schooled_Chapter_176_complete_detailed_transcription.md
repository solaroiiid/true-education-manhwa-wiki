## Strip 001 — The records around Yeesung’s arrival are suspicious

The chapter opens with Na’s side gathering background on **Yeesung**.

A character explains that Yeesung entered the orphanage when he was around five years old.

He is said to be especially close to **Minho**, who is around the same age.

The scene then shifts into Yeesung’s own memory.

He is shown looking at basic orphanage records and asking questions about his identity.

One paper gives him an assigned birthday:

> **AUGUST 7, 2008**

Yeesung reacts with uncertainty.

> **WAS THAT MY BIRTHDAY...?**

An adult tells him that the orphanage does not actually know his real birthday, so the director simply chose one for him.

Another dated scene shows Yeesung asking a more direct question:

> **IS THERE NO RECORD AT ALL OF ME COMING HERE?**

The answer is evasive.

> **NONE. YOU’D BETTER NOT ASK FURTHER.**

He is then shown being given or confirming the name:

> **YEESUNG. HAN.**

The sequence establishes that the orphanage did not merely raise him. It effectively **rewrote his identity**.

> **YOU KNOW? IF I DO THIS, YOU’LL DIE.**

> **WOOF WOOF...**

## Strip 003 — Children are punished for failing to remain profitable

Another flashback reveals how the orphanage rationalized brutality.

> **BUT MY RIBS ARE BROKEN...**

The response is heartless.

> **TRY YOUR BEST.**

> **WE DIDN’T EVEN ASK YOU TO DIE.**

The child is told not to become a burden to the others and is reminded how bad things become when the authority figure is disappointed.

The chapter also includes a line emphasizing the children’s economic role:

> **[THEY] ARE THE SOURCE OF OUR INCOME, AFTER ALL.**

The children are not being treated as wards in need of care. They are being managed as **assets**.

Yeesung is singled out as unusually intelligent:

> **YOU’RE SMART. YOU’RE UNLIKE THE OTHERS.**

That supposed compliment becomes another warning.

Because he is smart, he also understands the trap:

> **YOU KNOW NO ONE WILL BELIEVE ANYTHING YOU SAY.**

He is then ordered to perform degrading labor, including cleaning a pigpen or being treated like livestock.

The orphanage’s entire operating logic is exposed here: children are valuable only so long as they remain obedient, pitiable, and marketable.

## Strip 005 — The director explains the business model openly

This strip lays out the structure of the orphanage’s exploitation with disturbing clarity.

The director orders a child to get to work because:

> **WE’VE GOT A CUSTOMER.**

He then explains why this orphanage is “special.”

> **THE CHILDREN OF THIS ORPHANAGE... ARE THE SOURCE OF OUR INCOME, AFTER ALL.**

He describes the children as perfect tools for manipulating donors:

- they can deceive sponsors,
- draw pity through their circumstances,
- and beg for money as “orphans.”

He also admits to operating:

> **UNDER-THE-TABLE ADOPTIONS.**

The children are effectively a dual revenue stream:

1. fundraising props for public sympathy, and  
2. commodities for illegal private arrangements.

Yeesung is identified as especially valuable.

> **YOU’RE ONE OF MY STARS.**

The director says he cannot let Yeesung be “ruined over one mistake,” which means that preserving Yeesung’s usefulness matters more to him than the child’s body or mind.

He adds that children cannot be allowed to become too smart, so they must be taught obedience.

After a beating or coercive episode, an adult comforts the crying child with a line often used by abusers:

> **HE DID IT BECAUSE HE LOVES YOU.**

Affection is weaponized to erase violence.

## Strip 006 — Yeesung realizes what makes Minho familiar

The chapter shifts back into Yeesung’s internal reconstruction of the past.

The earlier records about birthdays and missing intake documents resurface in his mind.

He links together two facts:

- the orphanage had no authentic record of his original identity, and
- Minho has always felt strangely familiar to him.

Yeesung asks about the birthday issue again:

> **HOW COME... I DIDN’T HAVE A BIRTHDAY BEFORE?**

The old answer echoes back:

> **WE DON’T KNOW YOUR BIRTHDAY, SO THE DIRECTOR PICKED ONE FOR YOU.**

That pushes him toward the central realization.

He looks at Minho and says, in effect:

> **MINHO.  
> I’M YOUR YOUNGER BROTHER.**

Even if the exact family order is emotionally confused in the moment, the essential revelation is clear: **the two boys are not unrelated orphans who happened to meet there. They are brothers**.

The orphanage had concealed that truth.

## Strip 008 — The director “rescued” Yeesung only after he was already broken

Yeesung keeps following the logic of his own history.

He reflects that, because he had no real birthday and no ordinary intake file, he was not simply admitted through normal channels.

Instead, he remembers that the orphanage director took him in **after he woke up in a damaged condition**.

The implication is that Yeesung did not cleanly enter the institution as a documented abandoned child.

He was found, or delivered, **after some prior incident**, and the director then rewrote his life from there.

The narration-like fragments amount to:

- he had no usable identity record,
- the orphanage fabricated one,
- and the director inserted himself as savior after Yeesung regained consciousness.

This leads Yeesung to the next step:

> **IN THAT CASE, LET’S FIND OUT.**

He no longer wants only emotional truth. He wants documentary proof.

## Strip 009 — The missing-children database confirms the lead

The chapter closes with Yeesung or the investigators using logic rather than searching blindly for a mother.

A voice says:

> **NOT BY LOOKING FOR OUR MOM.**

Instead, they decide to check the **missing children database**.

The reasoning is precise.

If the “found child” was estimated to be around five years old, then they should search the database for missing children from that age range.

A public-service announcement system echoes through the scene:

> **ATTENTION, THIS IS THE PUBLIC SERVICE ANNOUNCEMENT SYSTEM.  
> A CHILD HAS GONE MISSING.**

The search yields a match.

At the end, the missing child’s information includes the name:

> **HAN YEESUNG**

This is the confirmation the chapter has been building toward.

Yeesung was not an unidentified orphan with no past. He was a **missing child** whose identity was buried inside the orphanage system.

The chapter ends on that discovery, turning his personal suspicion into verifiable fact.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The orphanage deliberately erased identity

Yeesung’s assigned birthday, missing intake record, and vague origin story were tools of control, not administrative accidents.

## Abuse and profit are inseparable in this institution

The director views children as income-generating objects—through pity-based fundraising, illegal adoptions, and obedience enforced by fear.

## Yeesung’s intelligence makes him dangerous to the system

The adults value him because he can perform well, but they also fear him because he asks the right questions.

## The brotherhood revelation reframes the whole arc

Minho is not just Yeesung’s closest companion in the orphanage. He is family, and their separation appears to have been part of a larger concealment.

## The final database search converts memory into evidence

By the end of the chapter, Yeesung’s past is no longer only a trauma story. It becomes a documented missing-child case.
