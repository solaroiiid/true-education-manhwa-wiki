# Strips 007–10 — Sang-wook’s claim

The hearing begins between the claimant and respondent.

The chair asks Sang-wook to explain why he requested an administrative trial and says he may remain seated.

Sang-wook chooses to stand.

He explains that Hong Song-hak sent messages implying retaliation after the school-violence committee’s decision and later came directly to his home.

If the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency had not arrived, Sang-wook believes the abuse would still be continuing.

> “I CANNOT GO TO THE SAME SCHOOL AS HONG SONG-HAK.”

That is why he requested the administrative trial.

The chair thanks him and permits him to sit.

# Strips 011–13 — The committee defends five days of suspension

A man introduces himself as **Lee Jun-bin**, a member of the committee that decided Song-hak’s original sanction.

He argues that the decision was appropriate.

The purpose of school-violence committees and other juvenile systems, he says, is correction rather than punishment.

Even in a serious case, evidence of reflection may justify consideration.

Most accused students deny wrongdoing. Song-hak admitted everything.

A flashback shows Song-hak saying:

> “YES, YOU’RE RIGHT. I DID IT ALL.”

He also promised:

> “I WON’T DO IT AGAIN~”

The committee interpreted these words as remorse.

Lee Jun-bin says a reflective student could not reasonably receive more than suspension. Keeping the students in the same class was intended to promote reconciliation rather than permanent separation.

He even suggests the five-day suspension may have been too severe.

---

# Strips 013–15 — Lim says Song-hak never reflected

Lim interrupts.

> “HONG SONG-HAK DIDN’T REFLECT ON HIMSELF!”

She identifies herself as the agency supervisor who personally handled his special education.

Although told not to speak out of turn, she insists that she observed Song-hak’s conduct throughout the period and never saw genuine reflection.

He mocked the lightness of the punishment and retaliated immediately.

Lim argues that the committee bears responsibility because it treated the performance of remorse as proof of remorse.

# Strips 015–18 — The youth lawyer attacks the agency

The youth human-rights attorney asks whether the committee’s weak decision is really responsible for Lim’s excessive special education.

He says he speaks with many children and knows the agency’s reputation among them:

> “THE GRIM REAPER.”

He accuses the agency of destroying young lives with criminal consequences extending beyond school discipline.

He watched the training video posted online and saw Lim use violence from the beginning.

The lawyer asks how Song-hak would feel when an authority with such overwhelming power appeared before him.

Would he not conclude that his life was over?

The attorney argues that minors are called juveniles because they are mentally immature. They make mistakes and require opportunities to return.

Not giving them that opportunity, he says, may cause more crimes later.

He claims to understand the boy’s feelings, then asks why Sang-wook concluded that Song-hak would retaliate.

Did he never expect the school to protect him?

The lawyer suggests that the administrative trial may be personal revenge:

The questioning places the victim’s fear on trial rather than the repeated retaliation.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The hearing treats spoken remorse as measurable evidence

Song-hak learned which phrases reduce punishment. The committee never tested whether his conduct changed afterward.

## 2. Correction and victim safety are framed as opposing goals

The lawyer protects the idea of another chance but repeatedly asks the victim to carry the risk of that chance.

## 3. Sang-wook is forced to justify fear after retaliation already occurred

The question “why did you expect retaliation?” ignores that Song-hak sent threats and came to his home.

## 4. Lim’s methods create a legitimate legal and ethical dispute

The chapter does not make the lawyer’s broader concern meaningless. It shows how that concern can become cruel when it erases the actual victim standing in the room.
