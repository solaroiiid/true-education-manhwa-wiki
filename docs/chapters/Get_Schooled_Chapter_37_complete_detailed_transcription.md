# Reader page 2 — Lim’s size becomes the first challenge

Kang Soo-yeon stands over Lim and asks:

> “WHAT DID YOU SAY JUST NOW…?”

> “YOU SAID I’M SMALL?”

Lim grips a mop or pole and looks up without intimidation.

Soo-yeon asks whether that is how Lim treats her coach—or even how she speaks to a person’s face.

The student gives a superficial apology.

> “AMONG THE BASKETBALL LEADERS, I’VE NEVER SEEN SOMEONE THIS SMALL BEFORE. I MADE A SLIP OF THE TONGUE.”

She immediately adds:

> “BUT CAN YOU HANDLE US WITH THAT PHYSIQUE OF YOURS?”

The team’s respect is explicitly tied to size and physical hierarchy.

# Reader pages 3–4 — Lim gives one warning

Lim acknowledges that the players may be suspicious of a small female coach.

She says she understands the instinct to test a leader.

Then she clarifies:

> “THERE WON’T BE A SECOND TIME.”

The page is dominated by a sudden physical exchange. Lim closes the distance, seizes the student, and puts her on the floor with a joint lock or takedown.

The student’s expression changes from mockery to shock.

Lim has answered the physique challenge without giving a speech about her background.

# Reader page 5 — Eun-ha is kept away from basketball

Practice begins.

Most of the team shoots and runs drills.

Lim notices Eun-ha alone at the side, repeatedly practicing footwork without a ball.

> “WHY ARE YOU THERE PRACTICING YOUR STEPS WHEN THE REST ARE PRACTICING SHOOTING?”

Lim orders her back onto the court.

# Reader page 6 — The captain invokes the old coach’s authority

Soo-yeon tells Lim to leave Eun-ha alone.

> “THIS WAS THE COACH’S SPECIAL ORDER.”

She claims Eun-ha lacks stamina and was assigned isolated step practice for a full week.

Lim points out the contradiction.

Eun-ha can perform footwork for more than an hour without a break, while the players shooting a few times are already drinking water in the corner.

> “I THINK YOU GUYS NEED MORE PHYSICAL TRAINING?”

Lim tells the others to get onto the court quickly.

# Reader pages 7–8 — What a substitute coach can supervise

Soo-yeon says the training system was created by the coach and herself.

She accuses Lim of interfering and ruining the team atmosphere.

Lim asks whether Soo-yeon knows what “substitute coach” means.

A substitute exercises the coach’s supervisory power.

> “IT MEANS, I’M JUST THE SAME AS THE COACH.”

Changing training and stopping harmful routines are therefore within Lim’s authority.

She tells Eun-ha to come over and says she will pass her the ball.

# Reader pages 9–10 — Eun-ha’s ability becomes visible

Lim feeds Eun-ha the ball.

Eun-ha moves, receives, and shoots.

The panels emphasize controlled footwork and a clean release.

Lim says:

> “YOU’RE GOOD.”

The captain’s expression tightens.

The team had spent months constructing Eun-ha as a useless player, but one ordinary basketball possession disproves the narrative.

Lim asks what is wrong and whether the captain is surprised.

# Reader page 11 — Ja-yeon attacks Eun-ha

Ja-yeon rushes toward Eun-ha in fury.

> “ARE YOU CRAZY? YOU FUCKING BITCH?”

She says her sister clearly ordered Eun-ha not to touch the ball.

She accuses Eun-ha of whining and breaking the team’s rule.

The confrontation shows that exclusion itself has become a privilege the sisters believe they own.

# Reader pages 12–13 — Soo-yeon offers a bargain

Soo-yeon approaches Eun-ha with false softness.

> “EUNHA, IT’S HARD RIGHT?”

She says that as captain she had many worries when Eun-ha tried to speak to reporters.

Then she offers a deal:

> “PROMISE ME YOU WON’T TELL ANYONE WHAT HAPPENED…”

> “AND NO ONE WILL BOTHER YOU IN CLASSROOM.”

The bargain reveals that the captain fully understands the abuse and can stop it whenever she chooses.

Eun-ha’s internal narration says that moments earlier, the issue was no longer basketball.

> “IT WAS MY LIFE THAT WAS ABOUT TO END.”

The offer therefore asks her to trade truth for temporary survival.

# Reader pages 14–15 — Lim catches the team in the act

The team surrounds Eun-ha in a closed or isolated area.

Soo-yeon orders someone to close the door.

The players move as a coordinated group rather than independent bullies.

A startled voice cries:

> “CAP… CAPTAIN!”

Lim appears and demands:

> “WHAT ARE YOU DOING?”

The door that was supposed to hide the punishment instead frames the substitute coach witnessing it.

# Reader page 16 — “We were just joking”

Lim orders:

> “EVERYBODY, SIT DOWN!”

The team scrambles to obey.

Soo-yeon tries to minimize the scene:

> “DON’T BE SO SERIOUS. WE WERE JUST JOKING.”

The explanation repeats the logic used in other arcs: violence becomes harmless “play” only after an adult sees it.

# Reader page 17 — The captain claims disciplinary authority

Soo-yeon says she only wanted to scare Eun-ha because the girl would not speak.

She describes controlling members as her authority as captain.

> “IT’S MY AUTHORITY AS CAPTAIN TO ESTABLISH DISCIPLINE AMONG THE MEMBERS…”

She asks the acting coach to tell them what is right.

Lim responds physically.

A sharp impact folds Soo-yeon or drives her backward. The captain cannot finish converting abuse into legitimate team discipline.

Lim addresses her as a thin or arrogant girl and reminds her that she had already been warned.

# Reader page 18 — No restraint the second time

Lim stands over the shocked players.

> “I CLEARLY TOLD YOU, RIGHT?”

> “I WON’T HOLD BACK THE SECOND TIME.”

The chapter ends with Lim’s authority established and the team’s private punishment system exposed.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The team mistakes height for authority

Soo-yeon’s first challenge focuses on Lim’s body rather than qualifications. Lim immediately demonstrates that physical appearance does not determine control.

## 2. The old coach’s “special order” institutionalized exclusion

Eun-ha was not merely ignored by teammates. Her separation was incorporated into official training language.

## 3. One pass disproves months of propaganda

The sisters’ system depends on no one seeing Eun-ha play. Lim’s simplest intervention is to give her the ball.

## 4. Soo-yeon knows the abuse is optional

Her bargain proves she can stop the classroom harassment. She preserves it because silence benefits her.

## 5. “Captain’s discipline” is the core ideological defense

Soo-yeon does not deny using fear. She claims fear is part of leadership. Lim’s intervention rejects the premise before the team can normalize it again.
