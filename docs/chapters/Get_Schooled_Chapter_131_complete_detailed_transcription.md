## Images 001–006 — The drug case becomes national news

> **AHEAD OF THE NATIONAL COLLEGE ENTRANCE EXAM, THE POLICE HAVE LAUNCHED A MAJOR CRACKDOWN ON ADHD MEDICATION,**

> **WHICH HAS BEEN MISREPRESENTED AS A DRUG THAT IMPROVES ACADEMIC PERFORMANCE.**

The report connects the investigation directly to Hyunmin’s case:

> **THIS CASE CAME TO LIGHT AFTER A STUDENT, IDENTIFIED ONLY AS B, SUDDENLY COLLAPSED DURING**

> **A NATIONAL MOCK EXAM SUPERVISED BY THE NATIONAL EDUCATION RIGHTS PROTECTION AGENCY AND WAS RUSHED TO THE HOSPITAL, REVEALING THE USE OF AN ILLEGAL DRUG NOT APPROVED FOR SALE IN THE COUNTRY.**

> **NO, OUR ROLE ENDS HERE. LET’S LEAVE IT TO THE POLICE NOW…**

Hanrim worries that more students may be endangered before the exam, but Na explains the agency’s limits:

> **AS I’VE SAID BEFORE, WE DON’T HAVE ENOUGH PERSONNEL TO SOLVE THE PROBLEM DIRECTLY. OUR FOCUS IS MORE ON RAISING PUBLIC AWARENESS.**

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The drug arc produces policy but not institutional omnipotence

The agency’s public intervention prompts police action and legislative attention, yet Na immediately emphasizes that the TRPA cannot investigate every seller. Their role is to expose educational harms and force larger institutions to respond.

## The second-generation team has contrasting temperaments

Gu Sera is reactive, competitive, and quick to turn frustration into physical aggression. Daniel is physically capable but restrained, stopping her punch without retaliation. Their equal recruit status foreshadows tension over method and authority.

## Daniel’s nonviolence is experience-based

His refusal to rely on violence is not presented as naïveté. He grew up around severe urban violence and believes punishment can suppress behavior without reaching its cause. Dowon Gyeongdang becomes the first real test of whether that belief can survive organized abuse.

## The school uses therapeutic language to conceal domination

The teacher’s lecture says evil behavior is merely an external layer hiding a true self. Yet he actively supervises cruelty. The spiritual vocabulary appears to absolve abusers and reclassify torture as guidance, purification, or communal correction.

## Suchan calls his attackers “brothers and sister”

The wording suggests the school has imposed a pseudo-family structure or communal terminology. His plea shows that these labels do not represent safety; they make abuse harder to identify and escape.

## The cliffhanger identifies adult complicity

The children are not secretly breaking the school’s ideals. The teacher enters, sees the naked boy being tortured, and praises the perpetrators. The abuse is therefore institutional policy, not isolated bullying.
