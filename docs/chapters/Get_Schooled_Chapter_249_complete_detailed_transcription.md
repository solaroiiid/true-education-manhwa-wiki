### Content note

The chapter contains child neglect, domestic violence, starvation, theft for food, assault, parental exploitation, and discussion of criminal responsibility for guardians.

## Strip 001 — Lee Jun-bin wants a larger debate

Lee Jun-bin says lowering the age threshold alone is statistically less important than the public believes.

Only a small number of children newly placed above the threshold would actually receive ordinary criminal punishment.

Many would still be handled through protective juvenile measures.

The effect may therefore be limited.

Jun-bin says politicians and media speak as though changing one number will solve juvenile crime.

He wants the televised debate to expose a more fundamental cause.

In exchange, he is willing to support the threshold proposal if Na agrees to discuss the wider issue publicly.

The chapter introduces the child whose life will become their central example.

---

## Strip 002 — Lee Yunseong is arrested again

Police call the father of sixth-grader **Lee Yunseong**.

The boy has been arrested again for theft.

His father responds with irritation rather than concern.

He tells the police to do whatever they want and hangs up.

Yunseong is shown bruised and emotionally hardened.

His family history explains part of the transformation.

His father is an unemployed alcoholic.

His mother was also violent during periods of stress and eventually left the household.

The child remained with a parent who neither fed nor protected him consistently.

---

## Strip 003 — Hunger becomes the first crime

After the mother leaves, food becomes Yunseong’s greatest problem.

His father does not cook and provides no allowance.

During a school vacation, Yunseong nearly starves.

He asks for approximately ₩1,000 to buy ramen.

His father beats him and tells him to beg or steal if he is hungry.

Yunseong goes to a convenience store.

He throws a rock through the glass, enters, and eats.

He understands that stealing is wrong.

He also understands that no food will appear if he obeys the law.

> **OTHERWISE, I WOULD HAVE STARVED.**

The chapter surrounds him with dark accusatory voices that condemn the crime while ignoring the adult who created the emergency.

---

## Strip 004 — “Juvenile offenders are kings”

Yunseong eventually enters **Adam’s House**, the rehabilitation residence connected to Lee Jun-bin.

Jun-bin asks whether the child has ever truly lived inside a home of his own.

The question refers to more than a building.

Yunseong has not experienced regular meals, supervision, affection, or consequences delivered by a reliable adult.

He has already absorbed a destructive lesson from other children and police encounters:

> **JUVENILE OFFENDERS ARE KINGS.**

The phrase becomes both shield and identity.

Because ordinary criminal punishment cannot reach him yet, he treats repeated arrest as proof that nothing can.

---

## Strip 005 — The cycle survives rehabilitation

Yunseong leaves Adam’s House without a stable family waiting for him.

He smokes, fights, steals, extorts money, and cycles through police stations and juvenile facilities.

He lashes out at an older man and treats arrest as routine.

Jun-bin explains that Yunseong is not an isolated curiosity.

The public sees the visible crimes.

It does not see the household that trained the child to survive through crime and then benefited from the proceeds.

Na says the debate needs more than general language about social support.

He wants to show a definite cause and a concrete form of responsibility.

---

## Strips 006–007 — The father profits from his son’s protection

At home, Yunseong’s father searches his bag.

He takes money the child obtained outside.

A visible amount of approximately **₩100,000** is removed.

When Yunseong resists, the father beats him.

The man says the child can simply go out and make more.

Because Yunseong cannot be sent to ordinary prison, the father treats him as a low-risk source of criminal income.

The adult who should prevent offending is actively reinforcing it.

Na watches the household from outside and reaches the conclusion the case materials could not prove alone:

> **THE PARENT CAUSED THE PROBLEM.**

---

## Strip 008 — Civil liability is not enough

Na and Jun-bin discuss the ordinary legal response.

A victim may seek compensation from a guardian.

That option means little when the parent:

- has no assets;
- receives welfare;
- refuses to cooperate;
- or treats an unenforceable civil judgment as meaningless.

The father can remain legally distant from the child’s criminal acts while consuming the money those acts produce.

Na says the law has created responsibility without a consequence strong enough to change behavior.

---

## Strip 009 — The Parent Responsibility Act

Na proposes a new direction.

Parents who substantially cause, encourage, or knowingly fail to restrain a minor’s serious offending should face more than civil damages.

They should bear criminal or administrative responsibility of their own.

The chapter refers to parental-responsibility systems used in countries including the United States, United Kingdom, and France.

The proposed Korean framework is described as:

> **THE PARENT RESPONSIBILITY ACT**

The concept becomes the bridge between the juvenile-age debate and the family environment that produces many repeat offenders.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Yunseong’s first theft is tied directly to survival

The chapter does not erase his agency, but it refuses to discuss the broken window without discussing starvation.

## Rehabilitation cannot replace a home that reproduces crime

A short residential program loses much of its effect when the child returns to the same neglectful adult.

## The father is not merely absent

He confiscates criminal proceeds and encourages the child to obtain more.

## Civil responsibility may be practically empty

A judgment against an insolvent or uncooperative guardian does not protect the next victim.

## The proposed law targets adult behavior

The chapter’s answer is not to imprison a parent automatically for every mistake a child makes. It focuses on guardians whose neglect, exploitation, or refusal to intervene materially sustains serious offending.
