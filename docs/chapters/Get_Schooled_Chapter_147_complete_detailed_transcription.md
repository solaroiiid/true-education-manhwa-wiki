# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The minor outside is not an innocent failed recruit

The purple-jacketed inspector reads his size, age, and lack of tattoos as evidence that the gang rejected him. The later G.Y. tattoo and the massacre inside reveal that he deliberately played harmless and had already seized control of the meeting.

## G.Y. is a rival youth crime organization

Its senior figures are only seventeen and eighteen. They are capable of overwhelming established adult gangs and intend to inherit criminal businesses rather than dismantle them.

## The national organization’s collapse creates a power vacuum

The agency and police pressure do not automatically end organized crime. G.Y. arrives before formal authorities and converts the national gang’s defeat into an expansion opportunity.

## Na and the teenage leader narrowly miss a direct confrontation

The G.Y. leader leaves before Na fully identifies him. His message—“tell them we did it”—is an intentional challenge to the Education Rights Protection Agency.

## The ending demonstrates Na’s physical scale again

An accelerating vehicle is treated as a lethal weapon. Na does not evade it; he meets it head-on and breaks through the windshield to disable the driver.
