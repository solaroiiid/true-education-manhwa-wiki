## Reader Images 001–003 — The history between Sera and Seo Hyuntae

The chapter opens on the bloody parking-garage strike from the previous cliffhanger. Seo Hyuntae’s head snaps sideways as Sera’s blow lands.

A flashback identifies him as:

> **SEO HYUNTAE**

> **HIGH SCHOOL YEAR 2**

In the school memory, Hyuntae has also been struck and is crouched or fallen near other students. Young Sera stands above him and warns:

> **LISTEN TO ME CAREFULLY, SEO HYUNTAE…**

She makes clear that their shared school year does not make them friends.

> **I DON’T THINK WE’RE CLOSE ENOUGH TO BE GREETING EACH OTHER SO CASUALLY…**

> **WHY ARE YOU HERE? HUH?**

Sera tells him the principle he would later mock:

> **PEOPLE ARE MORE IMPORTANT THAN MONEY, RIGHT?**

Hyuntae internally regards her as insane and dangerous. Sera orders another student to hand over a bag taken from the person Hyuntae attacked. Inside are large bundles of cash.

> **HEY, GIVE ME THAT FUCKER’S BAG.**

> **I KNEW IT…**

The flashback implies that Sera exposed or interrupted an early money-making scheme and left Hyuntae with the humiliation he still remembers.

## Reader Images 007–009 — Hyuntae rejects the application

Sera reminds him of her fighting ability:

> **YOU’LL HAVE A HARD TIME FINDING SOMEBODY AS TALENTED AS ME.**

Hyuntae considers her with visible distaste and refuses.

> **I DON’T NEED IT.**

Sera asks:

While he checks his phone, Sera watches the surrounding gangsters. Their visible tattoos, weapons, and brazen behavior seem almost cartoonishly reckless to her. She privately wonders whether one tattoo artist infected them all with a “retard virus,” noting that they openly carry weapons where anyone can see them.

Messages on Hyuntae’s phone reveal a dispute with another gang leader. A photograph shows younger members kneeling while a man boasts that he is “educating them on manners.” The exchange reads approximately:

> **THESE LITTLE SHITS SHOULD GREET ME IF THEY SEE A SENIOR, BUT THEY DARE IGNORE ME?**

The rival leader appears in person. A label identifies him as:

> **YANG BOKNAM**

Boknam greets Hyuntae with mock confidence, implying he expected Hyuntae to cower. The men press foreheads and trade threats.

Boknam argues that Hyuntae should control his juniors and let the matter go. Hyuntae refuses to accept Boknam entering his territory, attacking his people, and demanding obedience.

Boknam demands an enormous settlement:

A large group of tattooed men approaches carrying steel pipes, bats, and other weapons.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Sera’s poverty story is an infiltration performance built from real frustration

Her claim that she was rejected by a hundred employers and spent years on an unsuccessful production may contain genuine resentment. She weaponizes that vulnerability to make her sudden conversion to Hyuntae’s money-first philosophy believable.

## Hyuntae and Sera’s conflict began before adulthood

Sera previously caught him with money taken through violence or intimidation and told him human beings mattered more. Hyuntae built his adult criminal identity around rejecting that lesson.

## The gang recruits through visible strength rather than secrecy

Hyuntae initially refuses Sera because of personal disgust and gendered contempt. Her ability to destroy Boknam’s fighters changes the calculation immediately. Violence functions as the organization’s résumé.

## Modern gang culture is portrayed as both dangerous and performative

The men boast online, carry weapons openly, and fight in large, chaotic mobs. Sera sees their behavior as less disciplined than older criminal organizations, which makes them easier to provoke but no less harmful.

## Sera’s undercover identity has now been accepted

By defeating the rival gang and receiving Hyuntae’s explicit “you pass,” she has gained the access the agency needed. The next stage is discovering how the organization recruits minors and what crimes it assigns them.
