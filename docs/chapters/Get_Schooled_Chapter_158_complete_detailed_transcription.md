### Content note

The chapter contains class discrimination, parental intimidation, housing resentment, and forced school transfer.

## Images 001–003 — Daniel remains polite after being struck

Jaeguk’s mother continues shouting after slapping Daniel.

She calls him only a temporary teacher and says he had no authority to change an arrangement made between a parent and the permanent homeroom teacher.

Daniel does not strike back.

He says that because he is new, there may have been a misunderstanding and asks her to discuss the matter properly after school.

Internally, he recognizes that Jaeguk’s behavior came directly from this adult.

## Images 004–007 — The mothers’ café meeting

Daniel meets Jaeguk’s mother and other apartment mothers, including the mothers of Tae-byung and Eun-kyung, at the Sunshine Land residents’ café.

The women first question Daniel himself:

- Is he really American?
- Did he live in New York?
- Can he teach their children English after school?
- Why is a foreigner serving as homeroom teacher?

The conversation then returns to seating.

Daniel explains that he paired students because Woo-chan and Jaeguk were the two children without suitable desk partners.

The mothers say Woo-chan is not an ordinary classmate. She is the rental-unit problem.

---

## Images 008–011 — Poverty is described as contamination

The mothers claim:

- poor families raise children badly,
- crime occurs more often among lower-income households,
- Woo-chan’s parents work and leave her with grandparents,
- mixed public housing creates “trouble children,”
- and rental residents use facilities paid for by private owners.

They resent rental children using the playground and attending the same reorganized school district.

Their language treats statistical stereotypes as permission to punish a specific child.

Daniel listens without immediately correcting them.

---

## Images 012–015 — The history of Sunshine Land’s division

The chapter explains that the complex combined privately purchased buildings with rental units as part of a policy response to population decline and under-enrollment.

The mothers fought intensely against school reorganization and integration.

Jaeguk’s mother was especially active.

Building 103 contains the rental units. During repainting, she learned exactly which building was public rental and pressured the representatives and management to leave it a different color.

The pink exterior turned a contractual category into a public badge.

She argues that prospective buyers canceled purchases after discovering the rental building and that property values suffered.

In her mind, Woo-chan’s presence is connected to the debt and insecurity surrounding her own apartment.

---

## Images 016–018 — Daniel appears to agree

After hearing the mothers, Daniel says he understands.

He promises to take care of the situation immediately.

The women assume he will restore the discriminatory seating arrangement and keep Woo-chan away from Jaeguk.

The next day, Daniel changes the seating plan.

Jaeguk confidently expects to be moved beside a preferred classmate.

Instead, Daniel hands him transfer documents.

---

## Images 019–020 — Daniel applies the mother’s logic to her son

Jaeguk’s mother storms into school again.

Daniel says that because she considers contact with Woo-chan intolerable, he has arranged for Jaeguk to attend another school where the issue will not arise.

She protests that she only asked for separation inside the class, not removal of her son.

Daniel’s response exposes the asymmetry: she is willing to reorganize another child’s life but considers the same inconvenience unacceptable when applied to her own family.

---

## Images 021–022 — Sera enforces the exit

Daniel refers to an American **Dean System**, in which disruptive students or parents can be physically removed from school spaces.

A woman appears to escort Jaeguk’s mother out.

Gu Sera stands nearby, smiling with dangerous cheerfulness.

She addresses the woman as “auntie” and tells her to stop squawking before she causes more trouble.

Jaeguk’s mother is forced out while Daniel remains in the classroom.

The chapter ends with the transfer process underway and the adult power structure finally being used against the person who demanded exclusion.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The mother’s property anxiety is real but misdirected

She carries debt and fears falling apartment prices. The chapter does not deny those pressures. It shows her converting them into cruelty toward a child with no power over the housing market.

## Color-coding the rental building institutionalizes stigma

The unequal repainting was not an accident. It was intentionally preserved so owner-residents could identify who did not belong to their class.

## Daniel’s apparent agreement is strategic

He accepts the mother’s premise—that incompatible children must be separated—and applies the burden to her own son.

## The transfer is not the final educational goal

Moving Jaeguk may protect Woo-chan temporarily, but the deeper question remains whether Jaeguk and his mother can recognize the prejudice they carry into another school.
