# Opening — The juvenile-law rule

The chapter begins with a legal explanation based on Article 4 of the Juvenile Act.

Children aged ten or older but under fourteen who violate the Penal Code are handled as juvenile-protection cases rather than ordinary criminal defendants.

The delinquent group interprets the rule in the most self-serving possible way:

> “PEOPLE WHO ARE UNDER THE AGE OF 14…”

> “WILL NOT BE CRIMINALLY PUNISHED NO MATTER WHAT THEY DO.”

> “THERE WILL BE NO CRIMINAL RECORD.”

> “EVEN IF YOU COMMIT MURDER.”

The scanlation includes a note that the law quoted is Korean law.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The legal threshold becomes a countdown

The boy’s birthday is not a celebration. It is a date around which he has planned risk.

## 2. The children misunderstand why juvenile law exists

The law recognizes incomplete development and prioritizes protection. The gang converts that protection into permission.

## 3. Police custody alone would confirm their worldview

Because they expect immediate release, an ordinary arrest would become proof that they were right. Na therefore creates a different intervention.

## 4. The classmates already know the pattern

No one is surprised that the group skipped school or committed serious misconduct. Institutional habituation has protected them almost as much as age.
