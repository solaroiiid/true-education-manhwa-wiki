# Get Schooled — Chapter 119
## Complete Detailed Panel-by-Panel Transcription

### Scope

This transcription follows all **30 ordered reader images** in Chapter 119. It covers the opening of Gu Sera’s first field case, her relationship with her supervisor, and the discovery of a hidden school tyrant.

---

## Sequence 1 — A student beaten with knuckles

The chapter opens in a hospital emergency room. Staff wheel in an unconscious female student. Paramedics say she was found collapsed in an alley and appears to have been assaulted.

Doctors order a CT scan. A close inspection of the patterned wounds reveals the likely weapon: metal knuckles. One doctor curses the unknown attacker and asks that the Teacher’s Rights Protection Agency be contacted in addition to the police.

The medical setting establishes that the assault is not ordinary roughhousing. The student has been struck repeatedly in the face with a self-defense weapon and cannot initially explain what happened.

---

## Sequence 2 — Sera’s first business trip

Gu Sera rides in a car with her female senior/supervisor. Sera apologizes theatrically that her respected senior must drive because she has not obtained a license. The senior says she expects little from a twenty-year-old but asks whether Sera read the case summary sent by email.

Sera admits she barely looked at it and blames her poor memory and lack of academic ability. The supervisor becomes visibly irritated.

A flashback reveals Hwa-jin’s assignment. He will handle Hyun Daniel “man to man,” while the woman must educate Gu Sera. She complains that Sera is too problematic and says she gave her a zero during the interview. Hwa-jin replies that every person is problematic and tells her to turn Sera into a person from now on.

When asked why he chose her, Hwa-jin gives a comically shallow first answer: she is young—the youngest applicant. He says young rookies are the best, then hints that there is a second, more important reason.

---

## Sequence 3 — Pattern of weapon assaults

The supervisor finally explains the case. Three days earlier, the current female victim was beaten with knuckles. During the first three months of the semester, four students from the same school were hospitalized after attacks using different self-defense weapons.

Every victim claims not to remember the attacker.

Sera suggests hypnosis. Her senior orders her to listen without interrupting. Whether the pattern is indiscriminate assault or school violence, they must investigate.

The pair visit the hospitalized girl. They explain that recalling the attack may be painful but is necessary. She says she was hit from behind and remembers nothing until waking in the hospital. When pressed for clothing, shoes, or any identifying detail, she panics and insists she knows nothing.

---

## Sequence 4 — Sera notices the lie

Outside the room, the supervisor says the patient remains in shock and should be left alone before they visit the next victim. Sera says the result will be the same because the girl will deny knowledge even when she remembers.

Her basis is physical rather than psychological: the girl said she was struck from behind, but the injuries are concentrated on her face. She is lying.

Sera concludes that the victims are frightened of the culprit. From a student’s perspective, the agency is far away while the tyrant remains close every day.

This observation surprises her supervisor. The agency’s solved cases usually begin with victims reporting voluntarily or agents personally witnessing abuse. Hidden cases, in which everyone is too afraid to speak, may escape their entire model.

---

## Sequence 5 — The school tyrant

The chapter reveals the attacker: **Lee Chiho**, a heavily built male student with a shaved or closely cropped head, earrings, and a domineering expression. He tests a new weapon on another student and comments that it performs well.

Chiho rules through “perfect fear.” He beats victims publicly enough for the school to understand but makes the consequence of reporting appear worse than silence. No one dares step forward.

Sera explains the problem in simple terms. In ten cases of school violence, there may be only one where an outsider can definitively determine whether an assault occurred. This is why school violence persists: adults expect testimony while students calculate who will still be beside them tomorrow.

Her supervisor realizes Sera has perfectly identified the agency’s limit.

---

## Sequence 6 — Why Hwa-jin wanted a young recruit

The supervisor remembers Hwa-jin praising young rookies. Sera only recently graduated from high school, so she can enter the students’ social environment without carrying the obvious distance of an adult investigator.

She is asked whether she still has her old uniform.

The chapter cuts to a classroom. A teacher announces a new transfer student:

> **HER NAME IS GU SERA. EVERYONE, GET ALONG WITH HER WELL.**

Sera stands before the class in uniform, smiling with unnerving confidence. She is now positioned inside the system of fear rather than questioning it from a hospital corridor.

---

## End-of-chapter continuity note

Chapter 119 introduces a case the agency cannot solve through ordinary reports. Sera’s value is not formal education or legal analysis; it is her recent experience as a student from a violent school and her instinctive understanding that victims may lie to survive. Her undercover transfer places her directly within Lee Chiho’s territory.

## Additional visual and text details

- The emergency-room staff identify the patterned injury as a knuckle wound before the victim can speak, establishing a forensic link among the separate assaults.
- Sera’s car conversation is full of salutes, exaggerated apologies, and sudden boasts about beating someone into powder. Her supervisor repeatedly has to redirect her toward the written case.
- Hwa-jin’s “young rookie” explanation is initially played as a joke, but the hospital interview proves the strategic value: Sera notices what an adult investigator accepts at face value.
- The victim’s statement—hit from behind—contradicts the frontal facial wounds. Her refusal is depicted as fear, not unreliability.
- Chiho’s introduction shows him testing self-defense weapons on students while treating injury as product evaluation.
- The transfer-student ending places Sera behind a school desk among teenagers. Her agency authority is invisible; she must rely on student behavior, language, and reputation.
