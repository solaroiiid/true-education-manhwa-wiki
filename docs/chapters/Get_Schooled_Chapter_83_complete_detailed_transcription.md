# Opening — Lim becomes an elementary homeroom teacher

She introduces herself under the name rendered in the scanlation as **Na Han-rim** and explains that **Mrs. Choi** suddenly became ill and will rest for a while.

# Lim struggles with the lessons

Lim teaches mathematics and makes a calculation error, writing **68** where a student says the answer should be **58**.

Children whisper that she also made a mistake during social studies.

She becomes visibly nervous, repeatedly telling herself to relax.

The class does not become cruel. Several students correct her, laugh lightly, and continue participating.

Lim notices that most of them are more worried about Mrs. Choi than interested in attacking the replacement teacher.

The discrepancy deepens her suspicion that the original complaint was fabricated by adults.

# The parents’ café meeting

Lim meets a group of mothers at a café.

**Lee Woo-jin’s mother** immediately takes control of the conversation. She questions Lim’s age, university, and background.

Lim says she attended the **Non-Commissioned Officer Academy**, abbreviated NCOA. The parents misunderstand it as a prestigious foreign university and praise her English.

They then ask about Mrs. Choi.

Woo-jin’s mother says Choi’s panic disorder is an act and describes her as lazy, biased, and responsible for an unfair school-violence ruling.

She claims Choi sided with another child because of money.

Lim calmly corrects the claim. Under the revised system, the Ministry or Office of Education—not the homeroom teacher—conducts the school-violence deliberation. A teacher may submit an opinion, but it is not the decisive factor.

Woo-jin’s mother interprets the correction as an attack.

> “SO YOU’RE SAYING MY KID IS AT FAULT?”

Lim explains that she is only describing the process.

The mother accuses her of being young and unable to “read the room.”

# The juice-refill incident

Woo-jin’s mother demands a free refill for an expensive juice.

The café employee apologizes and explains that only Americano and espresso drinks qualify.

The mother threatens to post that the café is cold-hearted and warns that a business in the neighborhood must deal properly with mothers.

The employee offers a cookie as compensation.

Lim interrupts and orders the same drinks for the group.

She says her salary is not especially generous, but she does not want to be stingy over a few thousand won.

The gesture exposes how the mother uses the threat of online criticism to obtain personal concessions.

The mother asks what Lim is implying and becomes increasingly aggressive.

# “Teachers and parents have no upper-lower relationship”

The argument escalates.

Woo-jin’s mother complains that young people are rude and appears to move or throw ice toward Lim without permission.

Lim calls her **“ajumma,”** an ordinary term for a middle-aged woman that the mother treats as an unforgivable insult.

The mother calls Lim a crazy bitch.

Lim physically stops or controls her before the confrontation can continue.

She tells the woman to listen carefully:

> “TEACHERS AND PARENTS HAVE NO UPPER-LOWER RELATIONSHIP.”

The chapter ends with Lim rejecting the assumption that a parent stands above a teacher merely because the teacher serves the parent’s child.

# Chapter-level observations

They are imperfect and amused by Lim’s mistakes, but they show concern for Mrs. Choi rather than fear of her.

## 2. The café reveals the parent’s operating method

Woo-jin’s mother uses community reputation, online posting, and consumer pressure to force small businesses and school employees to comply.

## 3. Lim’s teaching mistakes are deliberate narrative contrast

She is less qualified at elementary instruction than Choi, yet no one accuses her of abuse until an adult’s pride is challenged.

## 4. The central conflict is hierarchy

The mother does not merely want an explanation. She expects obedience and interprets correction as insubordination.
