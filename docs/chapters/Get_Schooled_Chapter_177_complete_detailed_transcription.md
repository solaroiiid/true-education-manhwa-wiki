## Strips 005–006 — Why fraudulent cures continue

The chapter shifts into documentary narration.

Experts explain that fake treatment systems survive because they exploit desperation.

Developmental disorders such as autism, ADHD, and borderline intellectual functioning are difficult to cure completely with modern medicine. Parents may spend years managing behavior, education, diet, and social difficulties without receiving a simple answer.

In one example, a child refuses food and demands ramen. A caregiver patiently says he must eat the prepared meal.

Parents who are exhausted by these daily struggles become vulnerable to anyone promising a total solution.

The chapter also explains the regulatory gap:

- police say they struggle to act without a clearly identified victim,
- medical associations may avoid intervention unless licensed medical malpractice is involved,
- and pseudoscientific providers describe themselves as educational or counseling institutions rather than hospitals.

Behind these programs is not merely “bad advice,” the narration says, but:

> **A SERIOUS CRIME THAT EXPLOITS HUMAN DESPERATION.**

At the hospital, Na sits beside Hyunjong and helps him eat slowly.

The child is no longer treated as imperial blood or proof of genius. He is treated as a patient who requires structure and patience.

## Strips 008–009 — Giryong reports first

Jungtaek soon receives a phone call from a teacher.

The teacher says the school cannot discuss details over the phone, but a report has been filed and the alleged bully must be investigated.

Jungtaek is confused:

> **I BULLIED HIM?!**

He says he is the victim and asks why he must be investigated.

The teacher explains:

- the school cannot decide the truth in advance,
- once a report is filed, the accused student must be investigated,
- and Giryong submitted the report first.

Jungtaek therefore files a counter-report to defend himself.

The school-violence committee convenes.

Jungtaek explains everything Giryong has done.

The committee chair then says:

> **NOW THAT WE’VE HEARD YOUR TESTIMONY ABOUT THE HARM SUFFERED, LET’S…**

> **DISCUSS YOUR ALLEGED OFFENSES.**

Jungtaek’s face empties in disbelief.

The result sheet gives both students similar or identical punishment.

The wording visible on the decision includes:

> **MUTUAL ASSAULT? CATEGORY 3 PUNISHMENT?**

Jungtaek asks:

> **WHAT IS THIS…?**

He cannot understand how two years of abuse and one defensive confrontation became equal misconduct.

Giryong watches from nearby and laughs.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The chapter completes the K-Genius arc’s public consequences

The fraudulent laboratory is raided, its victims become visible, and Wang’s Ministry career collapses.

## The documentary section explains why enforcement is difficult

Fraudulent treatment operates between medicine, education, counseling, and belief. Each institution can claim the case belongs to someone else.

## Hyunjong is shown in ordinary care

The quiet meal scene contrasts with the K-Genius spectacle. Improvement begins with safety, structure, and patience rather than promises of royal genius.

## Giryong weaponizes the reporting system

He knows Jungtaek intends to complain and files first, ensuring the true victim enters the process as an accused student.

## Mutual punishment rewards strategic lying

The committee treats mirrored allegations as symmetrical, even though the underlying history is not symmetrical.

## The duplicated reports are the central clue

The same names and keywords are not accidental. Giryong appears to have copied or anticipated Jungtaek’s account and reversed victim and offender before the genuine report could establish the narrative.
