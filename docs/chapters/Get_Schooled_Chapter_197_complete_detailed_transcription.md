## Strips 001–002 — “You rigged the trial, didn’t you?”

The new lawyer confronts the long-haired man after their brief exchange of punches.

He asks whether the mother’s extraordinary victory was secretly arranged.

His accusation is directed beyond the TRPA toward:

> **EDUCATION MINISTER CHOI GANG-SEOK**

He argues that a person capable of changing the environment around a civil trial sounds more like a Minister of Justice than an Education Minister.

The long-haired man does not deny that Choi helped create pressure around the case.

He answers that this is precisely what the Teachers’ Rights Protection Agency was created for: cases in which ordinary procedure leaves a clearly harmed person without an effective protector.

The chapter title appears.

A flashback shows Choi speaking with another experienced official or political associate.

Choi says he once believed legal and educational systems would correct obvious wrongs if facts were simply presented.

What he found instead was a series of loopholes through which victims disappeared.

He remembers muttering, almost without thinking, that people responsible for such abuse needed a beating rather than another explanation.

That frustration became part of the idea behind the TRPA.

## Strip 003 — The agency as a lifeline through broken systems

The long-haired man and the new lawyer continue speaking outside at dusk.

They agree that schools are difficult institutions:

- students can attack teachers,
- teachers can suppress students,
- adults can misuse protection laws,
- and children can exploit loopholes created to keep them safe.

The legal system is also vulnerable to people who understand its procedures better than those they harm.

The long-haired man describes Choi’s goal less as perfect justice than a lifeline.

The Minister wanted an institution able to reach victims who were genuinely wronged but could not prove their case through ordinary channels before the damage became irreversible.

The new lawyer worries that such an institution can become authoritarian.

Who decides who is “truly” wronged?

What prevents powerful officials from using violence or pressure whenever they dislike a lawful result?

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The chapter questions who owns a legal victory

Choi and the long-haired man influenced visibility and professional pressure. The new lawyer still produced the courtroom argument that won the damages award.

## The TRPA is presented as both lifeline and danger

Its purpose is to reach victims abandoned by procedural loopholes, but the lawyer correctly asks what restrains an institution that believes its moral judgment outranks ordinary law.

## The mother’s victory is incomplete

Lee’s punishment addresses the second institutional injury. It does not punish every person who helped destroy Yunju.

## Ordinary appearances are part of the horror

The former bullies are not living as obvious villains. They graduate, work, socialize, and present normal lives while the victim’s family remains trapped in grief.

## The next arc returns to the original offenders

The purple-haired narrator promises to expose Lee Daebin, Kim Yuni, and Ok Seoyoung, shifting the story from legal malpractice back to the people involved in Yunju’s school and online abuse.
