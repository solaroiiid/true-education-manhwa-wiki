This transcription follows all **25 ordered reader images** in the uploaded Chapter 157 archive. Readable dialogue, classroom exercises, game rules, and narration are preserved where legible.

### Content note

The chapter depicts classist insults, peer exclusion, competitive humiliation, and an adult striking a teacher.

## Images 001–003 — Jaeguk obeys under protest

Daniel repeats the choice:

> **LEAVE OR FOLLOW THE TEACHER’S INSTRUCTIONS.**

Jaeguk moves beside Woo-chan, but his obedience is purely tactical. He tells her and Daniel that his mother will deal with them.

At home or at a salon, Jaeguk’s mother checks a classroom-management application and sees the changed seating plan.

She immediately becomes angry that her son is sitting next to the rental-building child again.

---

## Images 004–007 — A praise exercise exposes the prejudice

Daniel begins a classroom lesson built around the proverb that even a whale will dance when praised.

He asks every student to compliment the person seated beside them.

The activity produces shy but ordinary praise around the room.

When Jaeguk’s turn arrives, he refuses to identify anything positive about Woo-chan.

He finally says that her family **“shamelessly moved in cheaply”** and calls the family’s lack of shame her defining quality.

The insult treats public housing as a moral offense committed against private buyers.

Daniel calmly turns the word back on him. He explains the relevant four-character idiom for shameless behavior and says Jaeguk’s public contempt fits it far better.

The classroom laughs, but Daniel’s point is not merely to embarrass him. He demonstrates that social confidence does not make cruelty respectable.

---

## Images 008–012 — Dodgeball is divided by status

During physical education, the class prepares for dodgeball.

Jaeguk proposes dividing teams using the familiar status categories:

- family car,
- income,
- home ownership,
- expensive phone,
- and other possessions.

He expects the wealthier side to dominate.

Daniel agrees to the division only if the game itself remains fair.

The teams are visibly uneven in social status and confidence. Woo-chan stands with the children whom Jaeguk has categorized as inferior.

---

## Images 013–017 — Daniel enters the game

The dodgeball match begins.

Jaeguk and his friends expect to target Woo-chan’s side easily. Instead, Daniel joins the lower-status team.

He catches hard throws, protects the children behind him, and returns the ball with adult speed and precision.

Jaeguk is hit repeatedly. His team loses two games without scoring a victory.

The boys protest that Daniel’s presence is unfair because an adult is stronger and more developed than middle-school children.

Daniel accepts the complaint.

---

## Images 018–021 — The unfairness is the lesson

Daniel says the physical gap between himself and the students is a circumstance they cannot control.

He then compares that gap to Woo-chan’s housing status.

She did not choose her parents’ income, mortgage eligibility, or rental contract. Punishing her for those conditions is no more courageous or fair than an adult using full strength against children.

He tells the class that discrimination based on circumstances beyond a person’s control is cowardly.

Jaeguk has no answer because the game allowed him to feel the same structural disadvantage he normally assigns to Woo-chan.

---

## Images 022–023 — Woo-chan remembers the old friendship

After class, Daniel walks with Woo-chan.

He learns that she and Jaeguk attended the same kindergarten and once played together without conflict.

The information confirms that the hostility was taught later. It did not arise naturally from the children’s personalities.

Woo-chan remains uncertain whether the lesson will matter once the parents intervene.

---

## Images 024–025 — Jaeguk’s mother attacks Daniel

A woman storms into the classroom.

She identifies herself as Jaeguk’s mother and demands to know why Daniel ignored the arrangement made with the regular homeroom teacher.

Before a discussion can begin, she slaps Daniel across the face.

His glasses shift and blood appears near his brow or cheek.

Daniel looks at her without retaliating.

The chapter ends on the evidence that Jaeguk’s contempt is not merely copied language. His mother is willing to use status, parental pressure, and physical violence to enforce the same hierarchy.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The praise exercise reveals the absence of empathy

Jaeguk cannot see Woo-chan as an individual because every observation is filtered through her building and family finances.

## Daniel uses experienced unfairness rather than abstract preaching

The dodgeball exercise makes privileged students complain about a disadvantage they cannot overcome through effort. That complaint becomes the bridge to Woo-chan’s situation.

## The kindergarten history is crucial

The children were capable of friendship before adults attached property meaning to the apartment buildings.

## The mother’s slap escalates the case

Daniel now has direct proof that the discriminatory arrangement is maintained through adult intimidation of school staff.
