# Opening — The unanswered calls

Lim attempts to call the remaining parents and reaches voicemail.

She and Na review the messages Mrs. Choi received before taking leave.

They conclude that the parents are not automatically guilty merely because the teacher became ill, but the frequency and tone of the calls are serious.

The case also exposes a structural problem: teachers commonly give parents personal numbers and remain reachable after work.

Na and Lim discuss pilot programs in which teachers receive work numbers and after-hours calls go through a school civil-service line. Emergency calls can still be connected, but routine complaints no longer enter the teacher’s private life at any hour.

Na says the Ministry is considering institutionalizing the system.

Lim notes that no phone system can completely eliminate malicious people.

# Woo-jin’s mother demands a kneeling apology

The following day, Woo-jin’s mother appears in the principal’s office.

She complains that the substitute teacher called her “ajumma,” behaved rudely, and humiliated her.

She orders Lim to kneel and apologize.

Lim answers that she will kneel if she truly did something wrong and the victim requests it.

She then proposes that the mother kneel as well for ordering teachers around and sending messages throughout the night.

> “LET’S JUST KNEEL DOWN TOGETHER AND END IT.”

The mother explodes.

The principal apologizes on Lim’s behalf and promises to “educate” her properly.

He says he will visit the mother’s home after school with an official apology.

The woman leaves while calling the Ministry of Education to make another complaint.

---

# The principal’s fear of complaints

The principal scolds Lim.

He says the mother serves on the school steering committee and has previously organized other parents against the administration.

A single complaint can bring the Ministry, media, and parent community down on the entire school.

He insists that the easiest solution is for the teacher to endure the humiliation once.

Lim asks whether his duty is to calmly hear both sides and check facts rather than automatically obey whoever complains loudest.

The principal answers that regardless of truth, higher organizations create work and risk for the school.

His submissiveness is presented as part of the system that allowed the harassment to grow.

---

# Lim reveals her identity

Lim introduces herself again:

> “I’M AN INSPECTOR FOR THE TEACHERS’ RIGHTS PROTECTION AGENCY, LIM HAN-RIM.”

The principal realizes the undercover assignment was designed to observe not only the parent but the school administration’s response.

Lim says the Minister’s agency will provide an exemplary answer. The principal’s responsibility is to deliver it properly without making another mistake.

---

# The online smear campaign

Woo-jin’s mother posts in the neighborhood parent café.

She claims the new teacher called her “ajumma” maliciously, poured ice on her, and behaved like someone from a cheap nightclub.

Other mothers comment, threaten reports, circulate the photograph, and offer to post it in apartment elevators.

The post removes the context in which the mother threatened the café worker, initiated the confrontation, and demanded a kneeling apology.

---

# The principal finally refuses

The principal visits Woo-jin’s mother alone.

She expects Lim to appear and kneel.

Instead, the principal says he reviewed the recorded conversation and café CCTV.

The mother was also aggressive, and she was the person who tried to pour or throw the ice.

He says both sides made mistakes and asks her to stop escalating the dispute.

He adds a new rule:

> Even if a teacher does something wrong, the school will no longer comply with personal demands that the teacher kneel.

He tells her the school is not a place for parents to impose whatever punishment they desire.

The chapter ends with the principal taking the first stand he previously believed was institutionally impossible.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. Complaint avoidance becomes administrative policy

The principal knows facts matter, but fears the cost of proving them.

## 2. A kneeling apology is about rank

The mother is not seeking repair. She wants a public posture confirming that the teacher is beneath her.

## 3. Lim tests the principal as much as the parent

The undercover operation reveals whether school leadership will protect staff when a complaint appears politically inconvenient.

## 4. Evidence changes the response

The recording and CCTV prevent the mother from controlling the incident through a one-sided online narrative.
