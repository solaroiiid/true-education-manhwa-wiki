## Strips 001–002 — The shared-responsibility field trip begins

The school proceeds with a field trip described as a mudflat or outdoor-experience excursion.

The students are thrilled.

They laugh, pose, eat snacks, and treat the day as the best part of elementary school.

Hanrim and Sera supervise alongside school staff.

The homeroom teacher remains visibly anxious despite the additional adults.

She monitors every movement and repeatedly urges children not to separate from the group.

Sera initially thinks the teacher is excessively tense.

Na’s earlier explanation echoes through the chapter: if a field trip were only an enjoyable educational outing, teachers would want it too.

The real burden is that an individual teacher, not the office or school board, may be expected to answer for everything that happens.

One accident can become a prosecution.

## Strips 003–004 — The teacher prevents an early accident

Several boys begin roughhousing.

One child is pushed or loses his balance near a dangerous edge.

The teacher rushes forward, catches him, and stops the fall.

She angrily orders the children to settle down.

Sera is surprised by both her speed and strength.

The teacher says boys that age sometimes act without thinking.

When discipline is required, she contacts parents and documents the event.

Even that carries risk.

If she grips a student too firmly or a parent dislikes the tone she used, the safety intervention may become a child-abuse complaint.

Sera notices the contradiction: the teacher must prevent every injury while remaining afraid to touch the children forcefully enough to stop them.

## Strips 005–006 — Lunch and the hidden labor behind the day

At lunchtime, the children open packed meals.

Sera proudly produces a large lunch box she prepared at dawn and says her cooking is excellent.

She made food for Hanrim as well.

Hanrim doubts the claim but agrees to taste it.

The teachers use lunch time to check groups, count students, and identify anyone without food.

The chapter emphasizes the ordinary labor beneath the excursion:

- planning meals;
- managing allergies;
- ensuring no child is isolated;
- and supervising even while staff themselves eat.

The final scheduled activity is a treasure hunt.

The homeroom teacher explains that each child can find only one treasure.

The prizes are intentionally modest and nearly equivalent, discouraging aggressive competition.

Children who finish help classmates still searching.

Sera realizes that what looks like a simple game has been designed to teach cooperation.

## Strips 007–008 — The trip reaches a safe conclusion

A child has difficulty finding a treasure.

The teacher helps without simply handing over the answer.

She praises effort rather than first place.

Sera compares the program with her own school experience, when activities focused more heavily on ranking.

The staff distribute consolation prizes and ensure no child returns empty-handed.

The group reaches the end of the trip without a major accident.

Students say they had fun.

The homeroom teacher relaxes for the first time.

The successful day seems to support the shared-responsibility model: teachers, agency staff, and other adults divide the supervision rather than leaving one person to watch every child.

---

## Strips 009–010 — A student runs into the road

As the group prepares to leave, Sera reflects on how constant protection may also lower children’s independence.

A student suddenly realizes his phone is missing.

He believes it may be back at the mudflat area.

Without waiting for permission, he runs toward the road.

A vehicle approaches.

The homeroom teacher sees him and sprints forward.

She tackles or drags the child out of the vehicle’s path.

Both fall hard onto the pavement.

The teacher shouts:

> **HOW CAN YOU JUST RUN INTO THE STREET WITHOUT THINKING?!**

> **ARE YOU CRAZY? DO YOU HAVE A DEATH WISH?!**

The boy begins crying.

The teacher immediately softens and apologizes for frightening him.

The near-fatal event lasts only seconds and occurs after a full day of careful planning.

## Strips 011–012 — The child’s mother threatens the rescuer

A woman jumps from a nearby car.

She is the boy’s mother.

Sera asks why a parent is secretly accompanying the field trip.

The mother says she was worried about the school’s ability to keep the children safe, so she followed.

She saw the teacher grab her son’s collar during the rescue.

The mother ignores that the teacher saved him from the vehicle.

She seizes the teacher by the shirt and shouts that she witnessed child abuse.

> **YOU! I’M GOING TO SUE YOU FOR CHILD ABUSE!**

Sera tells her to let go.

The woman is threatening the colleague who saved her child’s life.

The homeroom teacher clenches her teeth but does not retaliate.

Sera’s anger rises as the chapter ends.

> **AND WHY THE HELL IS A MOM TAGGING ALONG ON HER KID’S FIELD TRIP?**

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The day succeeds because responsibility is shared

The school, TRPA, and multiple adults provide layers of supervision that one homeroom teacher could not.

## Safety work is largely invisible

Lunch planning, head counts, social inclusion, and activity design receive little attention until something goes wrong.

## The road incident is genuinely unpredictable

The child acts suddenly after the planned program is already ending.

## Rescue and alleged abuse become the same physical act

The teacher must grab the child roughly enough to save him, yet that contact becomes the basis of the mother’s threat.

## The mother’s presence does not prevent the danger

She secretly follows from a car but still cannot stop her son from running into traffic; the teacher does.
