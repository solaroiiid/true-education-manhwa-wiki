# Chapter-level continuity notes

## So-rin’s lie began as social maintenance

She did not possess evidence or even a concrete rumor. She agreed with Hee-soo’s paranoia to keep the friendship stable and avoid becoming the next target.

## Hee-soo’s identity depends on grievance

Even when commenters ask about the victim, she returns to the alleged insult against herself. The accusation gives moral cover to violence she already wanted to perform.

## The institutions struggle with manipulated evidence

The school committee and police see fragments that can be presented as mutual assault. The girls understand that ambiguity, age, and public performance make immediate accountability difficult.

## Na removes editing from the confrontation

The live discussion uses explicit rules: one speaker at a time, no violence, no profanity, direct evidence. This structure collapses the girls’ preferred strategy of shouting, staging, and clipping.

## The confession creates a second victim

So-rin participated in Soo-hyun’s abuse, but the moment she contradicts Hee-soo she is physically attacked. The scene demonstrates that her earlier loyalty was maintained by fear as much as admiration.
