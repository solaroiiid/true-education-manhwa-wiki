## Images 001–003 — Daniel confronts Sera in the office

Daniel returns to the agency office holding the quick-loan flyer.

Sera is reading an article about the Sunshine Land discrimination controversy.

Daniel asks directly whether she engineered Jaeguk’s mother’s financial collapse.

Sera does not initially deny manipulating the situation.

She says the woman’s ruin was caused by her own debt, class obsession, and decision to take a dangerous loan.

Daniel replies that placing the trigger still matters.

## Images 004–007 — Sera admits manipulating Jaeguk’s new school experience

A flashback reveals that Sera interfered after Jaeguk transferred.

She deliberately encouraged or arranged conditions in which the boy would initially be rejected.

Her stated goal was to make the mother see a new victim: her own son.

Sera says pain must sometimes be emotional and personal. A perpetrator who feels only abstract criticism can dismiss it; someone who sees a loved child suffer may finally understand.

Daniel is disturbed that she used a child as leverage.

## Images 008–010 — Their methods are contrasted

Sera argues that the outcome was good.

Jaeguk learned that apartment status does not determine friendship.

Woo-chan was protected.

The complex removed its discriminatory practices.

The mother lost the social platform from which she harmed others.

Daniel says the result does not erase the method.

He questions whether the agency can call itself educational if it manufactures bullying and financial catastrophe.

Sera answers that people who create deep wounds cannot always be reached with clean hands.

## Images 011–14 — The children reconcile

Back at Sunshine Land, Woo-chan and Jaeguk meet again.

Their conversation begins awkwardly.

They jokingly call one another **“villa bum”** and **“rental bum,”** then recognize how absurd the words sound when separated from adult hostility.

They begin playing together.

The repaired friendship demonstrates that the children’s conflict was never inevitable.

Jaeguk’s mother watches, exhausted and quieter than before.

Over the next six months, she continues working, and relations between the families gradually improve.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Sera’s philosophy treats consequences as instruction

She is willing to let targets experience fear, humiliation, and material loss if those consequences reproduce what they inflicted on others.

## Daniel’s objection is institutional, not sentimental

He understands the appeal of the outcome but worries that an agency empowered to engineer suffering can become indistinguishable from the people it disciplines.

## The children’s reconciliation confirms the source of the prejudice

Once the apartment system stops rewarding division, Jaeguk and Woo-chan can return to ordinary childhood interaction.

## The beach transition introduces employment-based hierarchy

The next victims are adults, but their temporary contracts make them vulnerable in a way similar to rental residents: they are physically present in the institution while treated as if they do not truly belong.
