# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The rescue from the rooftop does not resolve the addiction

Once the stream produces thousands of followers and supportive messages, So-rin feels happier rather than chastened. The suicidal threat is immediately absorbed into a new identity as a persecuted survivor.

## The grandmother cannot compete with the phone

A real caregiver physically holds So-rin and offers unconditional listening. So-rin ignores her because the anonymous audience provides measurable, rapidly increasing attention.

## So-rin tries to imitate the agency’s public tactics

She proposes livestreaming the destruction of an agency vehicle to regain control of the story. The act is not private revenge; it is planned as a performance for followers.

## The ban removes the feedback loop

Losing the account terrifies her more than being caught with a hammer. Followers, comments, and viewer counts had become the structure through which she understood reality.

## Daniel identifies selective perception

The supportive comments were a minority. So-rin mentally discarded the overwhelming criticism and treated the small defending group as evidence that everyone supported her.

## Destroying the phone is symbolic and coercive

Na does not claim the physical device caused every problem. He breaks the immediate access point and tells her she must return to a world where other people do not exist merely as numbers, comments, or an audience.
