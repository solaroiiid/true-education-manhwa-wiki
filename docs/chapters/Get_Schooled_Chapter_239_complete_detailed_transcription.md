## Strip 001 — A parent reports a school that has stopped taking risks

The TRPA has returned to processing its backlog while the G.Y. investigation continues elsewhere.

Sera presents an unusually long report submitted by a parent at **Sangmin Elementary School**.

The parent says the school has recently behaved strangely.

The writer’s child had looked forward to fifth-grade activities, including:

- a field trip;
- school excursions;
- and playing soccer during lunch.

The school abruptly cancels the field trip and announces that no future trips are planned.

It also bans lunchtime soccer, claiming that student safety requires “overreaction.”

The parent asks whether the school is violating students’ right to a proper education.

Sera complains loudly because elementary school trips and excursions were the only parts of school she ever looked forward to.

Na initially says extracurricular activities are generally left to school discretion.

Minister Choi Gang-seok enters and says the youngest inspector is right to find the pattern strange.

Schools that avoid field trips and excursions entirely are becoming increasingly common.

## Strip 002 — The Minister orders a national inquiry

Choi explains that the Ministry has been monitoring the trend.

A school may decide its own extracurricular schedule, but the nationwide retreat from outdoor activity could deprive children of diverse learning opportunities.

He orders the TRPA to analyze the cause and report back.

The team travels to the elementary school.

They begin by asking why lunchtime soccer has been prohibited.

## Strips 003–004 — Teachers fear injuries and complaints

The inspectors find older students secretly playing soccer.

A teacher runs onto the field and orders them back to class.

The students say the ban appeared suddenly.

Sera introduces the Ministry delegation and asks why the school will not allow a simple game.

The teacher says safety is only part of the answer.

Parents increasingly overreact to even small scratches.

If a child is injured badly enough to require a hospital visit, the school may be held fully responsible.

The staff also receives constant complaints:

- older students take too much space;
- younger children feel threatened;
- girls feel excluded;
- and any small injury is treated as proof that the school failed.

The school calendar originally included a field trip.

Teachers opposed it, so the event was replaced by a one-day study tour.

The immediate trigger was an incident at another school:

> **THE YOUNGBIN ELEMENTARY SCHOOL INCIDENT**

## Strips 005–006 — The acquitted teacher describes Youngjun’s death

The team meets the teacher prosecuted after Youngjun died.

He is currently on leave.

The accident occurred during free time near a stream on a school trip.

More than thirty children were playing in the water while he supervised them alone.

One child slipped backward, struck his head against a rock, and suffered a cerebral hemorrhage.

The teacher helped rush him to the hospital, but the child died.

At the morgue, Youngjun’s mother blamed him for failing to prevent the accident.

The teacher says he initially believed that bearing the family’s anger was part of being the homeroom teacher.

Then prosecutors indicted him for negligent homicide.

He began asking whether one supervising adult should bear sole criminal responsibility for an unforeseeable event involving more than thirty children.

A court eventually acquitted him because the accident was ruled unpredictable.

The acquittal did not restore his confidence or career.

He has no idea what will happen to another teacher at Youngbin Elementary whose similar case produced a guilty verdict.

## Strip 006 — Two pressures pushing teachers away from field trips

Back at the agency, the team identifies two major forces.

First, safety incidents can expose an individual supervising teacher to criminal prosecution and professional ruin.

Second, parents submit enormous numbers of complaints over trivial or uncontrollable matters.

The generation gap between parents and young teachers is already difficult.

The possibility that one mistake—or one accident that may not be a mistake—can end a career makes outdoor activities feel irrationally dangerous.

Sera initially criticizes overprotective parents.

Hanrim adds that the Youngbin court ruling appears to be the decisive trigger behind the national retreat.

---

## Strip 007 — The team chooses Youngbin Elementary

The inspectors agree that the problem cannot be understood from general statistics alone.

They will go to Youngbin Elementary the following day and study the case that frightened schools nationwide.

Na says a teacher should not be expected to bear sole responsibility for every event during a field trip.

The team leaves the office while the public controversy is already expanding.

## Strip 008 — Teachers protest outside the Ministry

News footage shows a national protest outside the Ministry of Education.

Teachers carry signs reading approximately:

- **TEACHERS ARE NOT GODS!**
- **PROTECT OUR TEACHERS IMMEDIATELY!**
- **IMMEDIATELY SUSPEND FIELD TRIPS THAT FAIL TO PROTECT TEACHERS!**
- **HOW MUCH LONGER MUST TEACHERS ALONE BEAR FULL RESPONSIBILITY FOR UNFORESEEABLE ACCIDENTS?**
- **ENFORCE FIELD TRIPS WITH GUARANTEED SAFETY!**

A reporter summarizes the Youngbin case.

A child died after being bitten by a snake during a field trip.

The supervising teacher was found guilty of negligent homicide.

Teachers’ unions across the country demand a system that protects staff from impossible liability.

Na looks toward the protest and concludes that, without reform, school trips and excursions may disappear permanently.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## The phenomenon begins with rational fear

Schools are not merely becoming lazy. Staff see outdoor activities as events where one accident can produce criminal charges.

## The chapter separates two cases

The teacher interviewed by the TRPA was acquitted after a stream accident. The Youngbin teacher was convicted after a snakebite incident.

## Parent complaints amplify legal anxiety

Even when no serious accident occurs, staff face constant retrospective claims that every inconvenience should have been predicted.

## Canceling activities transfers the cost to children

Avoidance protects institutions and staff but removes experiences from students nationwide.

## The investigation is aimed at the trigger, not only the symptom

Youngbin Elementary becomes the focal point because its guilty verdict changed teacher behavior far beyond one school.
