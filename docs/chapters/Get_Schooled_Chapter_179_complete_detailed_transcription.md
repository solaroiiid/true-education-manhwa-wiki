## Strips 001–002 — Daniel stops Sera from striking students

Sera sees several boys crowding and provoking another student during break.

One shouts obscene insults, while another complains that someone cannot watch where he is going. Sera assumes a fight is beginning and prepares to “shut him up.”

Daniel appears in his undercover-teacher role:

> **WHAT ARE YOU ALL DOING?**

> **YOUR BREAK’S OVER. GET BACK TO YOUR CLASSROOM.**

The students disperse.

Sera nearly swings at one of them, but Daniel catches her before she can turn a covert investigation into an open assault.

He says:

> **THAT ALMOST WENT REALLY BAD, SERA-SSI.**

Then adds dryly that it would have gone badly for the children, not for her.

Sera studies his disguised appearance. She finds the teacher unexpectedly handsome and vaguely familiar.

When she realizes he is Daniel, she asks whether the American inspector is cosplaying as a handsome boarding-house man.

Daniel explains that after the rental-apartment case, his personal information was exposed, so he had to alter his appearance for the new infiltration.

They agree to exchange what they have learned.

## Strips 003–004 — Daniel finds Giryong’s juvenile record

Daniel reports that both boys have remained quiet at school.

Giryong is reserved. Jungtaek reacts sharply even to small provocation. Based only on current behavior, Daniel says Jungtaek appears more suspicious.

However, Giryong’s school record contains a major irregularity.

He previously received a serious juvenile disposition and spent approximately **six months in a juvenile detention facility** after an assault.

The underlying victim suffered a **ruptured pancreas**, an injury severe enough that the committee could not treat it as a routine school fight.

Sera is stunned:

> **WHAT THE HECK? HE’S DONE THIS BEFORE?!**

Daniel warns that a person cannot be judged solely by the worst act in their past. He contacted Giryong’s previous school, which confirmed that Giryong has not caused significant trouble since returning.

Still, the old record may explain why the current committee distrusted him even when he presented evidence.

---

## Strips 005–006 — Giryong tells Daniel about his former life

Daniel finds Giryong completing school-service punishment, including cleaning a staff restroom with an injured arm.

Daniel asks whether the arm was hurt during the incident with Jungtaek.

Giryong says that during puberty he genuinely became the stereotypical bully.

He followed popular delinquents, picked fights over trivial provocations, and treated brawling as something impressive.

A flashback shows one fight going catastrophically wrong. Giryong hits another boy, who collapses unconscious with his eyes rolling back.

Students panic and call an ambulance.

The victim’s pancreas is ruptured. Giryong is sent immediately to juvenile detention.

His story is not framed as a wrongful conviction. He accepts that he was responsible.

---

## Strip 007 — Six months later, the family is financially broken

After Giryong returns home, the family no longer lives as it did before.

The victim’s medical bills and settlement costs have devastated their finances. Giryong’s mother works long hours and leaves food in the refrigerator for him to heat alone.

Seeing the consequences of the assault, Giryong finally understands the scale of what he did.

He resolves never to fight again.

Other students soon learn he has been to juvenile detention.

The reputation follows him into his next school.

---

## Strip 008 — Jungtaek uses Giryong’s reputation against him

According to Giryong, Yu Jungtaek knows nearly everything about the old case:

- that Giryong was feared in detention,
- that he injured another student severely,
- and that he promised never to fight again.

Jungtaek uses the reputation for his own status. He portrays himself as someone powerful enough to command or humiliate the dangerous former delinquent.

Giryong says he endured the bullying and never fought back because he did not want to see his mother cry again.

When she notices the injured arm, however, she assumes he has returned to violence.

> **GIRYONG, WHAT’S WRONG WITH YOUR ARM?! DID YOU FIGHT AGAIN?!**

His silence does not protect her. It only makes her believe the worst.

Giryong concludes:

> **MY PATIENCE ONLY RESULTED IN MOM’S TEARS.**

Despite knowing that his record would place him at a disadvantage, he files a school-violence report against Jungtaek.

---

## Strips 009–010 — The past discredits the present

The school-violence committee sees Giryong’s evidence but cannot fully separate it from his old reputation.

The result is declared mutual assault.

Afterward, Jungtaek posts or celebrates online. A visible caption reads approximately:

> **RETALIATION-REPORTING IS AWESOME!**

He mocks the idea that anyone will believe a person with a juvenile record.

The chapter reflects on stigma:

> **IT’S COMMONLY ACCEPTED THAT MESSED-UP PEOPLE HARDLY EVER CHANGE.**

For someone who genuinely did something terrible once, living afterward is not easy. Even correct behavior can be interpreted as another performance.

Giryong thanks Daniel:

> **YOU WERE THE FIRST ONE WHO LISTENED TO MY SIDE OF THE STORY.**

Daniel does not claim absolute certainty, but he finds the account coherent and supported by the available history.

He calls Sera.

> **DID YOU HEAR THAT, SERA-SSI?**

Daniel says he trusts the situation enough to keep watching rather than arresting either narrative prematurely.

## Strip 011 — A nighttime meeting is arranged

Late that night, Giryong arrives at a dark alley or underpass.

Jungtaek asks:

> **WHY DID YOU CALL ME HERE AT THIS HOUR, YU JUNGTAEK?**

The two boys are finally making direct contact after days of avoiding one another.

Sera and Daniel monitor the meeting remotely.

Sera announces dramatically:

> **DRUM ROLL~!**

> **ALRIGHT, NOW WHO’S THE REAL BULLY…!**

The chapter ends before the first decisive act.

# Chapter-level continuity notes

## Giryong’s confession gains credibility because it includes guilt

He does not erase his earlier violence. He describes the injury, detention, financial consequences, and mother’s suffering directly.

## Rehabilitation creates a new vulnerability

His promise never to fight means a new bully can act with confidence that Giryong will not resist.

## Stigma functions as evidence in reverse

Every detail that should demonstrate change—silence, avoidance, refusal to fight—can be reinterpreted as manipulation because of his record.

## Daniel is the first adult to listen without deciding from reputation

That attention matters even before the agency determines whether every part of Giryong’s story is true.

## The final meeting remains dangerous

Both boys know that accusations, evidence, and prior records now matter as much as physical strength. The encounter may be designed to produce another piece of usable proof.
