This transcription follows the reading order reconstructed from the uploaded MHT: **11 reader pages containing 21 chapter strips, plus the separate cover image**. Dialogue, narration, captions, credits, and legible English text are preserved as printed, including awkward phrasing, line-break-driven hyphenation, profanity, and translation quirks.

One short section on **Reader Page 9** is visibly damaged inside the embedded source artwork itself. The fresh MHT contains the same damaged pixels, so the obscured frames cannot be recovered from this archive. That gap is marked precisely below; no action or dialogue has been invented.

Korean sound effects are described by their visible function when the exact Korean lettering or English equivalent is uncertain.

## Reader Page 1 — The plan and the scandal

### Opening disclaimer

A black screen carries the site watermark **“1STKISSMANGA.IO”** and a centered all-caps disclaimer:

> **THIS WORK IS A**  
> **FICTIONAL STORY MADE VIRTUALLY.**  
> **IT HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH**  
> **CERTAIN PEOPLE, ORGANIZATIONS,**  
> **RELIGIOUS GROUPS, ETC.**

The severe black-and-white presentation delays the story image and gives the opening the tone of a legal notice.

### Government-office conversation

**Panel / beat 1:** A speech balloon appears in darkness before the speaker is fully established:

> **THIS WAS THE PLAN, RIGHT?**

**Panel / beat 2:** A hand drops or slaps a personnel file onto a polished wooden desk. The folder contains a photograph of **Ryu Jun-hyung**, shown with short, spiky black hair and a faint, self-assured smile. A large Korean impact sound effect beside the hand indicates the file landing sharply.

**Panel / beat 3:** The speaker continues while the framing begins to reveal a formal office:

> **WHAT IF WE CATCH A SCHOOL WITH THE SON OF A MEMBER OF THE CONGRESS IN THE HISTORIC FIRST MISSION?**

The wording treats the school not merely as a disciplinary case but as a politically useful test case for the newly created program.

**Panel / beat 4:** Na Hwa-jin sits loosely in a wooden armchair. He wears an entirely black outfit—black suit jacket, black shirt, and black trousers. His long, disordered hair falls over his eyes; one hand is lifted in a casual, almost theatrical gesture. Behind him, a broad-shouldered older official in a dark business suit stands with his back partly turned. The contrast is immediate: Na looks relaxed and irreverent, while the official occupies the room like a senior bureaucrat.

> **BUT THE TEACHERS WON’T COOPERATE BECAUSE THEY’RE SCARED OF HIS FATHER.**

**Panel / beat 5:** Close-up on the older official’s lower face. He has a heavy mustache and beard and holds a thick cigar between his teeth. He lights it with an expensive-looking gold lighter.

> **I ALSO CAN’T TAKE RESPONSIBILITY IF IT WERE TO TURN INTO A POLITICAL PROBLEM.**

A vertical Korean flame/flick sound effect accompanies the lighter.

**Panel / beat 6:** The official turns slightly in the foreground while Na, farther back, spreads his hands as if the objection is beside the point.

> **DON’T TAKE THAT INTO ACCOUNT AND JUST DO WHAT YOU HAVE TO DO!**

The line is directed at Na: he is being told not to calculate the political consequences and simply perform the assigned intervention.

**Panel / beat 7:** Extreme close-up of the official’s cigar and grin. The ember glows. His expression is confident rather than worried.

> **THOSE ARE JUST TRIVIAL THINGS…**

**Panel / beat 8:** In a large isolated balloon over a white-to-gray gradient, the official concludes:

> **I WILL TAKE CARE OF IT!**

The statement implies that the political protection surrounding the mission has already been arranged.

### The scandal breaks

**Panel / beat 9:** A television news broadcast fills the frame. A male news anchor sits against a blue studio background. The station mark reads **“JTV뉴스”**. A lower-third headline states:

> **REP. RYU KWANG-PIL’S SCANDAL,**  
> **SEXUAL HARASSMENT, BRIBERY…**

The broadcast publicly destroys the political shield that Jun-hyung expected to remain permanent.

**Panel / beat 10:** Close-up on Jun-hyung. His face is drained and slick with sweat; his eyes are stretched wide, with heavy gray shading beneath them. His mouth hangs open in disbelief.

> **FA…**  
> **FATHER…?**

**Panel / beat 11:** A brown-haired student stands in front of Jun-hyung and holds up a smartphone displaying the same television report. Na can be seen standing farther behind them, dressed in black. Jun-hyung is viewed from the back, his spiky hair filling the foreground.

> **N… NO,**  
> **SOMETHING’S NOT RIGHT**  
> **WITH THIS!**

**Panel / beat 12:** The page ends on the beginning of a close-up of Na’s face. His long hair shadows his eyes, and the visible corner of his mouth suggests a restrained, knowing smile. The composition implies that the scandal is not a coincidence from Na’s perspective.

**Panel / beat 6:** A rectangular narration box states:

> **I WAS TREATED LIKE GARBAGE AT HOME.**

A younger Jun-hyung, wearing a dark sleeveless shirt and beige shorts, is thrown violently onto a bed or padded surface. His body folds at the waist, one hand grabbing his thigh as he cries out. > **DIDN’T I TELL YOU TO STUDY BETTER?!**

> **WHAT’S WRONG WITH RYU KWANG-PIL’S SON’S GRADES? OH!**

The mother pleads:

> **HONEY…**  
> **PLEASE STOP!**

**Panel / beat 8:** Jun-hyung looks up from the floor, terrified and sweating, while the father turns his fury toward the mother.

> **YOU’RE NO BETTER! DON’T YOU KNOW HOW TO EDUCATE YOUR SON?!**

A narration box follows:

> **I WAS AN UNBELIEVABLE EMBARRASSMENT TO MY FATHER, A LOCAL CONGRESSMAN AND LAWMAKER.**

**Panel / beat 9:** The page ends with another narration box against black:

> **BUT THAT WASN’T THE CASE OUTSIDE.**

The transition establishes the central contradiction in Jun-hyung’s upbringing: humiliation at home, absolute privilege everywhere else.

## Reader Page 3 — How privilege taught Jun-hyung to enjoy cruelty

### Adults reward the connection

**Panel / beat 1:** A teacher bends over the seated younger Jun-hyung and pats his hair with false warmth. Jun-hyung’s smile is uneasy and guarded.

> **HOW’S YOUR FATHER? TALK ABOUT YOUR TEACHER TO HIM SOMETIMES. ALRIGHT?**

The teacher’s interest is openly transactional: Jun-hyung is valuable as access to a politician.

**Panel / beat 2:** A group of boys crowds around Jun-hyung’s desk, smiling and leaning toward him with exaggerated friendliness.

> **YOUR FATHER’S A CONGRESSMAN!**

> **OH! YOU HAVE IT EASY! YOU MUST HAVE A HECK OF A LIFE AT HOME, TOO!**

The irony is severe because the reader has just seen the violence inside that supposedly easy home.

### Social power becomes sadism

**Panel / beat 3:** A narration box reads:

> **EVERYONE BOWED DOWN TO ME BECAUSE OF MY FATHER.**

Jun-hyung, now older and wearing a black hoodie with red striping, stands outside the school with a cigarette between his fingers. He rests one heavy black sneaker on the head or upper body of a student lying face-down at his feet. Two companions watch from behind—one smiling, one filming with a phone.

A second narration box states:

> **IN SCHOOL, I EVEN ENJOYED MORE POWER THAN MY FATHER HIMSELF.**

The low camera angle makes Jun-hyung look enormous while the victim is reduced to a surface beneath his shoe.

**Panel / beat 4:** Close-up of the thick sole pressing into the victim’s face.

> **I NEVER LET ANYONE GO UNSCATHED IF THEY CHALLENGED MY AUTHORITY.**

**Panel / beat 5:** A partially obscured victim’s face is pinned to the floor. Jun-hyung’s narration continues over deep black space:

> **SEEING A PERSON CRUMBLE UNDER MY COMMAND LIKE A BUG…**

**Panel / beat 6:** Extreme close-up on Jun-hyung’s smile. His eyes are pale and distant, his grin narrow and satisfied.

> **IT WAS MY GREATEST PLEASURE.**

**Panel / beat 7:** The next narration box creates a pause:

> **THERE WAS JUST ONE PROBLEM.**

**Panel / beat 8:** A dark, blurred image evokes a body falling or the aftermath of a fatal drop. The motion is shown indirectly rather than as a clear corpse.

> **A BUG THAT I WAS PLAYING WITH COMMITTED SUICIDE.**

The dehumanizing word “bug” is retained in the narration, showing that even after the death Jun-hyung still categorizes the victim as an object.

### Summoned after the death

**Panel / beat 9:** A hallway sign reads:

> **COUNSELING ROOM**

Jun-hyung, in a white school shirt and black tie, hesitates with his hand on the doorknob. Sweat beads on his cheek. The page ends before he enters.

## Reader Page 4 — The moment he learns there are no consequences

### Fear outside the counseling room

**Panel / beat 1:** Jun-hyung’s hand trembles on the door handle. A repeated vertical Korean trembling sound effect runs beside him.

A narration box states:

> **THERE’S A POLICE OFFICER IN THERE.**

> **IF MY FATHER FINDS OUT ABOUT THIS, HE’LL KILL ME.**

The fear is not remorse for the dead student. He is afraid of his father’s reaction and the possibility that the private abuse at home will worsen.

**Panel / beat 3:** Jun-hyung opens the counseling-room door with a Korean creaking sound effect. Inside, two uniformed police officers sit casually, separated from the room by decorative folding screens.

One officer gestures toward the seat:

> **THIS IS JUST A FORMALITY, SO SIT DOWN FOR AT LEAST 10 MINUTES.**

The dead student and the allegations are not mentioned. The officers care more about maintaining the appearance of an interview and establishing political familiarity.

## Reader Page 6 — Na refuses to save him from the consequences

### Na emerges from the restroom

**Panel / beat 1:** Na steps out near the sinks, still adjusting his trousers with one hand and holding toilet paper in the other. He looks tired and irritated rather than alarmed.

> **I HAVE TO LOOSEN MY SPHINCTER, BUT I CAN’T CONCENTRATE!**

The deliberately crude line punctures Jun-hyung’s melodramatic rage.

**Panel / beat 2:** Na looks toward the battered Jun-hyung and clicks his tongue.

> **TSK, TSK, TSK… YOU LOST EVERYTHING YOU HAD JUST BECAUSE YOUR FAMILY GOT RUINED.**

The wording is blunt: Jun-hyung never possessed personal loyalty or respect; he possessed borrowed authority.

**Panel / beat 3:** Jun-hyung turns sharply toward Na.

> **HEY, KID. WHY DIDN’T YOU MAKE ANY GOOD FRIENDS?**

**Panel / beat 4:** Jun-hyung springs to his feet and shouts across the restroom entrance while Na calmly faces the sink.

> **WHAT, YOU WERE LISTENING ALL THIS TIME?!**

**Panel / beat 5:** Jun-hyung charges closer, accusing him:

> **THEN WHY DIDN’T YOU STOP THEM! YOU’RE A TERRIBLE TEACHER!**

> **YOU USUALLY DO MORE FOR LESS, YOU FUCKER!**

Jun-hyung expects Na to use force on his behalf because he has seen Na intervene violently in other situations. Even while being victimized, he still frames assistance as something he is entitled to demand.

### “Washing my hands”

**Panel / beat 6:** Na turns on the faucet. Water runs over his hand with a Korean water sound effect.

> **WHY? IT’S NOT MY BUSINESS ANYMORE.**

**Panel / beat 7:** Na holds both wet hands up loosely while the toilet-paper roll hangs from one wrist. His expression is almost bored.

> **I’M WASHING MY HANDS OF THIS SCHOOL, AS OF TODAY.**

The image literalizes the idiom: he is physically washing his hands as he announces the end of his assignment.

### Official departure and false normality

**Panel / beat 8:** Exterior of the school at sunset. Warm orange light falls over the empty courtyard.

> **AS OF TODAY, THE IN-SCHOOL GUIDANCE OF THE OFFICE OF PROTECTION HAS BEEN COMPLETED.**

> **THERE WILL BE NO MORE RECKLESS CORPORAL PUNISHMENT IN THE FUTURE, AND THE SCHOOL WILL RETURN TO NORMAL.**

The phrase “return to normal” is ominous because the school’s normal state was the culture that allowed Dae-seok’s abuse and death.

**Panel / beat 9:** Back in a classroom, a bespectacled male teacher stands at the lectern. The students are already talking, turning around, and celebrating. A purple-haired boy at the side grins toward his classmates. Small informal lettering beside him reads approximately:

> **Oh, no, they said it’s finished!**

The teacher addresses them:

> **THERE MUST BE A LOT OF THINGS YOU’VE ALL REALIZED FROM THIS, RIGHT?**

> **DO YOU THINK YOU CAN NOW FOLLOW THE TEACHERS’ GUIDANCE SO THAT THIS DOESN’T HAPPEN EVER AGAIN?**

His hopeful question is visually undercut by the students’ immediate return to noisy disorder.

# Chapter-level continuity and visual observations

## 1. The opening reveals that the political collapse was planned protection, not luck

The first office conversation reframes the end of Chapter 4. The intervention team deliberately selected a school connected to a congressman for its first historic mission. The senior official’s promise—**“I will take care of it!”**—is followed immediately by the televised destruction of Ryu Kwang-pil’s reputation. The sequence strongly implies that Na entered the school knowing Jun-hyung’s political shield would be removed at the decisive moment.

## 2. Jun-hyung learned two incompatible lessons at home and outside

At home, his father treated him as a defective extension of a political brand. Outside, teachers, classmates, and even police treated him as untouchable. The chapter presents his cruelty as learned entitlement without excusing it: he was abused privately, then rewarded publicly whenever he reproduced that domination against weaker people.

## 3. The counseling-room scene is the psychological turning point

Jun-hyung enters believing the police may expose him to his father’s punishment. Instead, the officers smile, ask about the congressman, and call the interview a formality. His conclusion is explicit: **“I’ll be forgiven no matter what I do at school.”** The close-up that follows shows fear transforming into pleasure. Institutional corruption does not merely conceal his behavior; it teaches him that cruelty is proof of kingship.

## 4. Removing one protected bully does not reform the school culture

After Na departs, teachers announce that the school will “return to normal.” The very next sequence shows that normality: humiliation notes, public assault, theft, and spectatorship. The only structural change is the identity of the victim. The narration states this directly—bullying has not ended; **the target has changed to Jun-hyung**.

## 5. Jun-hyung experiences victimization without developing empathy

Even after being beaten, stripped, mocked, and abandoned, Jun-hyung does not connect his suffering to what he inflicted on Dae-seok and others. He demands that Na protect him, threatens revenge when his father returns, and ultimately prepares to burn the school. Na’s question—**“You still don’t know what you did wrong, do you?”**—names the core failure more precisely than any physical punishment could.

## 6. Fire is used as a contrast between uncontrolled revenge and controlled authority

Jun-hyung floods the corridor with gasoline while repeating “Kill.” Na later produces a small, steady lighter flame. Both characters hold the ability to ignite the school, but the imagery distinguishes them: Jun-hyung is surrounded by spilled fuel and emotional chaos; Na holds the flame deliberately and announces the beginning of instruction. The chapter ends before revealing whether the flame is used literally, psychologically, or as intimidation.

## 7. The damaged source interval does not interrupt the main causal sequence

Although the embedded artwork is corrupted immediately after the father’s golf-club swing, the intact material before and after establishes the chapter’s progression: the report exposes Jun-hyung, Ryu Kwang-pil attacks him in rage, and Jun-hyung later enters the school with gasoline. No dialogue or specific intervening action has been supplied beyond what remains visible.
