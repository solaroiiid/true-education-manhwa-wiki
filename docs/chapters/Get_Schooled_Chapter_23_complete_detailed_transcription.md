# Reader page 1 — Cover and credits

The chapter opens with the *True Education* title card and Imperfect Comics scanlation credits. Na Hwa-jin appears against the series’ characteristic black-and-red cover design.

# Reader page 2 — Han Yeri’s background

After a warning about strong language and fictional content, Na and Lim Han-rim stand outside the school reviewing a file.

Lim holds up a formal student record containing a school photograph of **Han Yeri**.

> “THIS IS HAN YERI’S STUDENT RECORD AND TRANSCRIPT YOU ASKED FOR.”

The file says that throughout middle school Yeri earned good grades and behaved well. She remained within the top ten students in the school until **Cheon Sang-yeol** became her homeroom teacher.

Lim asks about Yeri’s family. Na explains that Yeri’s parents divorced when she was in first grade. Her father raised her, but his work as a salesman kept him away for long periods. Yeri spent about a year and a half in the countryside and largely lived as an ordinary, self-reliant child. Her father had little involvement with her school life.

# Reader page 3 — Choi Hyun-woong’s similar situation

Na says that **Choi Hyun-woong’s** situation is similar.

Cheon Sang-yeol had also singled him out despite Hyun-woong consistently ranking around third or fourth in school. Hyun-woong’s parents died when he was young. His grandfather supports them by collecting recyclable trash.

Lim reacts angrily:

> “WHAT’S THAT SUPPOSED TO MEAN?”

> “IS IT BAD FOR A CHILD FROM A BROKEN FAMILY TO HAVE GOOD GRADES?”

The dialogue connects Cheon’s discrimination to class and family status. The students being targeted are precisely those whose success contradicts the teacher’s assumptions about who deserves advancement.

# Reader pages 4–5 — Faculty abuse in plain sight

The inspectors observe ordinary school life.

A male teacher attacks a student for briefly going outside school to buy cup-bokki/tteokbokki. The student apologizes and insists he was not playing hooky.

The teacher refuses the explanation, strikes him, and treats leaving school grounds as proof of delinquency.

When another student raises a phone, the teacher shouts:

> “YOU? YOU’RE HOLDING UP YOUR PHONE?”

> “THIS! RECORD THIS!”

He acknowledges that the agency visited the previous day but claims his punishment is legal discipline.

Na and Lim realize that Cheon Sang-yeol is not the school’s only problem. Several teachers have normalized physical abuse, humiliation, and retaliatory threats. Na says that before concentrating only on Cheon, they must reconsider the wider nonsense operating throughout the school.

# Reader pages 6–7 — Cheon panics

Cheon Sang-yeol walks alone, sweating heavily.

He cannot understand how the agency learned his name because he never introduced himself to Na.

> “DON’T TELL ME… CHOI HYUN-WOONG, THAT SHITHEAD REPORTED ME…?”

Cheon reassures himself that he has never done anything “so horrible” that the agency would intervene. The denial is immediately contradicted by the next classroom sequence.

A teacher keeps students through the break and forces one boy to solve a difficult mathematics problem at the board. Classmates whisper that the teacher has started again and that they may not receive a break until the question is solved.

# Reader page 8 — Academic humiliation

The mathematics teacher screams that difficult problems cannot be solved merely by staring at the board.

When the student fails, she strikes or aggressively menaces him.

> “WHY CAN’T YOU SOLVE IT?! WE’VE ALREADY LEARNED IT ALL SO WHY CAN’T YOU SOLVE IT?!”

She accuses him of not paying attention and tells him to quit school and work at a factory if he does not want to listen.

Another teacher identifies her as **Kang Hyun-sook** and remarks that she is taking her frustration out on the students because her stock prices fell.

# Reader pages 9–11 — Sexual humiliation and emotional blackmail

A male teacher focuses on a female student’s hair and appearance.

> “KYA~ LOOK AT YOUR HAIR.”

He asks where she expects to find work in the future. The girl calmly says she has freedom to choose her hairstyle at school.

He responds:

> “ENOUGH WITH YOUR FREEDOM!”

> “YOU’RE TRYING TO SEDUCE MEN AT CLUBS, AREN’T YOU? RIGHT?”

He touches or mishandles her hair/body. When she reacts, he dismisses it:

> “AIGOO~ I MADE A MISTAKE. YOU’RE OVERREACTING.”

When she threatens to report molestation, he escalates into emotional coercion. He says that if she reports him, he will hang himself at the front door of her house, and she will have to take responsibility for his wife and babies.

When she cries, he accuses her of deliberately making him look like a bad person.

# Reader page 12 — Arbitrary classroom power

Another teacher throws a student out for looking in the wrong direction. Immediately afterward, he becomes cheerful and asks who wants Chinese food.

The abrupt tonal switch shows that the teachers’ cruelty is not caused by uncontrollable anger. They can turn it on and off depending on whom they are addressing.

# Reader page 13 — Agency summons

The public-address system activates.

> “THE EDUCATION PROTECTION AGENCY WILL INFORM YOU.”

All named teachers are ordered to gather in the gym. The faculty stop eating, teaching, and talking. Their expressions shift from casual confidence to alarm.

# Reader page 14 — Na announces the supervision period

The accused teachers stand in a row holding folded school uniforms.

One asks:

> “DIRECTOR… WHAT’S THIS?”

Na says that after observing the school over two days, he concluded that their teaching methods were deeply problematic.

During the supervision period, they must have a meaningful opportunity:

> “TO FEEL THE POSITION OF THE STUDENTS BY THROWING AWAY THE AUTHORITY OF THE TEACHER.”

The teachers explode in protest. One asks whether Na is joking. Another complains that midterms are approaching. Kang Hyun-sook calls the plan ridiculous.

A teacher shouts that the agency is supposed to protect teachers’ rights, not undermine their authority. Na bends close to the protesting woman’s face and asks whether she even understands what “teaching rights” mean.

# Reader page 15 — The right to education

Na defines educational rights as including the teacher’s right to educate, the parent’s right to education, and the **student’s right to education**.

He says:

> “AN IGNORANT TEACHER WHO INFRINGES A STUDENT’S RIGHT TO LEARN IS…”

> “ALSO A CLEAR VIOLATION OF THE EDUCATION PROTECTION AGENCY.”

He tells the faculty they do not have to wear the student uniforms if they object. The alternative is a disciplinary statement signed by the Minister of Education waiting on their desks the next morning.

The threat empties the room of resistance.

# Reader page 16 — Cheon becomes a “transfer student”

Cheon Sang-yeol stands in front of a classroom wearing the student uniform.

He stammers:

> “M… MY NAME’S… CHEON SANG-YEOL, A TRANSFER STUDENT…”

Na tells the class that, although Cheon is arriving late, a student is still a student.

> “TREAT HIM CASUALLY AND COMFORTABLY~ LIKE A FRIEND~”

# Reader page 17 — The class assigns him work

Students laugh that their homeroom teacher does not look like a student. They mock how skinny the uniform makes him appear.

A student says Na assigned Cheon to duty and tells him:

> “CLEAN THE WINDOW FRAMES, SANG-YEOL~”

Cheon’s face tightens. The chapter ends its story material on the first ordinary command he must obey as a member of the class he controlled.

# Chapter-level observations

## 1. The chapter broadens the case beyond Cheon

The original investigation concerns corruption and targeted academic discrimination, but observation reveals a faculty culture of violence, sexual humiliation, emotional blackmail, and arbitrary retaliation.

## 2. “Teaching rights” are defined relationally

Na rejects the idea that teacher authority exists by itself. A teacher who destroys a child’s ability to learn is violating the same educational system the agency is designed to protect.

## 3. The uniforms are an experiential punishment

The faculty are not only verbally criticized. They are placed inside the status category they treated as powerless.

## 4. Cheon still views himself as the victim

His private panic focuses on who reported him and whether the agency can identify him—not on what happened to Han Yeri or Choi Hyun-woong.
